Do the contacts belong to the partner or to the firm?

A large law firm runs on trust: clients who come back, referrals, doors that open. That network is its social capital. The thesis heard the leaders of 18 of Brazil's largest law firms and 359 lawyers to understand how they try to turn a network that belongs to one person into an asset of the firm. 02 The question

When a partner leaves, do the clients go too?

In law firms, clients usually trust a person. If the network belongs to that person alone, a partner's exit can take away clients, team and reputation. The thesis calls this the risk of discontinuity and shows how large firms try to protect themselves.

04 Study 1 · literature review

Almost no one had studied law firms' contacts

The author searched the Web of Science database for everything on social capital. There were thousands of articles, but almost none on law firms. Even after swapping the search terms for synonyms, few remained. In the end, 18 articles formed the basis.

12 In practice, limits and credits

Turning contacts into assets takes rules, culture and time

Governance

Committees and clear rules are not just about compliance: they keep the network from being tied to a few partners. Succession plans and a gradual handover of clients reduce the risk when someone leaves.

Careers

Promotion can measure, transparently, each person's contribution to the firm's network. And the author suggests making room for non-partners to build their own contacts early on.

Culture

Reward those who share clients and information, not only those who bill alone. Several firms said they have let go of technically excellent professionals whose attitudes ran against the firm's culture.

Diversity

For the interviewees, diverse teams open doors to new clients and markets. The thesis treats diversity as a concrete way to widen the network, not just a reputation issue.

What this research does not say

  • The 18 firms are large, national in scope and chosen from rankings: the picture does not apply to small or mid-sized firms. · our note
  • The leaders describe how the firm wants to work; the research did not observe the practices or measure whether the models bring more clients or profit. · our note
  • The survey compares percentages without statistical tests, was answered by those who chose to take part, and does not break answers down by firm or by model. · our note
  • The four models come from the author's reading of recurrence, convergence and relevance in the interviews; other researchers might group them differently. · our note
