The golden cage of ride-hailing apps

Uber and 99 promise drivers freedom and earnings. By reading the companies’ websites, listening to 8 drivers in São Paulo and to the comments on a Brazilian Chamber of Deputies poll, the thesis shows that the platforms promote and violate drivers’ dignity at the same time. Explained in 12 screens.

Author: Jerônimo Henrique Portes Advisor: Prof. Dr. Maria Luisa Mendes Teixeira Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

02 The lens · critical theory of technology

An app is not neutral

The thesis starts from the philosopher Andrew Feenberg. For him, there are four ways of thinking about technology, depending on two questions. Answer them and see where you land:

Does technology carry the values of those who made it?
Who is in charge of it?

Dignity promoted

recognition, independence, courtesy, levelling, empowerment…

Dignity violated

indifference, ignoring, suspicion, exploitation, restriction, deception…

The four views: Table 5 of the thesis (Feenberg, via Dagnino and Neder). Dignity elements: Tables 1 to 3 (Jacobson, 2009), with 24 ways of violating and 23 of promoting dignity (our count). For Jacobson, dignity is gained and lost in encounters between people.

03 The method · critical discourse analysis

Three sources of text read as clues

The method comes from the linguist Norman Fairclough: reading the words each side chooses, what they take for granted and who is shown doing what, in order to find the ideology behind the text. 07 The finding · freedom offline, control online

When the app is switched on, freedom shrinks

Freedom exists, the thesis says, but on the outside: choosing the day and time to switch the app on. Once it is on, the driver enters a system of commissions, ratings and penalties they cannot see into. And the bills to pay force them to stay online. Switch it on and off:

12 So what? · limits · credits

Promoting and violating dignity at the same time

For platforms

  • Show how much each ride deducts and set a known cap on the commission.
  • Explain blocks and ratings, with a way for drivers to contest them.
  • Allow drivers to refuse rides without penalty, and listen to them on day-to-day rules.

For public policy

  • Regulate with an eye on what drivers ask for: commission, rate per km, safety and transparency, not only social security.
  • Bear in mind that those who drive rented cars have less freedom of choice.

For drivers and for research

  • Do the maths with all the costs, not only what shows up in the app.
  • The thesis proposes combining critical theory of technology and critical discourse analysis to study social change; it suggests building a scale and a guide to dignified practices.

What this research does not say

  • How many drivers think this way. These are 8 interviews in São Paulo, at a single point in time; the author considers that the poll comments widen the reach. · according to the author
  • What the companies think. Uber and 99 managers were not interviewed; their voice comes from their websites. · according to the author
  • How much the platforms actually charge. Commissions of 35% to 60% are drivers’ accounts, not company data. · our observation
  • All drivers’ opinion of the bill. The poll is open and is not a sample; the 153 comments analysed were selected by keywords. · our observation
