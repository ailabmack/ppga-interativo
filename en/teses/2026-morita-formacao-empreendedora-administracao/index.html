Who takes entrepreneurship off the page at university?

Almost every Business Administration program promises students an "entrepreneurial profile". The thesis interviewed 16 entrepreneurship teachers in Greater São Paulo and read 11 program plans: the curriculum helps, but it is the teacher who turns paper into experience, with more or less institutional support. 02 The question

What is on paper does not always reach the classroom the same way

Every Brazilian degree program has a Pedagogical Course Project (PPC): the document that says what students should learn and how. The literature had already warned that the written curriculum and the lived one tend to differ. The thesis wanted to understand how one becomes the other. Flip the cards.

09 Results · the curriculum

Early, as a track or later: where entrepreneurship sits in the program changes everything

Teachers described very different designs across the four years of a Business Administration degree. The more the curriculum organizes the sequence and opens up flexibility (tracks, electives, schedules), the more room the teacher has to innovate. Choose a design.

12 In practice, limits and credits

Educating entrepreneurs is teamwork: curriculum, teacher and institution

Teachers

Plan each hands-on activity with a clear goal, reflection and follow-up. Projects and challenges teach more when they are not loose ends, and linking theory, experience and the class profile is part of the job.

Program coordinators

Clearer, more coherent and flexible PPCs that guide without straitjacketing: progression across the program, entrepreneurship in several courses and integration of classes, projects and applied experiences.

Institutions

Truly recognize teaching work, offer training and spaces for teachers to exchange ideas, and keep support structures such as incubators and events, so that nothing depends only on individual initiative.

Students

Make the most of what the university offers beyond exams: junior enterprises, student clubs, hubs, mentoring, real projects. In one teacher's words, "soak up" the university environment. · our reading, based on the interviews

What this research does not say

  • Only Business Administration programs in Greater São Paulo. Other programs and regions may work differently; the thesis suggests testing the model there.
  • No one asked the students. What is said about what they learn comes from teachers and documents; the thesis proposes hearing students and graduates over time.
  • Teachers were chosen through referrals and contacts, among people with a track record in the field, which may tilt the picture toward those who already value the topic.
  • Counting passages does not measure effects. The research shows how the pieces fit together, not how much each one improves learning. · our note
  • Only PPCs and national guidelines: course plans and institutional development plans were left out, and 5 of the 16 institutions had no PPC available.
