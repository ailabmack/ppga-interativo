What does company training need to teach for a team to work better together?

Learning has three sides: knowing, feeling and doing. The thesis measured all three in people who had been through a training course and compared them with collective intelligence, the ability to work well with colleagues, in 82 students and 210 employees of three companies. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The thesis’s idea

Learning has three staircases: knowing, feeling and doing

In the 1950s, the educator Benjamin Bloom and colleagues organised teaching goals into three domains, each a staircase from the simplest to the most complex: the cognitive (remembering, understanding, creating), the affective (paying attention, valuing, changing attitudes) and the psychomotor (imitating a movement until you do it without thinking). The thesis took the three staircases from school into company training. Tap a step:

03 The other end

Collective intelligence is the ability to work well with others

The thesis used a scale by researchers Kaur and Shah (2018), which defines collective intelligence as “the ability to work successfully with other members of the organisation”. It has four parts: coordination, a network of relationships, diversity of ideas and independence to disagree. Each person answers about themselves, from 0 to 100. 04 How the research looked

First a trial run with 82 students, then 210 employees of three companies

The author wrote the statements from the verbs of each of Bloom’s steps. To test them, she gave the questionnaire to 82 postgraduate (specialisation) students, treating a recent course module as “training”. Then she took it to employees of three companies who had attended a training course in the previous six months: 262 questionnaires came back, 52 incomplete, leaving 210. The links between the answers were measured with structural equations (PLS-SEM).

06 What it found

Scores were high, and the lowest came from the body

On the 0 to 100 scale, almost everything landed between 70 and 90. Collective intelligence had the highest scores, especially coordination. The psychomotor domain had the lowest: repeating a dance step just by watching scored 51.8. Change the domain and tap a column:

08 Final sample · 210 employees

In the companies, knowing and feeling moved so closely together that they became a single block

Among the 210 employees, those who rated highly what they learned also rated highly their own involvement: the link between the cognitive and the affective came close to 0.9 on a scale that tops out at 1, and the cognitive domain alone had no effect in the model. The author merged the two into a cognitive-affective domain. Some steps also merged and two dropped out. Switch the view:

Section 4.2.3 and Table 14. “Close to 0.9” is the correlation cited by the author; in Table 14, the steps of the two domains correlate between 0.66 and 0.83. Faded blocks = steps dropped from the final model.

09 The discovery

The practical side weighed about twice as much as knowing and feeling combined

In the final model, both blocks are linked to collective intelligence, and together they explain 53.6% of the differences between people. The psychomotor coefficient is 0.547; the cognitive-affective one, 0.260. For the author, this may be linked to the hands-on practice used in training. Move the controls:

10 Final sample · by company

The picture repeats in all three companies

The author ran the model in each company. In all of them, the psychomotor domain was the strongest, with a clear effect. The cognitive-affective block had a clear effect only in the sanitation company, was a hint in the automotive-systems company and had no effect in the pharmaceutical one. Even so, the test comparing the companies found no significant difference between them. Tap a company:

12 So what? · limits and credits

Training that exercises doing goes hand in hand with people who say they collaborate more

For those who plan training

Plan using Bloom’s three domains and include hands-on activities, simulations and debates, which foster participation and communication. (author) Our reading: the research did not compare types of training in the model; the link with practice is the author’s interpretation.

For leaders and HR

Link education to company strategy, evaluate training better and look after the emotional side: a culture that values learning, feedback and an environment of trust. (author)

For researchers

Repeat with larger samples, compare sectors, look at company culture, interview people in depth and measure whether collective intelligence improves organisational results. (author)

What this research does not say

  • Limited sample. 210 people from three companies, by convenience; the findings do not extend to all companies. · according to the author
  • Outside factors. Market and economic changes were not considered. · according to the author
  • Everyone about themselves. Everything comes from the same questionnaire, with each person rating themselves; collective intelligence was measured as each person’s way of working with others, not by a team’s results. · our observation
  • What goes into “psychomotor”. Besides imitating movements, the final block includes statements about care and precision, such as checking the grammar of a text. · our observation
  • Link, not cause. The arrows show that those who score high on one block also score high on the other; the research did not follow anyone before and after training. · our observation
