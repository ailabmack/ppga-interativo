Does the Internet of Things strengthen the car supply chain?

Sensors, platforms and connectivity promise a faster, cheaper supply chain. The thesis heard 10 car dealers and then 150 people from companies in the automotive sector to see whether using more Internet of Things changes the link between the chain, transaction costs and performance. Explained in 11 screens.

Author: Marília Teixeira dos Reis Advisor: Prof. Dr. Gilberto Perez Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

08 The unexpected

For almost 7 in 10, the “IoT platform” was their internet provider

In textbooks, the Internet of Things means connecting machines, sensors and objects to the network so that they exchange data by themselves. The questionnaire asked which platform the company uses for it. Guess: which answer was ticked most?

Our reading: in the interview part, the thesis itself concludes that for dealers the essential role of the Internet of Things is access to digital platforms and social media. In the questionnaire answers, the same seems to hold for many companies: what was measured as the “Internet of Things” is largely being connected. The thesis does not discuss this point.

09 The finding

More Internet of Things did not strengthen any of the links

The central bet was moderation: in companies that use more Internet of Things, the links between ecosystem, costs and performance would be stronger. Change the use of the Internet of Things and compare the illustration of the bet with what the data showed:

11 So what?

Partnership between companies mattered more than the technology

For the author, the Internet of Things changed how the sector sells and communicates, but in the questionnaire it did not change the link between the chain and performance. She attributes this to the small, varied sample and to the companies that do not yet use the technology.

For supply chain managers

  • Cooperating, sharing information and solving problems with partners was what went with reported performance.
  • Having more technology did not change that link in this sample.

For dealers and sellers

  • Dealers say sales moved online and that some have a staff member just for internet leads.
  • The author also notes the cost side: advertising on platforms became a fixed expense that weighs on the business.

For research

  • The author suggests describing the context and shape of each chain in more detail, since the literature is thin on these points.
  • She also suggests comparing the transaction costs of the Internet of Things across sectors of the economy.

What this study does not say

  • Small, varied sample. Answers collected at events, from very different branches of the sector; the interview part was meant only as a questionnaire test. · according to the author
  • 45 of 150 left out. The model used only those who did not tick “don’t know”; those unsure about their own chain dropped out. · our observation, section 4.4
  • It is all perception. Performance and costs are agreement with statements, at a single moment, not company figures. “Transaction costs” measures what the company takes into account in contracts, not how much it spends. · our observation
  • The blocks move almost together. The statements in each block are hard to tell apart (reliability 0.98) and the blocks correlate at 0.73 to 0.85: some answers may reflect an overall impression of the company. · our observation, Table 12 and Figure 56
  • A broad Internet of Things. For 69% the platform was their internet provider, and the thesis does not report the reliability of the Internet of Things statements. · our observation
