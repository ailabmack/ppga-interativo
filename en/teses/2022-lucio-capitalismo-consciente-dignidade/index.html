Do companies that practise conscious capitalism treat people with more dignity?

Conscious capitalism proposes that profit be a means, not the end. The thesis asked 205 private-sector workers how much they want these practices, how much they see them in their own company, and whether the company treats employees, customers and society with dignity. Explained in 12 screens.

03 The problem · dignity

A company has dignity when it does what it says

In the theory developed by the advisor, Maria Luisa Teixeira, organizational dignity is a way of being that people notice when they deal with a company: is it turned only towards its own interests, or also towards those of employees, customers, suppliers, government and community?

05 How the research looked

18,620 LinkedIn invitations became 205 valid questionnaires

Each person answered three blocks: 43 statements about what the company does (1 to 6), 47 about how favourable they are to each practice (1 to 7) and 24 about dignity (1 to 5). The author created and pre-tested the first two scales; scales of different lengths help avoid answering on autopilot.

205 kgof food donated to a care home for older people in São Paulo: 1 kg per valid answer, the incentive the author offered because of the survey’s length (120 questions).

Who answered

Sections 4.3 to 4.5 and Table 6. Data were collected from March to June 2022, with university-educated professionals from companies across Brazil. 08 What it found · dignity

Companies meet the basics and do little beyond them

The dignity scale (created by Teixeira and colleagues in 2014) groups 24 practices into five families. The 205 workers said their company pays on time and does not deceive, but few see career plans, promotion on merit or action for society. Switch the group or tap a family:

Tables 23 and 24 (scale from 1 = do not agree at all to 5 = fully agree). Height = (average − 1) ÷ 4, our calculation. In the “not deceiving” family the statements are negative (“tries to deceive the customer”) and the score was reversed: high = does not deceive. Statements translated by us.

09 The discovery

Attitude reaches dignity by way of practices

People who are more favourable to conscious capitalism see somewhat more of these practices at their company, and those who see more practices see much more dignity. The direct link between attitude and dignity, however, does not stand out from chance. Switch the path:

Tables 34 and 35 (205 answers, model with controls). The numbers are standardised coefficients: how much, on average, one measure moves with the other (0 = not at all; 1 = completely together). p below 0.05 = an effect that stands out from chance. As everything was answered by the same person at one time, “by way of” means moving together, not a proven cause.

12 So what?

For the author, practices need the support of the people who work there

Conscious capitalism helps dignity, but it is not enough for a company to be dignified, and a company can be dignified without calling itself conscious. The two scales created in the thesis remain as a tool to measure where a company stands.

For company leaders

  • The least seen practices were those with communities, with employees and those that take profit out of the centre: that is where the author suggests starting.
  • In dignity, most is missing in employee advancement and social responsibility.

For human resources

  • The author suggests using the attitude scale for training and engagement actions, and even when selecting people.
  • As attitude averages came out very high, he himself notes there may be “nice” answers that sound good.

For research

  • Repeat the scales inside companies that call themselves conscious, and in small and medium-sized firms.
  • Measure whether companies can actually solve community problems, which often call for government action.

What this research does not say

  • One person per company. The companies assessed are many and scattered; one worker’s view may not reflect what really happens inside. · according to the author
  • Everything is perception, at one moment. Attitude, practices and dignity come from the same questionnaire; “influence” here means moving together, not a cause measured over time. · our observation
  • Sample by invitation. University-educated professionals from LinkedIn, many with postgraduate degrees and leadership roles; 152 of the 363 complete answers were removed by the repeated-score rule. · our observation, section 4.4
  • New scales. One area of the attitude scale (unions, media, government and critics) sat at the threshold of the quality tests, and conscious capitalism and dignity practices did not fully separate. · according to the author
  • No comparison. The author found no other studies linking conscious capitalism to other topics to compare the figures with. · according to the author
