What helps a service company innovate?

In the service companies surveyed, those that are more digitally mature and those that learn more as an organization also say they are more able to innovate, with similar weights. The thesis heard 7 managers and 202 professionals in Brazil. Explained in 12 screens.

Author: Claudio Jardim Vargas Advisor: Prof. Dr. Gilberto Perez Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

03 The hypotheses

The thesis started from two bets

The first: the more digitally mature a company is, the more able it is to innovate. The second: learning works as an amplifier, so digital would make more difference where people learn more. Bet before you see the data:

08 What it found · the first bet

Those more digitally mature also say they are more able to innovate

The first bet was clearly confirmed. The coefficient (β) goes from −1 to 1 and says how closely the two ideas move together: here it was 0.808, high. On its own, digital maturity accounts for about 65% of the differences in innovation capability. Tap a dimension:

R² = 0.652: digital maturity alone explains about 65% of the differences in innovation capability across the responses.

Figures 15 and 16 and section 6.4.1 (p < 0.001; test with 5,000 resamples). Numbers in the boxes = how closely each dimension follows its idea. Data from a single point in time: they show a link, not proof of cause.

09 The unexpected · the second bet

Learning did not increase the effect of digital

If learning were an amplifier, the digital line would be steeper in companies that learn more. In the data, the three lines came out practically parallel: the term that measures this boost was 0.007, close to zero and not significant. Choose the level of learning:

10 The discovery

Learning weighs as much as being digitally mature

Without the amplifier effect, the author tested another idea: learning linked directly to innovation capability. The two arrows came out with almost the same weight, 0.472 and 0.490, and together they explain 78% of the differences. Compare the two models:

Figures 15, 21 and 22, Table 27 and section 6.4.4 (p < 0.001 for both arrows). The direct link from learning (H3) was proposed after the second bet was not confirmed. Arrow thickness = β.

12 So what? · limits and credits

“Innovation without learning is a short-lived flight”, in the words of one of the interviewees

For those who run service companies

Look after both fronts at once: mature digitally (strategy, partners, data, market) and create conditions for the organization to learn (clear mission, leaders who delegate, rewarded ideas, knowledge that flows). (author)

To measure your own company

The service innovation capability scale, with 16 statements in four dimensions (organization, market information, culture and knowledge), can be used by companies to assess themselves and see where to invest. (author)

For researchers

Repeat the research by type of service (finance, communications), by company size and stage, and in other countries; test learning as a path between digital and innovation (mediation). (author)

What this research does not say

  • Each person described their own company. The same person answered all three scales, at a single point in time: the links do not prove that digital or learning cause innovation. · our note
  • Convenience sample. Contacts, groups and referrals, several people from the same company and many types of service mixed together, from technology to tourism. · according to the author
  • Hypothesis made afterwards. The direct link from learning was proposed after the moderation did not appear, supported by the literature; it is worth testing again in another sample. · our note
  • Borrowed and new scales. The digital maturity one came from airlines, translated and expanded by the author; the innovation one is new, and two of its dimensions (culture and organization) came out very similar (correlation 0.829). · according to the thesis
