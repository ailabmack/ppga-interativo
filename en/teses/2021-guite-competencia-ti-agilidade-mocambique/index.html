In Mozambique, IT reaches innovation through agility as well

In 136 answers from people working in public and private organizations in Mozambique, those who see more IT competence in their own organization also see it as more agile and more able to innovate. About a third of the link between IT and innovation runs through agility. Explained in 11 screens.

Author: Destinado Artur Guite Advisor: Prof. Dr. Gilberto Perez Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

The research measures three ideas with 52 agree-or-disagree statements, from 1 to 10. Each block is an idea; each layer, one of its dimensions, with height equal to the number of statements. Tap a block or a layer:

03 The idea in the middle

Being agile means noticing the market change and responding in time

In the thesis, organizational agility has two parts, like a radar and a reflex. First the organization notices the change (a competitor, a new customer taste, a fluctuating price); then it reacts to it. Switch each part on:

Section 2.3 and Appendix A. The two parts move almost together: each follows agility with a loading of 0.96 (from 0 to 1; Figure 17). That is why the thesis treats them as a single idea.

05 How the research looked

A questionnaire over WhatsApp in the middle of the pandemic

According to the author, it is the first study of its kind carried out in Mozambique. The questionnaire circulated on WhatsApp, Facebook and e-mail between May and October 2020, while the country was in lockdown because of COVID-19. Tap a step or a slab:

Sections 3.3, 3.5 and 4.1. Side of each slab proportional to the square root of the number (area ∝ number). The minimum of 68 answers was calculated in G*Power; the author doubled the target to 136. Of the 141 answers, 5 were removed for being duplicates or giving the same score to everything.

08 What it found · the first bet

Where IT is more competent, the organization says it is more agile

The coefficient (β) runs from −1 to 1 and says how much two ideas move together, holding the others constant. From IT to agility it came out at 0.809, the strongest link in the model. IT accounts for about 65% of the differences in agility across the answers. Tap a part of agility:

R² = 0.655: IT competence explains about 65% of the differences in agility.

Table 9 and Figure 17 (t 21.656; p < 0.001; 5,000 resamples; f² 1.901, a very large effect). Data from a single point in time: they show a link, not proof of cause.

09 The finding · the second and third bets

10 The unexpected

The size of the organization weighed on agility, and its age did not

The thesis re-ran the model taking into account the organization’s age, its size (number of workers) and its sector. None of the three changed innovation capability. For agility, size and sector mattered, and age did not. Choose what to look at:

Table 12, model 1 (all controls together). Light grey bar = not significant (p ≥ 0.05). With age, size and sector in the model, it explains 70.7% of agility instead of 65.5%. The age, size and sector questions are not in the appendix, so it is not possible to check how the answers were turned into numbers.

11 So what? · limits and credits

Investing in IT pays off in innovation when IT is used at every level

For those who run organizations in Mozambique

Invest in IT across all five dimensions, not just equipment: planning tied to strategy, trained people and information that flows. Executives who understand the value of IT can train their peers. (author)

To measure your own organization

The thesis’s 52 statements (22 on IT, 10 on agility and 20 on innovation) can serve as a checklist to see where the organization is weakest. (our reading)

For researchers

Repeat the study after the pandemic and in other African countries, compare public and private organizations, and test age, size and sector as moderators. (author)

What this research does not say

  • Each person rated their own organization. The same person answered all three scales, at a single point in time: the links do not prove that IT causes agility or innovation. · our observation
  • A convenience sample, during the pandemic. The answers came from people who received the questionnaire on social media, not from a random draw, and the lockdown may have changed how people saw IT. · according to the author
  • Ideas very close together. IT and innovation move so closely together (0.887) that they go past the point where they can be told apart well; the author considers the difference small (about 3%). · Table 18
  • Public and private together. The thesis did not separate the two kinds of organization, which may see IT differently. · according to the author
