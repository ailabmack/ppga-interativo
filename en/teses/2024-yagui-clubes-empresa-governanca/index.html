Becoming a company is not the only way for a football club to be well run

Since 2021, Brazil’s SAF law (Sociedade Anônima do Futebol, a football corporation) has let clubs become companies. The thesis listened to executives and council members from 20 clubs, almost the whole top division, and compared how club-companies and clubs that remain associations are governed. Explained in 12 screens.

12 Takeaways

The legal format matters less than the governance a club practises

For the author, some associations are as well run as club-companies, but the associative model carries its own risks: political interference, a new project with every election and poorly prepared council members.

For associations

  • Professionalize management, with paid executives held to targets.
  • Review the bylaws: the deliberative council’s role, reporting deadlines and fast removal in case of wrongdoing.
  • Those who stay associations need to restructure now, the author says, to remain competitive.

For SAFs and investors

  • Build controls, management systems and compliance rules from the start.
  • Look after the relationship with the original club, which keeps rights over name, colours and symbols.
  • Becoming a company does not guarantee good management: the change may only start professionalization.

For members, federations and fans

  • Demand transparency: financial statements, reports and council members’ access to contracts.
  • One interviewee notes that change goes beyond clubs: refereeing, sports courts and federations too.
  • Well-prepared council members make a difference in any format. (our reading)

What this research does not say

  • Rules for all of football. Almost all clubs were in Série A; the findings do not apply to Séries B, C and D. · according to the author
  • A neutral reading. In qualitative research, the interpretation is the researcher’s and may carry preconceptions. · according to the author
  • The whole club’s view. Each club was heard through one person only, in different roles (directors, audit-board members, deliberative-council members). · our note
  • Measured finances or performance. No financial statements or results on the pitch are compared; interviews date from 2022–23, just after the law. · our note
  • A score per club. The counts say who mentioned a topic, not how well each club is governed. · our note
