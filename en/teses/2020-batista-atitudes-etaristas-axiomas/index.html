Doctoral thesis · PPGA Mackenzie · 2020

How you see life weighs on how you see a colleague aged 50 or over

With 424 workers, the thesis measured ageism in three layers (thinking, feeling and acting) and showed that people who distrust others tend to hold more ageist attitudes, while those who believe in effort, in life’s many ways out and in faith hold fewer. Being individualist or collectivist made no difference. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The idea · what ageism is

Ageism has three layers: what people think, what they feel and what they tend to do

Ageism is judging someone unfavourably because of their age. The thesis follows a classic idea from psychology: every attitude has a thinking part, a feeling part and an action part. Studies used to measure one part at a time; the author combined three scales to measure all three. Tap a layer:

04 How the research looked

424 workers who work or have worked alongside colleagues over 50

In 2019, the author ran a questionnaire of about 15 minutes, on paper (in graduate classes and some companies) and online, with invitations by email and WhatsApp that participants passed on to acquaintances. It was open to people over 18, in employment, who had worked alongside workers over 50. Responses came mostly from the states of Pará and São Paulo.

06 What it found · the measure

The compliment “they are warm” did not fit into the ageism measure

The stereotype scale has one positive idea (warmth) and two negative ones (lack of competence and of adaptability). Those who agreed most with the compliment tended to agree less with the negative ones, and the three did not form a single measure. With the two negative ones, stereotype joined prejudice and the intention to discriminate in a single ageist attitude: the first hypothesis was confirmed. Switch:

08 The unexpected

Being individualist or collectivist did not change ageism

Earlier studies linked more collectivist cultures, such as Asian ones, to more respect for older people. The thesis measured this value with 16 everyday situations, like this one. Choose what you would do:

You and your friends have decided to go out for dinner. How would you handle the bill?

Triandis, Chen and Chan’s (1998) scenario scale, adapted by Watanabe (2014); Annex D. Each answer scores a point for one side, and the person is placed on the side with more points (Table 8: 318 and 106). Result in Table 26 (β −0.032, p 0.493). The ageism heights are an illustration: in the “what the thesis expected” view, they show only the direction of the hypothesis.

09 The discovery

People who distrust others tend to be more ageist; those who believe in effort, less so

Four of the five beliefs were linked to the ageist attitude in the direction the thesis predicted. The two largest weights, almost tied, are social cynicism (more ageism) and reward for effort (less). Compare what the thesis expected with what the data showed and tap a bar:

Together, the beliefs and individualism-collectivism explain about 18% of the differences in ageist attitude between people (17% adjusted R²), a medium effect by the thesis’s criteria. The rest depends on other things.

Table 26 (PLS-SEM, 5,000 resamples). n.s. = not significant at 5%. Bar length = standardised coefficient (β): the further from zero, the stronger the link. The data come from a single point in time: they show links, not proof of cause. Age, sex, race and education had no significant effect (Table 25).

12 So what? · limits and credits

Measuring the three layers together helps reveal the ageism nobody admits to

For managers and HR

Use the three-layer scale as a diagnostic tool, especially in teams with several generations, to find where ageist attitudes are stronger and plan actions to reduce them. (author)

For the organisation’s climate

Reduce distrust between people and encourage tolerance of different ways of solving things; value each person’s effort, regardless of age. Faith is linked to less ageism, but the author notes that Brazil is a secular state and companies should not promote religions. (author)

For public policy and research

With an ageing population, policies to keep people aged 50 or over in work also depend on companies reducing ageist attitudes. Next steps: the long version of the beliefs scale, other cultural values and another measure of individualism-collectivism. (author)

What this research does not say

  • It does not hold for all of Brazil. The sample was a convenience sample, mostly from Pará and São Paulo. · according to the author
  • A link is not a cause. The answers come from a single point in time: beliefs and ageism go together, but the data do not show which comes first. · our note
  • “Acceptable” answers. Prejudice and the intention to discriminate had very low means; the thesis notes that discrimination is less and less accepted and that people tend to hide it. · according to the author
  • Individualism measured simply. The scenario scale could not be validated and became a two-sided label; another measure might give a different result. · according to the author
  • Fate control was left half-measured. The statements about changing one’s fate, those closest to the hypothesis, were dropped for low weight. · according to the author
