Doctoral thesis · PPGA Mackenzie · 2025

Software teams innovate when they learn together with the client

Apps, platforms and subscription systems are digital services, and the people who invent and improve them are development teams. The thesis listened to 12 people from 9 software companies and built, from those conversations, a theory of how these teams learn, earn the client’s trust and create value with them. Explained in 12 screens.

Author: José Antônio Afonso Nonato Advisor: Prof. Dr. Gilberto Perez Co-advisor: Prof. Dr. Diógenes de Souza Bido Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

08 The unexpected · theoretical code 2

Showing the client what is still imperfect can build trust

Before you look: the team takes an unfinished, "ugly" prototype to the client and admits what it does not know yet. What do you think happens to the relationship?

Abstract, sections 4.4.2, 5.1 and 5.2.1 (propositions 3 and 4), Appendix E. 12 Takeaways

Innovating in digital services takes learning, trust and protected time

For the author, innovation does not come from technical skill alone: it is a human, repeated process in which the team learns, experiments and creates with the client.

For technology and product leaders

  • Adopt design thinking: empathy, prototype, test and client feedback, in cycles.
  • Set aside moments and rituals for new work; without them, "the routine consumes us".
  • Tolerate honest mistakes and explain to the team why each delivery matters.

For development teams

  • Show prototypes early, still imperfect, to speed up feedback.
  • Validate hypotheses before building, and start from the problem, not the tool.
  • Create channels so client feedback reaches the people who did the work quickly.

For those who buy software

  • Taking part in defining what has value helps the solution fit daily work. (our reading)
  • Sharing outcome figures with the supplier, which interviewees say is rare, gives the team the feedback it lacks.

What this research does not say

  • A closed theory. Data collection stopped at 12 interviews for lack of time, before theoretical saturation: each conversation still brought new ideas. · according to the author
  • Rules for any company. Startups, software houses and traditional companies innovate differently; each type is worth studying separately. · according to the author
  • A bias-free reading. What people said reflects their own experience, and the interpretation is one researcher’s; more coders and other sources would help. · according to the author
  • The client’s side. Only team members were interviewed, all men: trust and co-creation appear from the developers’ point of view. · our observation
  • Measured effects. The 10 propositions were not tested; they say what may happen, not how much. · our observation
