Does it pay more to do ESG or to tell what you do?

Companies everywhere invest in environmental, social and governance (ESG) practices. Does it pay off? The thesis reviewed 108 studies and analyzed 2,987 yearly records of companies from Brazil, China, India and South Africa. In these markets, investors rewarded firms that disclose well, not those with the best scores. Explained in 10 screens.

02 Article 1 · 108 studies

Does ESG pay? The studies don't agree

Some studies find gains, others no effect, others losses. To understand why, the author searched Web of Science and Scopus for articles that tested what changes this relationship. Of 610 articles found, 108, published from 2019 to 2023, made it into the analysis.

05 Article 2 · 2016 to 2023

2,987 yearly company records in four countries

Listed BRICS companies with ESG scores and financial data on Bloomberg, from 2016 to 2023. Each company in each year is one record. Russia was left out: after the sanctions that followed the invasion of Ukraine, its data were no longer available.

Records by sector. China accounts for two thirds of the sample (1,967 of 2,987). Source: Tables 9 and 10 and section 3.4.

Telling cause from coincidence

More profitable companies may invest more in ESG, not the other way round. The two-stage method (2SLS) isolates the part of the score that does not reflect the result itself and compares companies in the same sector and year.

Standardized variables: the next screens show how much the result changes, in standard deviations, when the score rises by one standard deviation.

06 The finding

Doing, on its own, doesn't move the needle. Telling does.

With the overall performance score, no measure of return or profit improved. With the overall disclosure score, shareholder return, ROA and ROE went up. For the author, in markets with weak rules and little information, disclosure works as a signal to investors. Compare:

07 What it found · by pillar

Governance is the pillar that pays most consistently

Splitting the scores into environmental, social and governance, governance disclosure is the only one that improves all four measures, and governance never pulls results down. Social makes no difference in performance; in disclosure, it helps shareholder return but lowers market value. Explore:

08 What it found · the curve

Disclosure helps up to a point

The relationship is not a straight line. For shareholder return, ROA and ROE, the disclosure curve rises, peaks and falls: disclosing too much stops paying. Market value does the opposite: it penalizes firms stuck in the middle. The author calls this the "too much of a good thing" effect. Move the score:

09 What it found · both scores together

The gray zone: stopping halfway is the worst place to be

When the author put both scores in the same model, a gray zone appeared: with low-to-middle disclosure, predicted shareholder return, ROA and ROE fall below the average company. At high disclosure, the best shareholder return and ROE come with average performance, not maximum. Place a company:

10 So what?

Disclose coherently, and don't stop halfway

For managers

Disclose ESG in an integrated way, covering all three pillars, and in line with what the company actually does. Disclosure without substance invites accusations of greenwashing. Avoid the lukewarm engagement of the gray zone.

Section 3.6.3

For investors

Look at shareholder return together with ROA and ROE: the market may reward the narrative before profit shows up. Governance is the pillar that creates value most consistently.

Section 3.6.3 and chapter 4

For regulators

Standardize ESG reports, require independent verification and curb purely symbolic disclosure, so that what is told reflects what is done.

Section 3.6.3

What this research does not say

  • Only listed companies, and no Russia. Private and mid-sized firms were left out. · according to the author
  • A single source of scores. Bloomberg scores may miss qualitative aspects; comparing with MSCI or Refinitiv would help. · according to the author
  • The pandemic was not isolated, and the review covered 5 years and two databases. · according to the author
  • Two thirds of the records are from China, and the effects are small (tenths of a standard deviation). 2SLS reduces, but does not remove, doubts about cause. · our note
