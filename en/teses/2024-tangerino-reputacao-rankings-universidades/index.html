Does a ranking show a university’s reputation?

THE, QS, RUF: rankings have become the yardstick of quality in higher education. Listening to 8 managers from 7 community universities in São Paulo, the thesis shows that they use rankings more as a shop window than to make decisions, and that these institutions’ reputation rests on memory, community and enrolments. Explained in 12 screens.

07 Finding · the yardsticks mentioned

Only ENADE, which is mandatory, came up in every conversation

ENADE is the federal government’s exam taken by graduating students. The Brazilian media rankings (Folha’s RUF and Estadão’s) and the international ones come up less. 08 The finding · academic and market reputation

The same ranking feeds two reputations

12 Limits · credits

A ranking shows only part of a university’s reputation

What this research does not say

  • What students, teachers and families think. Only managers were interviewed; the author notes that their opinion is not that of all publics. · according to the author
  • Whether climbing a ranking improves reputation. The thesis does not measure reputation or ranking positions; it analyses how managers talk about them. · our note
  • How many managers think this way. There are 8 interviews at 7 community universities in São Paulo, between February and April 2024. · our note
  • Who said each sentence, in every case. In some passages the thesis swaps the interviewees’ codes (for example, between chapter 3 and the appendix); here we follow chapter 3 and the table of interviewees. · our note
