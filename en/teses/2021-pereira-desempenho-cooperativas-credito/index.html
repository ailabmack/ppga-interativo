Doctoral thesis · PPGA Mackenzie · 2021

A credit union’s performance has a memory

With 20 years of balance sheets from 660 credit unions, the thesis built a model that looks at several sides at once. One year’s return carries a good part of the previous year’s, and capital, cash, lending and expenses also matter. Explained in 12 screens.

Author: Rina Xavier Pereira Advisor: Prof. Dr. Leonardo Fernando Cruz Basso Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

03 The problem · Carton and Hofer (2006)

Looking at one side only can flip who is doing better

Performance has several dimensions, and doing well in one can be costly in another. A new credit union can grow and build resources at the expense of returns; a mature one can earn more from what it already has. Choose how to look at the two made-up credit unions below.

06 Central Bank · 2000 to 2019 · Tables 7 and 7.11

Twenty years of balance sheets, with very uneven numbers

The author used only credit unions with data in every year: 660 local ones, which serve members directly, and 26 central ones, which group local ones. Each square is a credit union. Some have numbers so far off the curve that the average misleads; so returns entered the model as logarithms, which shrink the extremes.

Local unions: 13,200 observations (660 × 20 years); central ones: 520 (26 × 20). Mean and median of local unions in Table 7.11; each ruler goes from zero to a little past the 75th percentile and the arrow shows when the mean goes past the end. Thesis measures: ROA = surplus ÷ total assets; ROE = surplus ÷ equity; ROS = surplus ÷ sales.

07 The finding · Tables 7.8 to 7.10 and 7.19 to 7.21

Return on assets carries 70% of the previous year’s momentum

The author used a model that lets the previous year into the calculation. In local credit unions, when one year’s ROA is 10% higher, the next year’s tends to be 7% higher, all else equal. The author calls this memory. Imagine a good year and see how much of it is left afterwards.

our calculation Each block is the effect left from a year 10% above, multiplied by the previous-year coefficient each year (Tables 7.8 to 7.10 for central unions; 7.19 to 7.21 for local ones), all else equal. Dynamic model in differences (Arellano and Bond GMM), with measures in logarithms.

When memory enters the calculation, more lending goes with lower returns

Simple model: fixed effects with robust errors (Tables 7.16 and 7.17). With memory: GMM in differences (Tables 7.19 and 7.20). Bars in % change in return when the measure rises 1%. The thesis does not comment on the sign change; the reading is ours.

12 In practice · limits · credits

Track returns together with their own history

Credit union managers

The author recommends explaining ROA and ROE by considering their own past, as well as the other measures: a bad or good year weighs on the following ones.

Members

For the author, understanding performance helps to see why it is hard to distribute surpluses better and be fairer to smaller members.

Regulators and systems

Many credit unions do not report all their data, and the Central Bank does not check the statements they publish, the thesis points out. Complete data would allow measuring the two missing dimensions.

Researchers

The author suggests including cash flow and intangible assets and creating an index with more local unions. Measuring market and value creation would complete the model.

What this research does not say

  • Three of five dimensions. Market and economic value creation were left out for lack of data (screen 05).
  • Relationship is not cause. The models show what goes together with returns; the sign of lending changed from one model to the other (screen 10). · our observation
  • Model tests with warnings. For ROA and ROE, the second-order autocorrelation test flagged a problem (p 0.000 and 0.001), and the Hansen test rejected the instruments in all three models. Read the numbers as strong clues, not exact measures. · our observation
  • Little explained in the simple model. In the model without memory, the four measures explain 1% to 7% of the variation in each credit union’s return over the years (within R²). · our observation
  • Only those that lasted 20 years. Credit unions that closed, merged or stopped reporting in some year were left out. · our observation
