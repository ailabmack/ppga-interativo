The value of the car is changing hands

Cars are becoming electric, connected, autonomous and shared. The thesis interviewed seven executives at carmakers and at a supplier in Brazil to understand where this shift comes from, who gains from it and how companies are changing the way they make money. Explained in 12 screens.

Author: Elcio Alves Ferreira Advisor: Prof. Dr. Dimária Silva e Meirelles Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

02 The topic

Four shifts at once, and some sit far from what carmakers know how to do

The thesis starts from four "new paradigms" of the car proposed by the economist David Teece. For each one, Teece measured the distance between what carmakers already know and what they will need to know: in technology, in business model (the way of making money) and in market. 07 The finding

The car's value is draining into electronics, which carmakers do not master

The thesis's central conclusion: value is migrating into the electric car, and it is not the traditional carmakers who lead that move, but technology companies, with plenty of capital and faster decisions. One of the interviewees (E1) gave figures for electronics' share of a car's cost. Pick a moment:

30% to 40%of an electric car's cost is the battery, which carmakers do not know how to make
2035from then on, the European Union wants only electric cars on sale
10 yearsfor E3, after that there will only be electric cars

11 What it found · decisions

The decisions that matter are made at headquarters, and only three show signs of going beyond watching

At the carmakers interviewed, strategy comes from headquarters; Brazil adapts products and handles tactics and operations. 12 So what?

Carmakers need partners, and Brazil needs to find its place in the chain

For carmakers

  • Build partnerships and alliances with technology companies to gain access to electronics, software and batteries. (author)
  • Adjust the main business model instead of waiting: direct sales and services in the connected car. (author and interviews)
  • Look for open-minded leaders who let teams think and understand the customer. (interviewees)

For government

  • Have an innovation and basic-science policy for the industry, not just subsidies. (author)
  • Choose where Brazil fits in the global chain, as Argentina is doing with lithium batteries. (author)
  • Discuss rules for autonomous cars, which do not exist today. (interviews)

For researchers

  • Interview technology companies and the new Chinese carmakers. (author)
  • Reach decision-makers at headquarters and policymakers in Brazil. (author)

What this research does not say

  • Those who spoke do not set strategy. The interviewees lead in Brazil, but strategic decisions belong to headquarters; so the thesis could not get at how they are made. · according to the author
  • Seven views, not the whole industry. Technology companies, new Chinese entrants and government are missing. · according to the author
  • Opinions, not measurements. Cost and timing figures are the interviewees' statements; the thesis tables summarise what they said. · our note
  • A snapshot from the pandemic. The interviews date from the covid-19 period; forecasts such as timelines and a US$25,000 Tesla by 2023 belong to that moment. · our note
