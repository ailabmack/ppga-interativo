Boal's theatre inside organizations

Can competencies be developed without handing out a list of skills? The thesis takes the Theatre of the Oppressed, created by Augusto Boal, to 37 women managers at a public financial institution and shows that the technique only transforms when those who use it side with the people who suffer oppression. 02 First act · theoretical essay

Feeling is also a way of knowing

The thesis has four "acts", one per article. The first asks how science is done in organizations. The most common ways of doing research favour thinking and measuring. The author proposes another starting point, the philosopher Félix Guattari's Aesthetic Paradigm: the body comes first, and feeling weighs as much as thinking, knowing and acting. 12 So what?

Competencies also develop as a group

The thesis swaps the question "which competencies should be developed?" for another: what experiences make a group perceive, feel and act on its work? The workshops answered with proposals: a support network, a reporting channel run by women and an anti-racism policy (the last two went into the report to the institution).

For those who hire or facilitate

  • Before starting, ask who is paying, whom the work serves, who will be in the audience and how much autonomy the facilitator will have.
  • With the boss in the room, the Jokers say, dialogue is rarely horizontal.

For HR and corporate education

  • Training each person to meet targets better is only one path. Another is making room for the group to name what makes people ill and rehearse ways out.
  • The agreed theme (sales) may lead to problems the company did not foresee, such as harassment and racism.

For managers and teams

  • What looks like an "isolated case" may be a pattern. Bringing colleagues together breaks "the loneliness of the position".
  • Small daily changes, like having lunch together every week, count as micro-revolutions.

What this research does not say

  • Whether the commitments lasted. 34 of the 37 participants were interviewed afterwards, but that was left for another study; the thesis does not follow what came after the workshops. · according to the author
  • Privileged access. The workshops happened because the author, a former superintendent, knew who could authorize them. · according to the author
  • One place, one group. A highly hierarchical public institution and only women managers; mixed groups or groups with bosses may react differently. · according to the author
  • An insider's voice. The interviews heard only CTO's board, who share the same commitments; managers and outside facilitators are missing. · according to the author
  • A map, not a measurement. The cartography follows movements; it does not measure gains in competency or compare with other training. · according to the author
