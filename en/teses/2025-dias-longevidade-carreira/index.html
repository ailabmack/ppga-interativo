Retired… and kept on working

More and more people stay active after becoming entitled to retire. Why? With 422 workers aged 40 and over, the research shows what sustains this career longevity. Explained in 10 screens.

03 How the research looked

422 people aged 40 and over, all of them working

The online questionnaire reached formal employees, self-employed people, entrepreneurs and business owners from 16 Brazilian states and abroad, aged 40 to 83. The author created three new scales, including the career longevity scale, and adapted two others into Portuguese.

Each dot is one participant. Dots light up in the number of the chosen group; they do not mark the same people from one group to the next. Source: Tables 39 to 46.

05 What it found · the model

The place of work, choice and adaptation

Three factors are directly linked to career longevity: work centrality (the largest), volition and adaptability. With health and sex, the model explains about half (51%) of the differences between people.

06 Two surprises

Health matters most, and it changes the game

Perceived health entered the model as a control and turned out to carry the largest weight. It also changes the rules: the author split people who rate their own health above and below the median and compared the two groups.

Height proportional to the β coefficient. "Everyone": final model (Table 95). Groups: multigroup analysis with 181 people in better health and 241 in worse health (Table 100). The difference between the groups is significant for centrality and adaptability; for volition, it is not (p = 0.063).

Feeling employable is not enough

Believing one could keep or find work had no direct effect on longevity (β = 0.052, not significant). Employability depends heavily on volition (0.490), and people who already receive a pension feel less employable (−0.221). For the author, motivation, purpose and the wish to stay active matter more.

Source: Tables 94, 95 and 101 and section 4.9.5.

07 What it found · sex

Women want to carry on; men have carried on for longer

Men and women look different depending on the yardstick. On the motives scale, women scored higher than men, even taking health and the other factors into account (β = −0.185 for men). In time already worked after retiring, men are ahead.

08 What it found · the retirees

Four in five carry on by choice

Of the 422, 113 already receive a pension and keep working, on average for 5.6 years so far. Most do not intend to stop. Pick a question:

10 Why it matters

Working longer, by choice and in good health

For the author, working later in life is not just an economic need: it is also about meaning, fulfilment and desire. Anyone who wants to retain and include older workers needs to look at these motives.

For companies

  • Career programs designed for people in their 50s, 60s and beyond.
  • Part-time work, phased retirement, project contracts and bridge employment.
  • Customized careers that adjust to each stage of life.
Section 5.2 of the thesis

Health at the center

  • Perceived health had the largest weight; caring for health means caring for staying on.
  • When health is worse, the meaning work has in one's life holds people more.
Our reading of Tables 95 and 100

For public policy

  • Active ageing, with autonomy and decent work at every age.
  • Attention to those forced to stay in or leave the labor market: not everyone gets to choose.
Section 5.3 of the thesis

For career guidance

  • The longevity scale and the six profiles can serve as diagnostic tools in HR and career counseling.
  • Develop adaptability and freedom of choice, which reinforce each other.
Sections 5.1 and 5.2 of the thesis

What this research does not say

  • A wealthier, more educated sample. 72% in income classes A and B, 96% with a university degree and 71% from São Paulo. Results may differ in classes C, D and E. · according to the author
  • People from different classes understand "changing careers" in different ways. So readiness for career transitions should be read with caution. · according to the author
  • A snapshot, with self-assessment. Each person answered once about themselves; the results show associations, not cause and effect. · our note
  • Time and motives measured separately. Years after retiring apply to 113 people; the model uses the motives scale, answered by everyone. · our note
