What makes working with waste dignified?

Social impact businesses promise to transform the lives of people on the margins. The researcher spent five months working on the sorting belt of two recycling cooperatives to see whether that happens. She found more of the workers’ own knowledge recognized than erased (20 against 8), but also unhealthy conditions and bribery at the center of the map. In 11 screens.

02 The lens · absences and emergences

Every label that erases someone has a kind of knowledge on its other side

The thesis uses the ideas of sociologist Boaventura de Sousa Santos. For him, the reason of the “North” (Western science and markets) sticks five labels on people living on the other side of an invisible line, the abyssal line, and so produces absences: people and knowledge treated as if they did not exist. The sociology of emergences looks for what these people know and do that has value. “Post-abyssal” dignity means having that knowledge recognized. Flip the cards:

The five monocultures (red) and five ecologies (blue) are the rows and columns of the thesis’s summary matrices (Charts 10, 12 and 14), based on Santos (2002, 2007). In Brazil, “social impact business” covers everything from companies to cooperatives; here, the impact businesses are the two cooperatives, and the impact falls on the members themselves (Figure 9).

03 The method · symbolic cartography

The researcher did not interview from outside: she sorted waste alongside them

Between February and June 2022, she worked on the sorting belt, in electronic waste and at the baling press of the two cooperatives, talking while the work went on, with no fixed script. She then drew the field as a map: with scales (how much detail each relationship allows), projection (what sits at the center and what at the periphery) and symbols. Tap a visit:

04 The field · two cooperatives, 49 people

Three in four are women, and average income is R$ 600 per family member

The two cooperatives are in northern Paraná state and grew out of a city hall initiative to get waste pickers off the city landfill: CP in 2017 and ZS in 2018, founded by people who left CP unhappy with the unfair split of the money. Today they swap week by week between the Plant (open, large belt) and the Shed (closed, small belt). Change the view:

05 Macro scale · the relationship with work

The landfill pays more; the cooperative gives dignity, though not without risks

For the members, dignity is not about the material they handle: it is about working conditions and society’s recognition (proposition 1). The same waste that shames at the landfill brings pride at the cooperative. But the Plant’s belt has no protection from rain and cold, and the researcher recorded five workplace accidents. 06 Meso scale · policies and processes

The body, not the clock, decides when the workday ends

At the center of the meso scale is the body. It decides how many hours people work, who does each task and how much weight goes in each bag: the PET station rotates because it’s very tiring, people with physical limitations get a seated task, and the day ends when the material runs out. 07 Emergences · the knowledge that resists

The strongest knowledge is equality: no one may get a privilege

Emergences show up in all five ecologies, but recognition (the one that suppresses hierarchy) produces the most. Cooperative presidents work the belt like anyone else, rules come from votes in assembly, and people whose past society condemns are welcomed. Tap a column:

08 Meso and micro scales · buyers and government

To keep the cooperative going, they accept what they would not accept among themselves

Inside, equality rules. In relations with outsiders, the cooperatives depend on others: city hall provides the space and pays for water and power, and large buyers set the terms. That is where most of the absences come from. See the three relationships:

10 The symbol · two gods of time

The cooperatives live by Kairos’s time, and the thesis holds

To sum up the field, the author uses two Greek gods. Chronos, who devoured his children, is clock time, which consumes health and leisure for the sake of output. Kairos is the opportune time, which respects the body and makes room for a barbecue on a Tuesday. The two are at war: those who live by Kairos are called unproductive and feel guilty. 11 Takeaways

For the author, transforming lives takes more than income

For the author, the cooperatives break the status quo because members can “be” who they “are” there, enjoy leisure without guilt and access resources the landfill would not give them. But when they depend on outsiders, they give up their own dignity to survive.

For impact businesses

  • Check whether practice matches the narrative: working conditions and dependence weigh as much as income.
  • Value workers’ own knowledge: their own vocabulary, learning on the job, decisions in assembly.

For those who deal with cooperatives

  • Partnership must not turn into trading favors or submission (propositions 23 and 24).
  • Keep the facilities safe: the uncovered, poorly maintained belt is at the center of the absences (our reading).

For research

  • Symbolic cartography can serve other organization studies, such as leadership, people management and occupational health.
  • Repeat the study in other kinds of impact businesses and in cooperatives in other sectors.

The cards summarize the author’s contributions and suggestions, except where marked as our reading.

What this research does not say

  • Whether it holds for every impact business. Recycling cooperatives have very particular features; the author suggests studying other models. · per the author
  • Conversations amid the noise. The unscripted interviews, recorded during work, were hard to transcribe and organize; short, targeted interviews would help. · per the author
  • How often each thing happens. The map counts categories, and center and periphery are the researcher’s reading of what holds the field up; there is no measure of frequency. · our note
  • Two cooperatives, one city, five months. The picture is from 2022 and one place. · our note
