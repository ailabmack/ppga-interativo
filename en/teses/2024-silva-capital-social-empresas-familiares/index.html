In family businesses, what goes hand in hand with results is the ability to learn from outside

In the 108 responses from people at family businesses, good relationships and knowledge sharing were not directly linked to performance. What was linked was absorptive capacity (picking up, understanding and applying outside knowledge), which goes hand in hand with relationships and sharing. Explained in 12 screens.

03 The vocabulary

Four ideas, measured with 52 statements

Social capital is the relationships and trust inside the company. Knowledge sharing is how much people tell what they know and ask what others know. Absorptive capacity is picking up outside knowledge, understanding it, combining it with what is already known and applying it. And performance. Tap a layer (height = number of statements):

04 How the research looked

108 people from family businesses answered, most of them in finance and with many years at the company

The author listed family businesses and e-mailed the questionnaire to 223 people in their management: owners, directors, controllers, managers and analysts. Within 45 days, 108 answered, all responses valid. Beforehand, six people (three owners, two directors and one family-business board member) tested the questions. These are large, long-established companies, mostly in industry.

07 The hypotheses

The thesis tested six links between the four ideas

All predicted “the more, the more”. Three reach performance: relationships, sharing and absorption. The other three link relationships to sharing and absorption, and sharing to absorption. Bet before you see:

08 What it found · the six links

Relationships and sharing did not reach performance directly

Four of the six links were confirmed. In the model with everything together, social capital goes strongly with sharing (0.806) and with absorption (0.591), and sharing also with absorption (0.279). Only the arrow from absorptive capacity reaches performance (0.494). Tap an arrow:

Table 9 and Figures 19 and 20 (final model in SmartPLS 4, 5,000 bootstrap samples). Thickness = standardised coefficient (β); grey dashed = no clear effect (p above 0.05). Turnover entered as a control and was the only one with an effect (0.214, p = 0.018). The data come from a single point in time: they show links, not proof of cause.

09 The discovery

Alone or together, only absorptive capacity is linked to performance

The author also tested each idea alone against performance. Even then, relationships (0.258, p = 0.123) and sharing (0.194, p = 0.390) did not pass the test. Absorption passed both ways, and got stronger with the others in the model (from 0.358 to 0.494). Switch:

Figures 13, 16 and 18 (each idea alone) and Table 9 (all together). Height = β without its sign. The sum of paths is the thesis’s own calculation (product of coefficients): it did not test whether the indirect paths are significant.

12 So what? · limits and credits

Relationships and knowledge sharing are worth more when the company knows how to use what it learns

For those who run the family business

Get to know and look after internal relationships and encourage knowledge sharing as ways to strengthen absorptive capacity, not as ends in themselves. (author)

For those who manage knowledge

Work on absorption not only as seeking knowledge, but above all as applying it: turning what is learned outside into practices, products and processes. (author)

For researchers

Include other variables that better explain how relationships and sharing link to performance, such as governance and the competitive environment, and measure performance more fully. (author)

What this research does not say

  • Small sample. 108 responses may not represent the universe of family businesses. · according to the author
  • Perceived performance. It is what respondents say happened over three years to sales, customers, operating profit and efficiency (the four statements that remained), not accounting data; the author acknowledges the scale may not cover everything. · our observation
  • Indirect paths untested. The thesis says relationships and sharing act through absorption, but calculates those paths only by multiplying coefficients, without testing whether they are significant. · our observation
  • Ideas that move together. Relationships, sharing and absorption correlate from 0.76 to 0.82 and were answered by the same person, in the same questionnaire: it is hard to separate the weight of each. · our observation
  • One moment, one list. The answers come from a single moment and from companies chosen by the author, mostly large and long-established; they show links, not cause. · our observation
