Did ESG help Latin American companies get through the pandemic?

ESG stands for how a company handles the environment, people and its own governance. In three studies of listed companies from 2018 to 2023, the thesis asks whether a higher ESG score changes executive pay, profit, market value and the interest a company pays, and whether the pandemic changed any of that. 02 The idea

Three letters sum up how a company treats the planet, people and its own leadership

Companies disclose what they do on each front, and databases such as Refinitiv turn that into an ESG score. The thesis uses this score, both the total and each letter. Flip the cards.

Definitions from sections 3.3.2 and 4.3.2 of the thesis. The score measures what the company discloses and does on each topic; the thesis speaks of ESG disclosure.

03 The problem

Does being responsible pay off? Studies still disagree

The topic took off: articles mentioning "ESG" went from fewer than a hundred a year up to 2018 to 1,164 in 2023. But a review of more than a thousand studies found a bit of everything. And almost all of them look at developed markets, not at Latin America.

06 Study 1 · Brazil · 441 records

With a higher ESG score, executive pay tracks performance more closely

According to agency theory, executive pay should rise and fall with company performance, so that executives defend shareholders' interests. Among companies listed on B3, Brazil's stock exchange, this link was stronger the higher the ESG score. The pandemic did not strengthen it.

Performance

Table 4 (system GMM, six models, 441 records from 2018 to 2023). Pay = total executive compensation (salary, bonuses and options), in logs. The arrow thickness is a diagram: it shows the direction of the effect, not its size. Association, not causation.

07 Study 2 · 5 countries · 488 records

A higher ESG score came with more market value and more profit

Comparing each company with itself over the years, the ESG score moved together with market value (Q) and return on assets (ROA). For return on equity (ROE), only a weak hint. The pandemic years, in turn, pulled value and profit down.

08 The turn · Study 2 · 1,018 records

In the pandemic, the ESG score mattered even more for value and profit

To see whether ESG worked as protection in the crisis, the author added a term that measures the extra effect of the ESG score in 2020 and 2021. The pandemic alone knocked Q and ROA down; in companies with a higher ESG score, part of the drop was offset.

09 Study 3 · 5 countries · 1,019 records

Higher ESG score, lower interest, but the pandemic did not widen that advantage

Lenders look at risk. Companies with a higher ESG score paid lower interest on their debt, for the total score and for each letter. In the pandemic years, however, the score brought no extra discount: the crisis weighed more than ESG.

12 In practice, limits and credits

ESG is also a financial decision

Managers

The author recommends bringing ESG into financial decisions, especially governance, to obtain cheaper credit and more resilience to crises.

Investors

For the author, the ESG score is a signal of resilience, more valuable in uncertain times, as the extra effect on value and profit during the pandemic showed.

Boards

Our reading: in B3 companies with higher ESG scores, executive pay tracked performance more closely, which matters to those who design pay policy.

Regulators

The author argues for rules that promote authentic and transparent ESG practices that contribute to the economy, society and the environment.

What this research does not say

  • Six years (2018–2023): too short to see the long-term effects of ESG and of the pandemic. · according to the author
  • Only listed Latin American companies, and Study 1 only Brazilian ones: the results may not hold for private, small or other regions' companies. · according to the author
  • Scores from a single database (Refinitiv), which may have flaws, treated the same way in every sector. · according to the author
  • Association, not causation; the models explain less than 20% of the variation, and we show direction and strength, not size. · our note
