In on-campus college, the virtual environment is used for what it offers

At a private college in the Brazilian state of Espírito Santo, 101 students and 60 teachers rated 67 statements about the virtual learning environment (VLE), the website that holds classes, materials and forums. The VLE’s resources weighed most on its use. And those who report more use also report more co-creation (teacher and student building the class together) and more quality, performance and satisfaction. Explained in 10 screens.

Author: Ketter Valéria Zuchi Caliari Advisor: Prof. Dr. Gilberto Perez Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

02 The vocabulary

Co-creating value means teacher and student building the class together

The idea comes from marketing: a service’s value is not handed over ready-made; it emerges from the exchange between provider and user. For teaching, the author combined three facets of the DART model (Prahalad and Ramaswamy) with three facets specific to the classroom. Tap a facet:

Chart 8, section 5.8 and Chart 12. Each facet was measured with 5 or 6 statements, scored from 1 (strongly disagree) to 10 (strongly agree). The original DART has a fourth letter, risk, which the thesis did not use.

04 What it found · the interviews

In the interviews, the topic that kept coming back was access

The author counted the words and expressions the six teachers used most. Access came up 50 times, ahead of VLE use itself (44). Participation, one of the facets of co-creation, came up only 7 times. See each interview:

Table 3 (content analysis, Bardin). The row totals add up. Red bar = the most cited word in the selection; blue = participation. Counting words shows what was talked about, not whether it was praise or complaint.

05 What it found · reading the interviews

Teachers use the VLE more to hand out materials than to talk

For the author, learning in the VLE happens much more through materials (video lessons, slides, texts, practice tests) than through collaboration tools. Interaction, participation and collaboration show up in the interviews, but are still becoming effective. Tap an item:

06 The hypotheses

The thesis started from five linked bets

Three things would lead to VLE use; use would lead to co-creation; and co-creation to better outcomes. Place your bets before seeing the data:

Section 5.10 and Figure 14. Answers were analysed with structural equations (PLS, in SmartPLS 2.3.0), a technique that measures several arrows at once. Each bet was tested three times: students only, teachers only and everyone together.

07 What it found · the first three bets

The VLE’s resources weighed more on use than the network and skills

The coefficient (β) runs from 0 to 1 and shows how much each thing goes together with use, holding the others constant. In all three groups, the resources (materials, forum, e-mail, question bank) came well ahead. Switch the group:

Table 15 (standardized coefficients) and Tables 6, 9 and 12 (R²). Light grey bar = did not pass the test (t below about 2, with 300 resamples). Bars start at zero. In the teachers’ group, 3 of the 67 statements were dropped from the model for measuring poorly.

08 The unexpected

Teachers complained about the network, yet overall it barely changed use

Five of the six interviewees criticized the internet or the computers. In the questionnaire, infrastructure passed the test among students and among teachers, but with everyone together the coefficient fell to almost zero. And among students, technology skills did not pass. Tap a cell:

Tables 15 and 16 and category C3 (our count: E1 to E5 criticize the infrastructure). Solid cell = passed the test; dashed = did not pass. The explanations are the author’s: many use the VLE on their own phone or laptop, and the statement about training was the one that least tracked students’ skills.

09 The finding · the last two bets

More VLE use goes with more co-creation, and more co-creation with more satisfaction

The last two arrows were the strongest in the model, in all three groups. Use explains about two thirds of the differences in co-creation; co-creation, about three quarters of the differences in perceived quality, performance and satisfaction. Switch the group and the arrow:

Table 15 (β and t) and Tables 6, 9 and 12 (R²); every arrow passed the test. Height = R², from zero; the light slab at the top marks 100%. "VLE use" was measured with 6 statements about using the VLE in every course and about the college’s encouragement and effort to expand its use. Everything comes from the same questionnaire, answered once: the arrows show association, they do not prove cause.

10 So what? · limits and credits

The VLE pays off more when the college invests in resources and the teacher drives the conversation

For college leaders

Use the model to plan the VLE as a space for co-creation: access, transparency, interaction, participation, collaboration and learning experiences should be institutionalized by the college. The interviews call for a simpler, more interactive environment. (author)

For teachers

The teacher’s engagement is decisive: the teacher is the one who encourages use and turns the materials depot into exchange. Forums, group assignments and questions to answer in the VLE are examples cited in the interviews. (author and interviews)

For researchers

Repeat the study at other colleges, including public ones, with more students and teachers; interview students too; study active learning methods; compare by gender, course and age; refine the questionnaire. (author)

What this research does not say

  • A single college. Two campuses of one private institution; the spread across courses was uneven, and tutors, technical staff and coordinators were not heard. · according to the author
  • Few teachers. 60 questionnaire answers; in this group, 3 statements had to be dropped from the model. · according to the author
  • It is all perception. Quality and performance are what people say they perceive; the thesis did not look at grades, pass rates or dropout. · our observation
  • One questionnaire, one moment. The same people answered about use, co-creation and outcomes at once: the arrows show association, not cause. · our observation
  • Selected interviewees. The six teachers were nominated by management for standing out in VLE use: it is the view of those who already use it well. · section 5.5
