Four ways of being a present father

When both partners have careers, who looks after the child? The thesis listened to 27 fathers in couples where husband and wife both work and invest in their careers. All of them say they want to be present fathers, but they live it in four ways, depending on how much of the care they take on and how clearly they know what kind of father they want to be. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The problem

Women went out to work, but the home still weighs more on them

The thesis starts from a change: with husband and wife both working, men have moved closer to the home and the children. But the IBGE (Brazil's statistics office) figures the author cites show the split is still far from equal. Pick a measure:

PNAD Contínua 2016 (IBGE, 2017), cited in section 2.5 of the thesis: this is national context, not a result of the interviews.

One word sums up the problem, according to the literature the thesis reviews:

“I help out at home.”
HelpingThe task is still hers; he does it when he can, when something is missing or when he likes it.
Being responsibleThe task belongs to both; he remembers, plans and does it without waiting to be asked.

The distinction between "helping" and "taking it on" comes from Wagner et al. (2005), Jablonski (2010) and Bruschini and Ricoldi (2012), cited in the thesis, and it comes back in the results.

06 How the research looked · thematic analysis

From 27 stories to four types, layer by layer

Each interview was transcribed word for word. The author tagged passages by theme, split themes into sub-themes, summarized each story and went back to the start several times, with the help of the NVivo software. In the end, three dimensions were enough to describe the fathers. Tap a layer:

07 What it found · the thesis model

Two questions set the fathers apart: how much care he takes on, and how clearly he knows what father he wants to be

All 27 came across as close to the "new father", present and involved. What changes is the intensity. Crossing the two questions, the author arrived at four types of active fatherhood. Tap a quadrant:

Figure 5 and Table 10 of the thesis. Each dot is one father; the thesis only says high or low on each axis, so the position inside a quadrant is illustrative. For the author, no type is better than another, and a father can move to another quadrant over time.

10 The unexpected

In all four types, even the most involved, she still carries more

Even the fathers in the full type, who share the most, say their partner is overloaded. Tap each card to see, on the back, how the thesis describes the split of roles in that type:

11 Fatherhood and the workplace

12 So what?

Companies that think about families need to see fathers too

For companies

  • Put fatherhood on the agenda, not just motherhood: policies designed for the family. (author)
  • Do not let the use of a benefit depend on the boss's goodwill. (author)
  • Take the types of fatherhood into account in men's professional development. (author)

For fathers and couples

  • Ask yourself what kind of father you want to be: in the thesis, that awareness is what most shapes each man's way. (author)
  • Swap "helping" for "being responsible together". (our reading)

For researchers

  • Study how a child's arrival affects a man's career, which also stops or slows down. (author)
  • Look at the weight of national and company culture. (author)

What this research does not say

  • Only his voice. The partners were not interviewed; hearing them would broaden the picture of the couple. · according to the author
  • Only heterosexual couples and a "dual career" sample that is hard to pin down: who counts as career-focused was self-reported by the father. · according to the author
  • Types, not measurements. Each father's position comes from reading the story he told; 27 fathers, found through personal networks, do not represent all fathers in Brazil, and the conformist type has only 2. · our note
