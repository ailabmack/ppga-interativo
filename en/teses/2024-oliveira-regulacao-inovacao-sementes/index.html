The rules of the game and innovation in maize seeds

Biosafety laws, plant variety protection, labelling: every country writes its own. The thesis gathers 156 rules from 12 countries, asks five experts to score each one and compares those scores, year by year, with maize seed patents. The question: do rules help or hinder innovation? Explained in 12 screens.

12 So what?

Predictable rules matter more than identical rules

For the author, a rule's effectiveness lies not in its rigidity but in its predictability, clarity and fit with the country's economic system.

For governments and regulators

  • In long, risky research such as biotechnology, a stable, predictable, science-based process is worth more than direct subsidies, says the author.
  • Regulate the product (its safety and efficacy) rather than the process that created it.
  • Design policies that fit the country's strengths, without copying models.

For agritech companies

  • Assess each country's "regulatory risk" as a central part of international strategy, beyond market size.
  • Talk to regulators early and often, as partners, according to the author.

For researchers

  • Interview the experts to understand the scores behind counter-intuitive results, such as intellectual property in Canada.
  • Apply the method to other heavily regulated sectors and look beyond patents (startups, venture capital).

What this research does not say

  • Patents are not all of innovation. In agribusiness, much protection comes from plant varieties and trade secrets. · according to the author
  • Few years per country. Each regression has 19 years and up to five themes, and scores change in steps (in Japan, the same from 2005 to 2018). · our observation
  • Intertwined themes. Standardised coefficients above 1 (in Australia, from −4.3 to +3.5) point to themes that move together; read the direction, not the size. · our observation
  • Moving together is not causing. Scores and patents may simply follow the same rhythm over time. · our observation
  • Five viewpoints. The scores come from three researchers at a Brazilian federal institution and two agronomists at Chinese multinationals. · our observation, from the thesis's method
