Doctoral thesis · PPGA Mackenzie · 2022

Does knowing about finance and taxes help you decide for the business?

Checking the interest rate before taking a loan, working out how long an investment takes to pay back, keeping household and business accounts apart. The thesis surveyed 232 Brazilian managers and entrepreneurs to see whether those who know more about money and taxes say they take more care with these decisions. Explained in 12 screens.

Out of every 100 firms opened in Brazil, 77 close before ten years

03 What was already known

Of 888 articles found, 11 dealt with literacy inside businesses

In August 2021, the author searched a database of business journals (EBSCO) for articles from 2018 to 2021, using five English terms. The search returned a lot from outside the topic. By title, 36 remained; after reading abstracts and full texts, 11: six on financial literacy and five on tax literacy. 04 The thesis idea

Each literacy has two storeys: what the person knows and what they do

Literacy means being able to handle a subject in practice. The thesis measures two: personal financial literacy, about one’s own money, and tax literacy, about the firm’s taxes. Each combines five knowledge questions and five habits, from “never” to “always”. On the right, the yardstick for business decisions. Tap a block:

Questionnaire in Appendix 1, adapted from an OECD model (Atkinson and Messy, 2016) and tested twice before data collection. Knowledge score from 1 (zero or one right) to 5 (five right), Tables 5 and 7. The thesis does not publish the answer key to the tax questions or how many got each question right. Storeys of equal height: it is only a drawing.

05 How the research looked

Of 322 responses, 232 managers and entrepreneurs remained

The plan was to survey São Paulo retail workers. With the pandemic, more than 20,000 e-mails brought few replies, so the author also sent the questionnaire to personal contacts and through social media. He gathered 163 responses from his own collection and 159 from a Boa Vista (SPC) database. Duplicates and those who skipped the tax questions were removed.

07 How the research looked

In each literacy score, what people do weighed more than what they know

The author used structural equation modelling (PLS-SEM), which combines several answers into a single score. First, he summarised the habits: people with good habits with their own money tend to have good habits with the firm’s taxes (β 0.524; explains 27.5%). Then he combined habits and knowledge into each literacy. The loading shows how well each part fits the final score; below 0.5, the part is dropped. Switch the block:

Figures 4 and 6, Tables 12, 15, 18 and 19. Height = loading (0 to 1). The thesis itself notes low reliability in both literacies (Cronbach’s alpha 0.487 and 0.466), which have only two parts each.

08 What it found

Both literacies go along with more good practices in the business

People with higher scores on both literacies say they follow the good decision practices more often. Financial literacy also goes along with tax literacy. On the side of who the person is, only education and sex made a difference, and only for financial literacy. Tap an arrow or switch sides:

Final model, Figure 6 and Table 23, with 1,000 resamples. Thickness = standardised coefficient (β), written on the literacy arrows and on the buttons; solid arrow = t above 1.96 (significant at 5%); dashed = no clear effect. The R² inside each block is how much of it the model explains. It is association: the research does not test cause and effect.

10 The finding

Tax literacy explains more of the good practices than financial literacy does

Together, the two literacies explain 65.2% of the differences between people on the good-practice yardstick. Take one out of the model at a time and see how much the glass empties: without tax literacy it loses more than without financial literacy.

Tables 22 and 24 and Figures 6 to 8. f² above 0.35 is a large effect; between 0.15 and 0.35, medium (Hair and colleagues, cited in the thesis).

11 The unexpected

Education seems to reach tax literacy through financial literacy

Quick question: in the final model, do people with more schooling have higher tax literacy?

our reading
Figures 6 and 7 and Table 23. The thesis shows the model without financial literacy (Figure 7) only to compute the f² and does not publish that model’s t values; nor does it test indirect effects. That education “passes through” financial literacy is our reading of the two figures.

12 So what? · limits and credits

Understanding taxes and money goes along with deciding more carefully

For people running a business

For the author, the financial literacy of people running a small or medium-sized firm is “not a luxury but a necessity”, and knowing the tax and financial field helps them act more effectively. Here, habits weighed more than knowledge in both scores. (author; the last point is from the thesis data)

For schools and public policy

Since 2020, financial education has been part of Brazil’s national school curriculum (BNCC). For the author, bringing financial and tax knowledge to business owners is a major step, and schools can help. In this sample, schooling was what weighed most on financial literacy. (author)

For researchers

Repeat with larger samples, sector by sector, in other regions and countries, and also measure the firms’ growth and performance, which this research did not measure. (author)

What this research does not say

  • Small convenience sample. 232 people from mixed sectors, gathered during the pandemic through contacts and a credit database: it does not hold for all firms. · according to the author
  • Self-reported frequency, not outcomes. “Decision quality” is how often people say they follow six practices, not whether the decisions worked; 21% had never been managers. · our observation
  • All in the same questionnaire. Similar habits and practices were answered at once, by the same people; part of the strong link may come from that. · our observation
  • How much they know is unknown. The thesis does not publish how many got the knowledge questions right, nor the tax questions’ answer key. · our observation
  • Association, not cause. A single survey, without tests of indirect effects; and the literacy scores have low reliability, as the thesis says. · our observation
