Doctoral thesis · PPGA Mackenzie · 2024

In a tech startup, age has rules too

Nobody writes in a handbook what the right age is for each place, but people know. The thesis followed a fully remote software startup founded by young people for about three months and showed where these age norms come from and what they do: they weigh on who gets hired, on the labels people give colleagues, on conflict and on the wish to leave. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The starting idea · age norms

Age norms are expectations the group enforces

"Act your age." The phrase sums up the idea: each place expects a certain behaviour and a certain position at each age. Phrases like these, quoted by the thesis from Barbara Lawrence, show the norm at work:

“How did she manage to secure that job, she is so young.”
“If he doesn’t get promoted now, he may never have another opportunity, he is too old for it.”

04 The method · netnography

The research followed a company that exists only online

The company has no office: people work from several countries over Zoom, Slack and other tools. So the research used netnography, ethnography done in an online setting: observing, talking and reading what the community produces. There were three sources. Choose one:

12 Takeaways

Noticing age norms helps decide whether they make sense

For the author, age norms form without people noticing. Understanding them helps improve the company’s culture, hiring and practices.

For founders and leaders

  • Founders’ beliefs become house rules: it pays to look at them early, while the structure is still being formed.
  • Benefits and long-term plans weigh on the decision to stay.

For recruiters

  • "Culture fit" can hide an age preference. (our reading)
  • Noting a candidate’s age in the feedback, as the thesis found, weighs on the choice.

For employees

  • Labels such as "younger people can’t manage" and "older people can’t adapt" show up without being noticed.
  • Conflict between generations in a team can come from these expectations.

What this research does not say

  • Other companies and sectors. It is a single tech startup; the author suggests studying older companies and other sectors. · per the author
  • How the norms change over time. A longer study would suit a startup, and the observation was online only. · per the author
  • Penalties. The research could not show that people who break the norm are penalized, or whether dismissals had to do with age. · per the author
  • An insider’s view. The researcher had worked at the company, which helps understanding and may sway the reading. · our note
  • How many think this way. There are 15 interviews, with no count of statements; and some quotes carry different codes in different passages. · our note
