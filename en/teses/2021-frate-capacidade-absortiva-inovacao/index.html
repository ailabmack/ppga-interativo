Doctoral thesis · PPGA Mackenzie · 2021

How what Metrô managers learn turns into innovation

With 192 supervisors and coordinators of the São Paulo Metro, the thesis follows the path of knowledge that comes from outside: those who say they seek it out and understand it also say they transform and apply it, and those who apply it say they innovate more at work. With more autonomy, that link gets stronger. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The problem

A company learns through what each person brings in from outside

Absorptive capacity is the ability to recognise outside knowledge, understand it and use it (Cohen and Levinthal, 1990). The thesis looks at this capacity in each person, in four steps. Each person’s background is usually measured by education and work experience. The thesis added a third part, life experience: family, friends, home. Answer as the managers did, from 1 (never) to 5 (always). “You see yourself as someone who…”

09 The discovery

With more autonomy, applying what one learns weighs more in innovation

Autonomy at work is the freedom to plan, decide and choose how to do things. The thesis found no literature testing it this way: as a dial that changes the strength of the link between applying and innovating. Move autonomy:

Table 13 and Figure 21 of the thesis, redrawn. Autonomy × applying → innovating interaction: 0.193 (p = 0.005). Because the bootstrap gave a two-peaked distribution, the author used Kock’s (2018) standard error. Slopes recalculated by us: 0.638 ± 0.193 (0.445 with autonomy 1 standard deviation below the mean; 0.831 with 1 above); the height of each line uses the 0.017 for autonomy on its own, which appears only in the diagram (Figure 19), untested. 11 What did not hold

Enjoying the job helps people seek out the new, but not apply it

Intrinsic motivation is doing the job because one enjoys it. The thesis bet that it would help with all four steps of absorbing. It helped only with the first two. And autonomy shows up again: those with more freedom say they enjoy their work more. Choose a link:

12 So what?

Giving freedom and encouraging the search for outside knowledge may yield more ideas put into practice

For the author, the results work as a diagnosis for the Metro and for other companies that want more innovative behaviour from their managers.

For managers and HR

  • Give more autonomy day to day: it goes with more motivation and strengthens the link between applying and innovating. (author)
  • Use the scales to plan training and the allocation of people to roles. (author)

For team leaders

  • Education is the part of the background that can be worked on deliberately; work and life experience come with each person. (author)
  • Our reading: the weakest link in the portrait is winning support for ideas; it is worth making room for them to be heard.

For researchers

  • Test the scales at other levels of the Metro and in companies from other sectors. (author)
  • Relate motivation and absorptive capacity to organisational commitment; study leadership and work environment. (author)

What this research does not say

  • One company and one group of roles. A non-random sample of supervisors and coordinators in a public company with job stability; the results cannot be generalised. · according to the author
  • A single moment. The data are from 2021; under different management, the results may change. · according to the author
  • Everyone rated themselves. The same person answered about what they seek, apply and innovate; the questionnaire shows what goes together, not what causes what. · our observation
  • Indirect paths not tested. The thesis says background and motivation reach innovation indirectly, but did not test these effects; the calculations on screen 10 are ours. · our observation
  • Autonomy by another method. The autonomy effect could only be tested with Kock’s standard error, because the bootstrap gave a two-peaked result. · our observation
