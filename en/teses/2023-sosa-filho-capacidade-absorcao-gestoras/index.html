What makes a fund manager innovate with knowledge from outside?

Investment managers live on information: reports, talks with consultants, banks and government. The thesis listened to 6 fund managers and 250 professionals at asset managers to see what turns this knowledge into new funds, processes and strategies, and what role ties with the market play. 02 The concept · absorptive capacity

Absorbing outside knowledge takes four steps, in two halves

The research uses Zahra and George’s (2002) idea of absorptive capacity. The first half, potential capacity, is finding and understanding what comes from outside. The second, realized capacity, is combining it with what the firm already knows and applying it. Tap each layer:

Thesis terms: acquisition, assimilation, transformation and exploitation (section 2.1). We use everyday verbs instead.

05 The method · mixed methods

First six conversations, then 250 questionnaires

  1. 6 interviews with managers of six independent asset managers in February 2023 (8h38 in total), to check whether the ideas made sense in daily work.
  2. 8 reviewers (professors and sector professionals) reviewed the questions translated from foreign scales: 50 statements became 45.
  3. 77 test answers (February and March) cleaned up the measure: 5 statements dropped out, 40 remained. Each is rated from 1 to 10.
  4. 250 professionals answered by June 2023 (the 77 test answers plus 173 new ones). A structural equation model (PLS-SEM) measured the links between the ideas.

Section 3.2.2. The author started from the 400 largest managers in the ANBIMA ranking and looked up on LinkedIn the first two investment professionals at each one. Of the 800 contacted, 324 were already his contacts. The power calculation (G*Power) required at least 77 answers.

09 The discovery

Finding and understanding only reach innovation by way of combining and applying

Firms that find and understand more knowledge also combine and apply more, and these are the ones that innovate more. The direct link between finding and innovating, however, is indistinguishable from chance. Switch the path:

Tables 7 and 8 (250 answers). The numbers are standardized coefficients: how much, on average, one measure moves with another (0 = not at all; 1 = completely together). p below 0.05 = an effect distinguishable from chance. The model explains 54% of the differences in innovation between answers and 70% of those in combining and applying. The indirect effect (0.54, Table 8) is computed separately by the software and is not the exact product of the two arrows. As everything came from the same questionnaire, “by way of” means moving together, not a proven cause.

10 The unexpected · ties with the market

Strong ties with the market did not bring more innovation

In the interviews, trust and frequent contact with consultants, banks and other managers seemed essential. In the numbers, the direct link between ties and innovation was zero:

−0.007ties with the market → innovation (p = 0.889, Table 7)

What is more: where ties are stronger, the passage from finding and understanding to combining and applying becomes weaker, not stronger. Change the level of ties:

Moderation from Table 10 (−0.106; p = 0.001). Slopes recalculated by us: average effect of 0.767 (Figure 18) plus or minus 0.106, for ties one standard deviation above or below the average. The thesis sees here the risk of a “protective” network, closed around a few trusted sources, as Navas and colleagues (2019) had pointed out.

12 So what?

To innovate, a firm needs routines that carry knowledge all the way to the decision

Buying reports and having good sources is necessary, but not enough. What sets innovating firms apart is joining the new with what they already know and taking it into funds, processes and strategies.

For those running asset managers

  • The author suggests reviewing internal processes for combining and applying knowledge, not just investing in finding it.
  • Firms managing liquid funds, under more pressure from redemptions, depend even more on these routines, in the author’s reading.

For the network of relationships

  • The author recommends mapping relationships of trust with consultants, banks, distributors and government.
  • But he flags the risk: strong, repeated relationships can tie the firm to few sources. Diversifying the network may be worth as much as deepening it (our reading).

For research

  • Follow the firms over time instead of a single snapshot.
  • Test ties inside the firm itself and each type of tie separately; take the model and the 40-statement Portuguese measure to other sectors.

What this research does not say

  • A snapshot of one moment. Absorbing knowledge and innovating take time; the questionnaire measured everything at once, in the first half of 2023. · according to the author
  • An invited sample. Professionals found on LinkedIn at the 400 largest firms; 324 of the 800 contacted were already the author’s contacts. It does not represent all 953 firms. · our observation, section 3.2.2
  • Each person speaks about their own firm. Innovation, ties and steps are respondents’ perceptions, not company measures (such as the number of funds launched). · our observation
  • Two halves very close together. Finding and understanding and combining and applying move almost together (correlation of 0.83), at the limit of the test that tells the two measures apart. · our observation, Table 6
