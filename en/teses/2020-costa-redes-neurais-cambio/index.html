A neural network with memory beat “tomorrow equals today” on exchange rates

The thesis mapped 245 articles on neural networks and exchange rates, then tested an LSTM network on the real, the euro and the pound against the dollar, one day ahead, in 2019. Error fell by 13% to 31%; the direction was right on 56% to 80% of days. In 11 screens.

02 The problem · Introduction

Since 1983, the guess “tomorrow equals today” has been hard to beat

In 1983, Meese and Rogoff showed that economic models almost never forecast exchange rates better than repeating today’s rate, the so-called random walk. The thesis notes that the debate goes on and asks whether neural networks, which learn patterns from data, can do better.

Tomorrow = today. That is the opponent in every screen that follows.

The thesis also uses ARIMA, a classic statistical model that looks at the latest changes in the series. It joins the contest as a second opponent.

08 Result · Tables 3.1, 3.5 and 3.9

For the real and the pound, the best network had one layer and three neurons

The author tested 19 designs: 1 to 5 neurons in one layer, or two layers with up to 5 neurons each. The one-neuron network was the worst of the 19 in all three currencies; for the euro, a two-layer one (5 and 3) won. For the author, one or two layers are enough. Tap a square.

09 Result · Tables 3.2, 3.6 and 3.10

Only networks made the best group: 9 for the real, 6 for the euro and 12 for the pound

A small difference can be luck. The model confidence set (MCS) test removes the worst forecaster, one at a time, until those left are tied within the margin of chance. The α setting decides how strict it is; the thesis uses 10% and 25%. Move the slider.

10 Result · Tables 3.4, 3.8 and 3.12

The network got right whether the rate would rise or fall on 56% to 80% of the test days

The “tomorrow equals today” guess never says up or down, so the thesis compares each network with always picking the more common side (about 50%). By our count, 259 coin tosses land between 44% and 56% in 95% of cases. Toss the coins.

11 In practice · limits · credits

A reference network for forecasting exchange rates one day ahead, not a buy signal

Researchers

The author proposes LSTM as the network to beat and the day’s four prices as inputs, and suggests widening the review with other databases (Web of Science, ProQuest, EBSCO).

Investors

Nothing here says when to buy or sell currency. The thesis does not simulate trades, costs or taxes, and getting the direction right is not making a profit.

Firms and government

The exchange rate weighs on trade, competitiveness and foreign debt. The thesis shows a gain at a one-day horizon, in 2019; horizons of weeks or months were not tested (our reading).

Teachers and students

A good example of a fair comparison: the same test days, a naive opponent and a test that separates winning from luck (the MCS).

What this research does not say

  • A forecast is not a profit. There is no simulation of buying and selling, bid-ask spreads, costs or taxes. · our note
  • One test year, one day ahead. The 260 test days run from December 2018 to December 2019. The test is worth repeating in other years before trusting such high direction hit rates. · our reading
  • Three currencies, all against the dollar. Real, euro and pound; other currencies and other inputs may give another result. · our note
  • Review in a single database. The 245 articles come only from Scopus. · according to the author
