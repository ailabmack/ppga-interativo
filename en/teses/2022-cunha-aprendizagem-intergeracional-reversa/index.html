What people over 40 learn from their younger colleagues

With 109 professionals over 40 who work with people under 30, the thesis measures learning that runs from the younger to the older. What people learn most is the technology of their own job; those closed to learning from the young say they learn less, and those motivated by the young people’s education say they learn more. Explained in 12 screens.

Author: Janine Silva Nascimento Cunha Advisor: Prof. Dr. Diógenes de Souza Bido Programme: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

    08 What it found · the scoreboard

    Two barriers hinder and motivation helps

    People who say they are closed to learning from the young and who see the company environment as unsuitable say they learn less. Those who became motivated by the young people’s education say they learn more. Three of eight bets were confirmed. Tap a block or a card:

    09 The discovery

    Seeing young people arrive well educated motivates older workers to learn

    Motivation emerged from the interviews: young people arrive with degrees and postgraduate studies, and this awakens in older workers the wish to train. It is the factor with the greatest weight in the model. Move the three levers:

    12 So what?

    Companies can measure what is learned between generations and what gets in the way

    For the author, the two scales work as a diagnosis: they show what older workers learn from the young and where the barriers are, by area or team.

    For managers and HR

    • Use the scales to compare areas and teams and find the barriers that weigh there. (author)
    • Look after the environment: encourage multigenerational teams, integration moments and intergenerational projects. (author)

    For people over 40

    • Our reading: openness counts more than age. What weighed most was the willingness to learn from the young, not years at the company.
    • The young people’s education can be a spur to invest in one’s own, as the interviewees said. (author)

    For people under 30

    • Our reading: what you know about the job’s systems and technology is what older colleagues most say they learn; it is worth offering help.
    • The thesis heard only the older side; what young people feel when teaching is left for further research. (author)

    What this research does not say

    • Small, varied sample. 109 people from various sectors, three in four from the public sector, most with postgraduate degrees. · according to the author
    • Smaller age gap than planned. The cut-off dropped from 50 to 40; 30% are 41 to 49. With a larger gap, the barriers might have shown up more. · according to the author
    • Low barriers. With so little agreement, there is little variation left for the barriers to show an effect; that is why only two appeared, the author says. · according to the author
    • One side, one moment. Everything comes from what the older worker reported in one questionnaire: it shows what goes together, not what causes what, and it does not observe learning itself. · our observation
    • Model adjusted to the data. The measures were refined with the same sample (statements and barriers removed or merged); the scales are worth testing on another group. · our observation
