Why Generation Z stays in a job, or leaves

Young people born between 1997 and 2010 bring eight symbols of dignity to work, such as respect, security and recognition. The thesis listened to 121 of them and shows that staying or leaving depends less on how many symbols the company delivers and more on whether it gets right what is central to each person. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The problem

Every young person who leaves costs the company dearly

Generation Z changes jobs often, so-called job hopping. Replacing an employee usually costs 50% to 60% of their annual salary, and can reach 200% in roles that require specific skills, according to a study cited in the thesis. 10 The central finding

What matters is not how many symbols match, but whether the central one does

A misalignment in a symbol that is central to the person can be enough to make them leave, even with everything else aligned. Several problems in peripheral symbols, by contrast, are tolerated as long as the central one is preserved. Build a person: mark with ★ what is central to them and say what the company delivers.

Our illustrative simulation, using the rule of the thesis's final model (Figure 8): a misaligned central symbol leads to leaving (or the intention to leave); central symbols preserved with problems around them lead to conditional staying; everything aligned leads to genuine staying. In real life, what is central varies from person to person and over time.

11 The unexpected · discussion circles

For many young people, a formal job became a stage and contracting a gamble

In Brazil, a formal job under the labour code (CLT) comes with paid holidays, sick leave and severance. "Pejotização" means hiring people as contractors (PJ, a legal entity) instead. It came up so often in the interviews that the author took it to the discussion circles. Young people know the risks, but many still want to be PJ. 12 So what?

Changing jobs can also be a search for dignity

For the author, job hopping is neither disloyalty nor immaturity: people leave when the symbols the company delivers no longer match what they consider necessary to feel dignified. Dignity, the thesis says, is negotiated every day in the gap between what is promised and what is delivered.

Words and practice that match

  • Audit not only the employer brand, but what is delivered day to day.
  • There is no point promising autonomy when hiring and then managing by micromanagement.

Careers as portfolios

  • Learning paths, rotation between areas and projects with a clear beginning, middle and end.
  • Make room for employees' passions, causes and side projects.

Contractor hiring

  • Present formal employment (CLT) as a set of rights and protections, not as red tape.
  • Even in a legitimate contractor arrangement, provide by other means the conditions missing without those guarantees.

What this research does not say

  • How many people think this way. It is qualitative research: it shows patterns of meaning, without prevalence or cause-and-effect relationships. · according to the author
  • What it would be like elsewhere. Only in Brazil, with young urban people in the formal market, with an emphasis on those with a university degree and previous work experience. · according to the author
  • Whether it holds for other generations or over time. The symbols were captured at a single moment; what is central may change. · according to the author
  • What is central to whom, measured. The fits and reasons were classified by the researcher from what people said; the thesis did not ask people to rank the symbols. · our observation
