Doctoral thesis · PPGA Mackenzie · 2020

Do people who learn better perform better? And what lies along the way?

Learning is not only knowing: it is also feeling and doing. The thesis asked 204 employees at Petrobras’s research centre how they learn, how they absorb new knowledge and how they rate their own work. Explained in 12 screens.

Author: Adeildo Pereira dos Santos Filho Advisor: Prof. Dr. Silvio Popadiuk Co-advisor: Prof. Dr. Aparecida Laino Entriel Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

09 The finding

Much of the link between learning and performing runs through absorbing

People who say they learn better also rate their work higher. When absorptive capacity enters in the middle, the direct link shrinks and more than half of the path goes through it. Switch the model:

19,2%of performance differences explained
0,439direct link learning → performance

Table 18 and Figures 17 and 19 (204 answers). Numbers are standardised coefficients: how much one measure moves with another (0 = not at all; 1 = completely together). Path through absorbing: 0.633 × 0.399 = 0.253, the thesis’s own calculation; the direct link that remains (0.175, p = 0.020) still differs from chance, hence “partial mediation”. The thesis does not print a separate test of the indirect path.

10 The unexpected · willingness to innovate

Willingness to innovate did not change the strength of the link, but the groups tell another story

With all 204 together, the moderation came out at 0.035 (p = 0.682): no effect. But the author also ran the model in each group. Your guess: in the medium-willingness group, does learning still move with performance?

Table 18, models 1.1/1.2 and 2.1/2.2. Striped column = cannot be told apart from chance. The thesis does not test whether the difference between groups is itself significant, and 67 answers give the test less power (our reading). In the diagram of the moderation model (Figure 20), willingness to innovate itself has an arrow of 0.483 to performance, larger than learning’s, but it was not a hypothesis and the thesis gives no p-value.

Of the 70 questionnaire statements, how many had different averages by group (ANOVA, p below 0.05):

Role, department and sex did not change the answers, according to the author. Age, education and time at the company did: people up to 35 years old and with under 10 years at the company gave lower scores on several steps of learning and absorbing. That is why the author sees education, including master’s and doctoral study, as a path for the research centre.

12 So what?

Training is not enough: people need help absorbing and using what they learn

In the author’s reading, performance follows from absorptive capacity, which in turn follows from the three ways of learning. It is not only technical knowledge that counts.

For people managers

  • The author suggests expanding development processes, including master’s and doctoral study in the R&D setting.
  • Pay attention to people with under 10 years at the company and up to 35 years old, who gave the lowest scores.

For training designers

  • Measure feeling and doing too, not only knowing: all three go into the learning score.
  • Create occasions to share and apply what was learned, the step where knowledge becomes work (our reading).

For research

  • Repeat the model in companies from other sectors and in multiple cases.
  • See how the company’s career system affects learning, absorbing and performance.

What this study does not say

  • Everyone rated themselves. Learning, absorbing and performance come from the person, compared with colleagues; scores may be inflated. · according to the author
  • One place only. Three research departments at Petrobras, not the whole company nor other companies. · according to the author
  • Moving together is not causation. Everything was measured at once, in the same questionnaire; “runs through” means moving together, not a proven effect. · our note
  • Groups without a test of the difference. The abstract mentions moderation in one group, but the tables do not confirm moderation; the difference between the groups was not tested. · our note, Tables 18 and 19
