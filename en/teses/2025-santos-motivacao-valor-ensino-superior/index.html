Doctoral thesis · PPGA Mackenzie · 2025

The value of a degree is decided by the experience of those who study

Why does a student feel a degree is worth what it costs? The thesis listened to 15 students of Psychology, Nutrition, Nursing and Speech Therapy at a private university center in Foz do Iguaçu, Brazil, about what motivates them and what gives value to their undergraduate course. Explained in 12 screens.

More than half of those who enter college in Brazil never finish the course

The author starts from a figure in the 2024 Map of Higher Education in Brazil, by the Semesp Institute. If students drop out, they never receive the whole service. How can we understand the value of a course that many never finish?

11 The finding · effort and reward

When effort does not come back as reward, motivation and the value of the course fall together

The main conclusion: reasons from within and from outside mix, and students lose heart when what they put into the course does not come back as learning, facilities or career prospects. Build an experience and watch the scale:

12 Takeaways

A course’s value depends on what it gives back for the effort of those who study

For the author, perceived value is complex: it weighs what you can touch (facilities, total cost) and what you cannot (teachers, reputation), and it changes over time.

For colleges

  • Invest in real-world experience: internships, labs, clinics and site visits.
  • Look after facilities and technology (rooms, equipment, internet).
  • Monitor the student experience with ongoing evaluations and adjust quickly.

For teachers and coordinators

  • Active learning, group work and hands-on exercises.
  • Closeness: answer questions, give feedback, recognize effort.
  • Training in educational technology, asked for by a third of the students.

For students and families

  • Count the costs beyond tuition: books, copies, transport, events. (our reading)
  • Ask about internships, labs and the routine for those who work during the day. (our reading)
  • The thesis suggests subsidies, bundles and flexibility to lower financial barriers.

What this research does not say

  • Rules for all students. These are 15 students aged 24 to 39, from four health courses, at a single institution. · according to the author
  • Why students drop out. All were enrolled; those who left the course were not heard. · our observation
  • The size of each effect. It is an exploratory study: the groups describe what people said and do not measure how much motivation weighs on value. · our observation
  • A change tracked over time. The change in opinion on quality was recalled at the end, not measured at two moments. · our observation
