Does sustainability pay off for a bank?

Banks lend the money that moves the economy, and they are pressed to adopt environmental, social and governance (ESG) practices. But does that raise or lower their profit? The thesis reviewed 40 studies and followed hundreds of listed banks from more than 50 countries, from 2014 to 2023. Explained in 12 screens.

02 To begin

A bank pollutes little, but decides who gets money

Banks have no smokestacks, so they are usually seen as a low environmental impact sector. The thesis points out that they affect the environment in two ways: directly, through their own operations, and indirectly, through the criteria they use to lend.

Governments and the UN also expect banks to finance the shift to a low-carbon economy. The thesis asks: do these practices improve or hurt banks' financial results? Earlier studies disagree.

03 Article 1 · literature review

Of 1,201 articles, 40 made it through the sieve

The first article searched two large research databases (Web of Science and Scopus) for studies that measure the effect of sustainability on banks' results.

To choose without bias, the author used the SMARTER method: each article gets a score on three criteria with fixed weights, and only the top-scoring quarter moves on. Then a reading removed those off topic.

04 Article 1 · what the review showed

The studies do not agree

The topic is recent: 15 of the 40 articles came out in 2023. In 14 of them, sustainability improved banks' profitability or value; in 7, it hurt them. The most used measure is the year's profit.

06 Article 2 · the country context

Does a country's credit flow through banks or the stock market?

The thesis splits countries by comparing bank credit to companies with the value of companies on the stock market, both as % of GDP. If bank credit is greater or equal, the country has banks at the center (bank-based); if not, the stock market at the center (market-based).

Where banks are at the center, they had much higher ESG scores in every pillar (Table 3.3, averages from 0 to 100):

07 Article 2 · result 1

Higher sustainability score, more profit and more value

In the 441 banks, higher ESG scores came with higher profitability (ROE) and higher market value (Tobin's Q). The result holds for the overall score and for each pillar: eight models, eight positive effects.

08 Article 2 · result 2

With banks at the center, more profit in the year and less value on the market

Being in a country with banks at the center of credit favored short-term profit (ROE) in all five models tested. But it lowered market value (Tobin's Q) in four out of five.

For the author, these systems give banks stability and margins, but they are more regulated and conservative: they invest less in innovative projects, and investors expect less from the future. The exception was the model with the governance score, in which value also rose.

09 Article 2 · the finding

Sustainability pays more where banks are at the center

The author tested whether the effect of the scores changes with the country's system. In all eight models, the score paid extra in countries with banks at the center, in both profit and value. The pillar with the largest extra effect was governance.

For the author, where banks finance the economy, relationships with clients are long and close, and sustainability becomes strategy. In countries with the market at the center, however, the score was negative for profit; for value, only governance had a proven effect, also negative.

10 Article 3 · the environmental side

Almost half assess the environmental risk of borrowers; fewer than 1 in 10 joined the UN initiative

The third article looks only at the environment, with three measures for the 579 banks:

  • Direct practice: the bank's environmental score (0 to 100), which reflects its own operations.
  • Indirect practice: does the bank assess the environmental and biodiversity risks of projects before financing them? (yes or no)
  • Policy: is the bank a member of the UN Environment Programme Finance Initiative (UNEP FI)? Members follow principles such as the Principles for Responsible Banking and the alliance for net zero emissions by 2050.

11 Article 3 · results

Environmental practices and policy go with more profit and value, and pay more together

Each of the three measures, on its own, came with higher market value and higher profitability: six models, six positive effects. And when the bank is also a UNEP FI member, the effect of the practices, direct or indirect, increases: four out of four.

12 In practice, limits and credits

Sustainability can be bank strategy, not just a cost

Investors and lenders

ESG criteria can serve as a complementary indicator of a bank's soundness and good risk management.

Bank managers

Consistent environmental practices can align profitability goals and sustainability commitments, especially in lending.

Regulators

The results support policies that require transparent, standardized social and environmental information.

Governments

Promoting sustainable practices in banking can align financial stability and sustainable development.

What this research does not say

  • Only listed banks. They tend to be more transparent and to have sounder governance, which may bias the sample. · according to the author
  • Environmental data are still poorly standardized, especially in emerging countries. · according to the author
  • Numbers only. The quantitative approach misses context factors that weigh on the relationship. · according to the author
  • Association, not proof of cause. The method (dynamic panel) handles part of the problem, but the effects per score point are small and measured within the year. · our note
