Does knowing how to handle household money help with the business’s cash decisions?

Credit or cash? The bank or the supplier? The thesis surveyed 163 owners and managers of small businesses in retail and services in Brazil’s Northeast to see what weighs on these everyday decisions: personal finances or the company’s finances. Explained in 11 screens.

03 The thesis idea

Handling household money and handling company money are different skills

Financial literacy means knowing how to deal with money. The thesis measures it in two parts: what people know (questions with a right answer) and what they do (habits, from “never” to “always”). And it separates personal literacy, about household finances, from business literacy, about the company’s finances. Tap a level:

On household questions, 77% right; on business ones, 31%

08 The unexpected

In the final model, what counted was what people do, not what they know

The plan was to measure each literacy with both levels. But when the author ran the statistical model, the scores on the knowledge questions did not fit with the habits and were dropped, in both towers. What remained as “literacy” was habits only. Run the model:

Section 4.5 and chapter 5: in the 2nd round, the knowledge scores had loadings below 0.5 and were dropped in the 3rd. For the author, people who already run a business learn finance in practice, and habit weighs more than formal knowledge. The two final towers move together (correlation of 0.694, Table 46).

09 What it found

Of the six links tested, only one held

The two hypotheses became six: each literacy with each group of decisions. Household finance habits were not linked to any decision. Business finance habits were linked to negotiating with suppliers. Among the controls, only age made a difference. Tap an arrow or switch the group:

11 So what? · limits and credits

For the business’s cash decisions, what counts is business finance practice

For small-business owners

Keeping household and business accounts apart, tracking cash flow, checking interest and terms before taking credit and using financial statements are the habits that went along with negotiating with suppliers instead of turning to the bank. In 2021, half (50.9%) said they always or often kept the accounts apart. (author)

For Sebrae, banks and trade associations

Training in business finance (reading and using the balance sheet, income statement and cash flow; knowing the financing options), designed for each region. Getting personal finance questions right guaranteed nothing for the business. (author)

For business schools and researchers

57% had a degree, many in Business or Accounting, yet most business questions were answered wrongly: for the author, formal education is not providing the right tools. She suggests testing the scales in other regions and sectors. (author)

What this research does not say

  • Convenience sample. Those who chose to answer, only from the Northeast and from retail and services: it does not apply to the whole country. · according to the author
  • New scales, tested on the same sample. Ideally they would be confirmed on another group; and the pandemic forced the use of few questions. · according to the author
  • Frequency, not quality. The decisions measure how often people make a given choice, not whether it was good for the business. · our observation
  • Only one firm link, and a small one. With customers and with cash and stock, business habits showed no clear effect; the model explains at most 8% of the differences. · our observation
  • All self-reported. Habits and decisions are what people said, at a single point, in 2021, with no data from the businesses’ accounts. · our observation
