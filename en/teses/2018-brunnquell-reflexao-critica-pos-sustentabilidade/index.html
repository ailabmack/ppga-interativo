Doctoral thesis · PPGA Mackenzie · 2018

In sustainability graduate courses, students' views change; their jobs, much less

The thesis followed two graduate courses in sustainability in São Paulo. Of the 24 students heard, 22 said the way they see the topic had changed. Only 11 managed to take that into their jobs: they lack the position, the autonomy and the room inside companies.

Author: Claudine Brunnquell Advisor: Prof. Dr. Janette Brunstein Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

04 How the research looked

32 people followed for a year and a half, inside and outside the classroom

Students wrote answers to reflective questions twice: at the start and near the end of the course. Some were also interviewed. 05 The thesis's yardstick

Thinking about sustainability is not the same as questioning it

The thesis sorts students' statements into three bands. Reflecting on asks what and how, to solve a problem that is already given (problem-solving). Critical reflection asks why: it questions the problem itself and the assumptions behind it (problem-posing). And deep transformation is when the way of seeing changes for good.

The author warns that the line between the bands is thin, and that statements come and go: someone questions assumptions at one moment and pulls back at the next, without returning to square one. That is why the thesis speaks of a continuum, not fixed steps.

06 What they reflected on

In both courses, students pointed out almost the same flaws in companies

Sustainability seen as a cost or an obligation, actions done for image, short-term focus, the topic stuck in one department: the criticisms repeat. For the author, this suggests that course design weighed little on what students think, and calls for further studies to understand why.

You can get outraged, but saying, look, which is the way and how are we going to do this, that's harder.Student in course Beta (B10); translated by us

    08 Barriers and ways out

    What blocks it is the area, the position and a company that leaves no room

    Those who did not change their work say they are in an area unrelated to the topic, that sustainability is stuck in one department, or that they lack autonomy to decide. Those who did change are usually linked to the area, hold a position with autonomy or started their own business.

    09 The unexpected

    The courses also feel the market they want to change

    Both courses have fewer and fewer students, and younger ones, in lower positions. To attract a class, Beta changed its name twice and says it will focus even more on tools. Alfa wonders whether a course only about sustainability should keep existing. For the author, the courses end up “suffering from the same ill they criticise”.

    10 What opens minds

    Experienced teachers, a mixed class and open conversation helped students reflect

    All students said the course spurred reflection and changes of mindset. In Beta, all of them credited some classroom activity, and 7 named a person (a teacher or the coordinator). Even so, classes were nearly always the usual ones: lectures, debate, case studies, visits, films. There were no immersions, art or meditation, which other experiences use to also touch values and emotions.

    There were several teachers and all of them came with a lot of experience.Student in course Beta (B11); translated by us

    Table 21 of the thesis. The question about activities was closed, so the thesis does not detail how each was run. Dashed items are practices the thesis cites from other courses that did not appear here.

    11 So what?

    The courses open room to think, but lean more toward solving than questioning

    Course coordinatorsThe author suggests giving more weight to questioning problems (problem-posing), without dropping the tools, and valuing teachers with experience in the topic and classes with varied profiles at admission.
    TeachersFor the author, students arrive open and willing to change, and that potential may be under-used when classes stay only on ready-made solutions.
    CompaniesOur reading: if the topic stays with one department and with people who lack autonomy, what employees learned in the course tends to stop at the company's door.

    What the research does not say

    • Shortly afterwards. It did not hear former students who graduated long ago; taking learning into work may take time. (author)
    • Two courses in São Paulo. Both created a whole sustainability programme; other formats, such as the topic spread across subjects, may give other results. (author)
    • What students report. Changes in views and practice are students' own accounts, not observed at work. (our note)
    • No count of who reached where. The author chose not to place each student at a level of reflection; only the change questions have counts. (author)

    Next steps suggested by the author: hear former students who have worked in the field for longer and compare courses that include sustainability in other ways.
