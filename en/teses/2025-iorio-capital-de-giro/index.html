How long does a company's money sit idle?

Between buying, storing, selling and getting paid, a company's money spends weeks tied up in day-to-day operations. This is working capital. The thesis followed Brazilian companies listed on the stock exchange (B3) from 2015 to 2024 to see how they managed this money before and after the pandemic, and whether it affected profits. 02 The idea

The working capital clock counts days

The company pays its supplier, keeps the goods in inventory, sells on credit and only later collects from the customer. The days its money stays tied up along this path make up the cash conversion cycle.

Cycle = days of inventory + days to collect − days to pay the supplier. Move the periods and watch the cycle change.

Two strategies. According to the thesis, conservative management keeps more inventory and more sales on credit (more safety, lower expected return); aggressive management trims these days and stretches supplier terms (higher expected return, more risk). The averages are from the 92 companies in article 3 (Table 3.2), rounded.

03 How the research looked

One question, three articles, two periods

How did Brazilian companies listed on B3 manage their working capital before and after the pandemic? The thesis answers in three steps.

04 Article 1 · 168 articles

After 2020, researchers paid more attention to working capital

The author combined the two largest databases of scientific articles in the field (Scopus and Web of Science) and kept 168 open-access articles on working capital, published from 2015 to 2024.

Publications per year jumped in 2020, the year the pandemic began, and did not return to their previous level.

05 Article 1 · who writes and where

Many wrote once; hardly anyone wrote several times

This is what Lotka's Law predicts: a few researchers publish a lot and many publish little. Something similar happens with journals (Bradford's Law): a small core concentrates a large share of the articles.

06 Article 3 · 92 companies · averages

After 2020, money stayed longer in inventory

Comparing the averages of the 92 companies, the cash cycle went from 94 to 103 days. Inventory drove it: 25.7% more days sitting idle.

Companies also took longer to pay suppliers and collected from customers slightly faster. For the thesis, this was a more flexible (conservative) management, with more slack to get through the uncertainty. Tap a pair of blocks.

07 Article 2 · 107 companies

Strong cash flow, large size and growing sales: a shorter cycle

Article 2 measured what goes along with a shorter or longer cycle. In both periods, companies that generate more cash from operations, that are larger and whose sales grow faster each quarter had shorter cycles.

The thesis measured working capital in two ways: in days (the cycle) and in money (the working capital requirement: how much of total assets is tied up in inventory and receivables, minus what suppliers finance).

08 Article 2 · before × after

The pandemic changed the strength, not the direction

The factors that shortened the cycle before 2020 kept shortening it afterwards. What changed was the size of the effect, and debt, which only started to matter after the pandemic.

09 Article 3 · the finding

Shorter cycle, slightly higher profit, before and after the pandemic

Among the 92 companies, those that kept their money idle for fewer days had a higher return on assets (ROA). This holds in both periods; after 2020, the relationship got stronger.

For the thesis, this argues for aggressive working capital management. But the effect per day is small, as the author himself points out. Shorten the cycle and see.

10 Article 3 · each period

After the pandemic, the time given to customers to pay weighed three times more

The thesis also tested each piece of the cycle. Before 2020, 30 more days to collect from customers came with an ROA 0.38 point lower; afterwards, 1.17 points lower.

Inventory days weighed little (and, after 2020, only with weak significance, at 10%). And the time taken to pay suppliers? It appears on the next screen.

12 In practice, limits and credits

Payment terms are day-to-day decisions, and they add up

Financial managers

Tracking inventory, collection and payment periods one by one helps explain profit: a shorter cycle came with a higher ROA in both periods.

In times of crisis

The relationships did not change direction with the pandemic; they changed intensity. After 2020, the time given to customers weighed more on profit.

Indebted companies

After the pandemic, more indebted companies had shorter cycles, which the thesis reads as greater care for liquidity amid high interest rates.

Researchers

Brazil barely appears in world research on the topic and had no international partnerships in the period; the thesis points to the scarcity of studies on Brazilian companies.

What this research does not say

  • Few companies, concentrated in some sectors: 107 and 92 listed companies, almost half of them in manufacturing. · according to the author
  • Chosen time windows and variables. Other periods, sectors and small and medium-sized companies may show something else. · according to the author
  • Only open-access articles, from two databases, in the survey of article 1. · according to the author
  • Small effect per day ("reduced practical significance", in the author's words) and association, not proof of cause. · our note
