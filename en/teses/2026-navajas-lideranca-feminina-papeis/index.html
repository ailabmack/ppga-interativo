Leading, caring and the prejudice women face

Why does a woman in charge still seem out of place to many people? With 108 professionals who wrote down the words that came to mind and 260 who answered a questionnaire, the thesis follows the path from beliefs about the roles of men and women to prejudice against women leaders. Explained in 12 screens.

03 The theory

Two lists of qualities: acting and caring

Social psychology calls qualities aimed at acting and achieving agentic, historically linked to men, and those aimed at others communal, linked to women. Sort the words the way the thesis did:

Leaders are expected to act

Direction, firmness, authority, focus on results.

≠When people see these two expectations as incompatible, there is incongruity.

Women are expected to care

Nurturing, motherhood, family, sensitivity.

↓And the incongruity produces two kinds of prejudice:

Access

She is seen as less fit and has less chance of getting the job.

Success

Once there, if she acts firmly, she is judged harshly for stepping out of the expected role.

Role Congruity Theory by Eagly and Karau (2002), the basis of the thesis (sections 2.5 and 2.6). The words and their classification come from Chart 2, which links each scale item to a word the participants used.

09 The finding

The weight lies in women's role, not the leader's

Of the four expectations that reach prejudice, the two about leadership make no difference. What weighs most is the expectation that women care: people who see it more strongly also see more prejudice against women leaders. Seeing women as people who act and decide goes along with less prejudice.

11 The unexpected

The distance shrank, and the prejudice did not go away

The theory predicted: the larger the distance between leadership and women, the more prejudice. In the data, for comparisons with the caring side, it came out the other way: less distance, more prejudice. 12 So what?

Valuing care in leadership was not enough

For the author, today's leadership already includes empathy, trust and developing people, but traditional expectations about women still weigh on how women leaders are judged.

For people managers and HR

  • Watch for stereotypes in hiring, promoting and evaluating the performance of women.
  • Use leadership development to promote gender diversity in company culture.

For equity policies

  • Identify what sustains the stereotypes, the distance between roles and the prejudice that keep women from reaching management.
  • The scales validated in the thesis make it possible to measure this distance and track change.

For researchers

  • Measure the distance with other methods (response surface analysis, Euclidean distance).
  • Compare genders, generations, sectors and cultures.

What this research does not say

  • A specific group. 74% women, 84% with a postgraduate degree, 74% born in São Paulo, a convenience sample. · according to the author, with data from Tables 41 to 46
  • No comparison between women and men. Gender entered as a control, with no separate analysis by group. · according to the author
  • Perceptions, not actions. The scales ask what "most people" or "society" think; they do not measure real hiring or promotion decisions. · our observation
  • A single point in time. The links are associations in a single questionnaire, not proof of cause. · our observation
