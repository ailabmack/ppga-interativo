Wisdom that becomes innovation, innovation that becomes wisdom

How do agribusiness cooperatives in western Paraná, Brazil, turn what they know into something new? The author talked with 11 leaders and managers from 5 cooperatives and found a cycle: what people learn together feeds innovation, and innovation put into practice comes back as wisdom. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The concepts · what the thesis compares

Knowing how to decide and knowing how to create are different capabilities

Organizational wisdom is an organization's ability to learn from what it has been through, adapt and make good decisions. Innovation capacity is the ability to create new ideas, products and processes and put them into practice.

There is a lot of research on innovation and much less on wisdom in organizations. Studies that look at both together are rare, and the author found none on agribusiness cooperatives. Pick an aspect and compare.

Definitions and authors cited in the thesis literature review (sections 2.1 and 2.2). The definition of wisdom (López and Rodríguez-Cruz, 2019) was read to the interviewees at the start of each conversation.

05 The method · interviews

Eleven long conversations with the people who make innovation happen

Between December 2023 and April 2024, the author interviewed leaders and managers working in innovation, technology and management. The script had 19 open questions and planned for up to 30 minutes, but every conversation ran longer.

06 What was heard most

The most-used word was “gente” (people)

The author combined the 11 interviews into a word cloud: the more often a word came up, the bigger it is. In the center, bigger than “inovação” (innovation) and “sabedoria” (wisdom), is “gente”, which the author reads as “people”.

For the author, it is a portrait of the thesis: people are the link that carries the organization's knowledge and wisdom and that drives innovation. Use the filters to see the word groups.

07 The analysis · Grounded Theory

From 368 excerpts of conversation to 5 propositions

The author used Grounded Theory, in Kathy Charmaz's version. Instead of starting from a hypothesis to test, it lets the theory emerge from what people said: read, code, compare and group, in layers, until the ideas stop bringing anything new.

Each dot on the right is a real item from the analysis. Tap a layer to see how one leads to the next.

Before the fieldwork, a literature review gathered 151 articles on organizational wisdom (Web of Science and Scopus), of which 41 were selected.

09 The finding · the thesis model

Everything goes through people learning and ends in wisdom

The author linked the 7 themes in a diagram. On the left, what comes in: strategies, challenges and incentives. In the center, knowledge generation and employee learning. From there come innovation capacity and innovation in practice, and every path arrives at the building of wisdom.

In other words: wisdom is not only the starting point for innovation. It is also what remains after innovating.

12 So what? · limits and credits

Investing in people is investing in innovation

For cooperative leaders

  • Continuous training, such as a corporate university, also open to members.
  • Spaces for exchange: idea programs, improvement groups and innovation committees.
  • Recognize and reward those who innovate; give teams autonomy.
Categories and propositions of the thesis

For the innovation ecosystem

  • Partnerships with startups, universities, research institutes and other cooperatives extend what each one can do alone.
  • Regional networks, such as Iguassu Valley and Sebrae (Brazil's small business support agency), appear in the interviews as part of the path.
CT05 and CT06 of the thesis

For the region and society

  • According to the author, the research values local knowledge and cooperative practices and can support the development of western Paraná.
  • Other organizations can use the model as a starting point.
Section 5.1 of the thesis

What this research does not say

  • A specific context. Agribusiness is very diverse (crops, sizes, conditions); the practices may work differently elsewhere. · according to the author
  • Who was heard. 11 leaders and managers from 5 cooperatives. Members and other employees were not interviewed. · thesis data
  • A model to be tested. The diagram and the propositions come from a qualitative study; the author suggests testing them in other regions and countries, with mixed methods. · according to the author
