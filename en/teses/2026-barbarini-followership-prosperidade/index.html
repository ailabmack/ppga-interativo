Between leaders and followers

What makes someone learn and feel energized at work: the boss, or the way the person who is led behaves? With 305 professionals who answered as followers, the thesis traces the path from the leader, openness to change and followership to thriving at work. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The problem

Followers help make leadership too

For a long time, research treated people who are led as the boss's audience. Since the 1960s, some authors have looked at the other side and classified followers. The thesis calls this view followership (keeping the English term, because the Portuguese equivalent sounds passive). Pick an author and tap a type:

Typologies by Zaleznik (1965), Kelley (1992), Chaleff (1995) and Kellerman (2008), section 2.2.2 and Chart 4 of the thesis. Map positions are approximate, based on each author's dimensions.

08 What it found · the measure

The follower's critical thinking split in two

Kelley's questionnaire has 10 engagement questions and 10 critical thinking questions. In the answers, critical thinking separated into two ways of acting: solving problems without waiting for the boss, and speaking up, even disagreeing with them. Go through the steps:

Height = number of questions. Exploratory factor analysis (section 5.1.2.1, Chart 14) and items removed in rounds 3 and 5. Of the 20 items, 14 stayed in the final model (our count).

09 What it found · the model

Followership is what is most strongly linked to thriving

Because the two leaders blurred together, the thesis tested one model with each. In all of them, the thickest arrow runs from followership to thriving: people who engage and think for themselves report learning more and having more energy. Switch the study and the leader, and tap an arrow:

Standardized coefficients (β) from SmartPLS 4, Tables 12 (Study A) and 20 (Study B). Arrow thickness ∝ β; dashed = not significant. R² = how much of the differences between people the model explains. Association at a single point in time, not proof of cause.

10 The finding

Part of the effect goes through the follower

Openness to change has no direct arrow to thriving: it reaches it only through followership. The leader has both paths, one direct and one through the follower. The thesis measured each path separately.

11 The replication test

The same question in another sample

In the company (Study A), all six hypotheses held. In the open survey (Study B), with people from many companies, each had a second chance to fail. Place your bet: did it hold?

R² of the models (Tables 12 and 20). For the author, authentic leadership seems a more stable predictor across contexts; adaptive leadership, more sensitive to the company and culture (section 6.1).

12 So what?

Thriving also depends on the people who are led

For the author, training only the leader is not enough: companies need to encourage followers to take the lead in their own work and a culture open to change. Thriving at work takes shape in the space "between leaders and followers".

For HR and NR-1

  • Since May 2025, Brazil's NR-1 rule requires companies to map psychosocial risks; the author suggests going beyond compliance.
  • Measuring learning and energy can help spot teams at risk of burnout.

For those who lead

  • Acting with transparency and consistency, and adjusting one's style to the situation, goes along with teams that learn more and have more energy.
  • Making room for followers to disagree and propose is part of the process.

For those who are led

  • Engaging, solving problems without waiting for the boss and speaking up were what was most strongly linked to thriving.
  • Valuing novelty and autonomy goes along with this active way of being led.

What this research does not say

  • One company and a convenience sample. Study A comes from a single company; B from whoever chose to answer, highly educated (93% with a degree or more). · according to the author, with data from Tables 2 and 13
  • One moment, the same person. Each person answered about the leader and about themselves in the same questionnaire; this does not prove cause and may inflate the links. · according to the author
  • Two leaders that do not separate. The adaptive and authentic leadership scales measure almost the same thing for respondents, perhaps a general "positive leader". · according to the author (section 6.2)
  • A measure that is still fragile. The engagement questions are long or mix ideas, and fell below the quality criterion (AVE < 0.5). · according to the author
