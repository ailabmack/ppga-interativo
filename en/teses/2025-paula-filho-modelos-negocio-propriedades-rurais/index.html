Doctoral thesis · PPGA Mackenzie · 2025

From field to cash: how rural properties create, organize and capture value

Producing well is only the start. In western Paraná, Brazil, 22 farmers growing grain, meat, vegetables, flowers and honey told how they built their businesses. The thesis looks at them through the value cycle (create, organize and capture) and finds four business models, from artisanal farms to properties integrated into large supply chains. 02 The starting idea

Value is not just the harvest: it is created, organized and captured

The thesis uses the value cycle proposed by the advisor, Dimária Silva e Meirelles (2019). A business model is the way a property goes through the three stages, and what it learns in the last one feeds the first.

03 Literature review · 57 articles

Hardly anyone has studied how farmers capture value

The author searched three research databases for studies on business models and value in rural properties. 57 articles remained: the topic is growing, but hardly anyone looks at the whole cycle, and capturing value had none.

04 How the research looked · 22 properties

Twenty-two stories, chosen to cover different kinds of property

Between February and August 2024, the author visited properties in western Paraná and asked each farmer to tell the story of the business: how it started, what changed, who they work with, how they sell. Crossing the type of property, the technology and the production system gave 64 possible combinations; the research reached 22.

06 Creating value · 22 decisions

Three paths to creating value: gain scale, diversify or stand out

Read each property's value-creation decision and say which path it took. Grain farms bet on scale and efficiency; fish and crop-livestock-forestry farms diversify to reduce risk; honey, flowers, lamb and most organic growers look for a niche.

Our grouping, based on the wording of each property's value-creation decision (section 6.1). The thesis describes the decisions one by one, without adding them up into groups.

07 Organizing value · what the 22 told

Inside the farm gate: the family in charge, a spreadsheet in hand and the cooperative alongside

Organizing (configuring) value means deciding with which people, machines, money and partners to produce and deliver. In the accounts, management is mostly family-run and simple, and cooperatives provide inputs, technical assistance and markets.

08 Capturing value · how prices are set

Who sets the price? Hardly ever the farmer

The interviewees describe a market in which the farmer is a price taker with little bargaining power. Capturing value depends on how the price is set: by international exchanges, by agreements with cooperatives and companies, or case by case.

“The challenge, the thesis sums up, is that farming does "not set prices": with no bargaining power, in perfect competition, what is left is to seek efficiency and control costs (section 5.1.5).

12 In practice, limits and credits

Seeing your own cycle helps you choose the path

Farmers

Looking at all three stages, not just the harvest, shows where value slips away. Joining supply chains standardizes production and price; selling directly takes a brand, logistics and customer relationships. Our reading of the thesis.

Cooperatives and supply chains

According to the author, the four models help discuss whether a business can join a value system and how to share, in a balanced way, the gains generated along the chain.

Government and rural extension

The author suggests governments use this picture to communicate public policies and rural extension programs, since family and commercial farming have different models.

Researchers

Repeat the study with numerical data, measure the links between the stages and add performance indicators such as costs, productivity and market share.

What this research does not say

  • Only western Paraná, in the Atlantic Forest biome: other biomes may have other business models. · according to the author
  • Not every combination turned up: for example, large estates and minifundia with some technologies were missing. · according to the author
  • These are 22 convenience cases: they show the variety, not how common each model is in the region. · our note
  • There are no profit or income measures: the study describes how each property creates and captures value, not how much it earns. · our note
  • The four models are a proposal drawn from the accounts; the author himself suggests testing them with numerical data. · according to the author
