IT only turns into value when it goes through innovation

In 107 companies in Brazilian technology parks, those with more IT competence say they are more able to innovate, and it is through innovation that IT is linked to value creation. Without innovation along the way, the direct link almost disappears. Explained in 11 screens.

Author: Vorster Queiroga Alves Advisor: Prof. Dr. Gilberto Perez Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

04 How the research looked

From 1,472 e-mails to 107 companies that answered

In the Web of Science database, the author found only 1 article that brought the three topics together. To fill the gap, he built a questionnaire and sent it, from July to November 2021, to companies in 27 technology hubs and parks in every region of Brazil. Tap a stage:

Sections 3.3 and 3.4 and Table 1 (Quadro). Each plate’s side is proportional to the square root of the number (area ∝ number). The minimum calculated in G*Power was 88 companies; the author doubled the target to 176 and reached 107 (61% of the target, about 9% of the valid e-mails). He also used WhatsApp and LinkedIn.

06 What it found · the dimensions of IT

The IT that counts is more about management than machines

The six dimensions go together and form a single competence, one of the thesis’s aims. Each column shows how much the dimension follows the whole, from 0 to 1. The highest are the proactive stance and business knowledge; the lowest, infrastructure. Tap a column:

Table 12 (all with p < 0.001). Height = loading, starting from zero. The differences are small: all pass the usual minimum of 0.708. The six dimensions extend Guite and Perez’s (2019) five by adding business knowledge.

07 What it found · the first bet

Those with more IT competence say they are more able to innovate

The coefficient (β) goes from −1 to 1 and says how much two ideas go together, holding the rest constant. From IT to innovation it was 0.839, very high. IT accounts for about 70% of the differences in innovation capability. Tap a type of innovation:

R² = 0.705: IT competence explains about 70% of the differences in innovation capability across companies.

Tables 6, 8 and 12 (t 25.977; p < 0.001; 5,000 resamples). Numbers in the boxes = how much each type follows innovation capability. Data from a single point in time: they show a link, not proof of cause.

08 The unexpected · the second and third bets

On its own, IT seems to create value; with innovation on the map, the direct arrow almost disappears

Looking only at IT and value, the link is strong: 0.752. When innovation comes in, the arrow from IT straight to value drops to 0.068, not significant, and what leads to value is innovation (0.814). Compare:

Model with IT and value only: Table 16 (t 14.839). Full model: Tables 6 and 8 (IT → value t 0.665, p 0.506; innovation → value t 8.435, p < 0.001). Arrow thickness = β; dashed and grey = not significant.

11 So what? · limits and credits

Investing in IT without investing in innovating leaves value along the way

For those who run technology companies

Know the six dimensions of IT competence and give them resources, together or separately, while also investing in the capability to innovate, which is how IT reaches the customer. (author)

To assess your own company

The thesis’s 58 statements (25 on IT, 20 on innovation and 13 on value) can serve as a checklist to see where the company is weakest. (our reading)

For researchers

Repeat the study in other sectors and countries, compare parks and regions, and separate companies that sell to the public sector from those that sell to the market. (author)

What this research does not say

  • Each person rated their own company. The same person answered all three scales, at a single point in time: the links do not prove that IT causes innovation or value. · our note
  • Sample smaller than the target. 107 of the 176 target companies (61%), chosen by access, not at random, and only in technology parks; contact was online only because of the pandemic. · according to the author
  • Very close ideas. IT and innovation go together so closely (0.839) that they come near the point where they stop being distinct (0.855). · our note, Table 3
  • Self-reported value. “Value creation” is what the respondent perceives (decisions, products, costs), not measured profit or sales. · our note
