Doctoral thesis · PPGA Mackenzie · 2018

In logistics, workers aged 45 or over describe themselves first of all as experienced

The thesis asked 203 workers from four companies in São Paulo for three words about “the logistics worker aged 45 or over”. “Experienced” came up 165 times, six times more than the second word. Around it sit praise and labels such as tired, unprepared and retired. Explained in 12 screens.

02 Do what the participants did

Write the first three words that come to mind

This is the free word association technique: the researcher read out an expression and each person wrote three words, without thinking too much, then numbered them from 1 (most important) to 3. Quick answers reveal what a group shares in its thinking about a subject. Try it with the thesis’s central expression:

“logistics worker aged 45 or over”

Nothing is saved. We compare your words, by simple similarity, with the 15 most cited by the 203 (Charts 12 and 14), which were collected in Portuguese. The thesis merged synonyms before counting: the 1,218 words written for the two central expressions came down to 198 different ones. In the study, the expression came after four warm-up ones (work, logistics, worker and logistics worker).

03 The problem · the portrait in studies

Studies tend to portray the older worker through decline

The author read 22 articles from 2000 to 2017 and gathered the negative stereotypes they record about people who grow older at work. In this thesis, “older” starts at 45, as in Conen, Henkens and Schippers (2010); the World Health Organization uses 60. Tap a stereotype and see whether it reappears in the workers’ own words:

04 The problem · the gap

Nobody had asked the workers themselves how they see themselves

The author ran two systematic searches in the journal portal of CAPES (Brazil’s federal agency that gathers academic journal databases). In the first, on workers over 45, most articles dealt with illness, retirement or elderly care. In the second, on the social representation of these workers (the shared image a group holds about something), the few studies looked at retirees, carers and families, not at what the workers think of themselves. 05 The context · 6 managers

Managers say logistics holds on to workers aged 45 or over because it needs experience

Before hearing the workers, the author interviewed six managers from the companies in the study, all with more than ten years in the sector, to understand the setting. High-value cargo, poor roads, clients in a hurry and lots of new technology: in this setting, track record and coping with pressure count. Choose a topic:

Chapter 4 (semi-structured interviews in the second half of 2017, content analysis). Two managers were heard in each of three companies; the fourth could not take part in this stage. Quotes translated by us, without identifying the speaker.

06 How the study looked · 203 workers

203 workers aged 45 or over, most of them men in operational jobs

In the second half of 2017, the author herself handed out the questionnaires during breaks, while people waited for loading and unloading and before training sessions, on every shift, including overnight. Anyone aged 45 or over, not retired and working in logistics at one of the four companies could take part. Four tests with 15 people each adjusted the questionnaire: the word “envelhescente” (a Portuguese term for someone starting to age) was understood by only 10% and was dropped, and the request for five words was cut to three.

07 What it found · 609 words

“Experienced” is the centre: 165 of the 609 words, usually among the first written

09 The turning point · words that go together

Praise and negative labels hang on the same word: experienced

The similarity analysis links words that appear together in the answers. A single star came out, with “experienced” in the middle. On the skills side, the strongest links are committed and responsible; on the other, tired, unprepared, undervalued and retired are also tied to the centre. For the author, it is a split self-image, not yet settled. Filter the sides:

10 The unexpected · 90 drivers

Among drivers, “retired” rises into the core, next to “experienced”

With the 90 drivers only, “experienced” stays at the centre (63 mentions), but “retired” and “tired” (17 each) join the core, and the similarity splits into two groups: one around experience and another linking tired, retired and healthy. For the author, they value their experience while sensing that the end of their career is drawing near. Compare:

11 The finding · two views

Managers and workers agree on experience and also on what is missing

We placed side by side what the managers said and the words the workers used about themselves. Experience is the common ground. The lack of schooling and technology that managers point out shows up as “unprepared”. And the idea that after 50 one heads for retirement is on both sides. Tap a row:

12 So what? · limits and credits

For the author, the sector should train people aged 45 or over instead of waiting for them to leave

For managers and HR

Include people aged 45 or over in training on new technology, fleets and processes, instead of treating them as people just waiting to retire. That is what keeps the experience the sector depends on. (author)

For public policy

With the population ageing, create policies that keep people aged 45 or over active, not just support and retirement-preparation policies. (author)

For the workers themselves

The author suggests that this image is also fed by the workers themselves, when they sideline themselves and wait for retirement instead of seeking to update their skills. Knowing the portrait is a first step to changing it. (author; the last sentence is our reading)

What this study does not say

  • It does not apply to all of Brazil. One sector, four companies, only in the state of São Paulo; the author calls for studies in other states and sectors. · according to the author
  • The core is a hypothesis. Word positions change with the ruler; that is why the thesis used two programs and the similarity analysis. · according to the author
  • Who answered. 90% men, invited by the managers of the companies that agreed to take part; and without a younger group for comparison, we cannot say what is specific to age. · our observation
  • Words, not actions. The technique shows the shared image; it does not measure whether these people leave work earlier, or why. · our observation
