Does reporting more mean doing better?

Six sustainability reports from a large Brazilian meat company, from 2018 to 2023. The reports became more complete. Social practices did not always follow. And what moves the company most is the law, not society. Explained in 10 screens.

02 The problem

A report is a thermometer, not the medicine

Of the three parts of sustainability (environmental, economic and social), the social one is the most neglected. It is about the company's impact on people: workers, communities, customers, suppliers. In the meat industry, the risks are well known:

  • worker health and safety
  • working conditions
  • child labor
  • Indigenous rights
  • land grabbing

Many companies describe what they do in a sustainability report that follows the international GRI standard. The social part of the standard (GRI 400) lists the topics a company should report. But a more precise thermometer does not bring down the fever: reporting well is not the same as doing well. The dissertation measures the two separately.

Brazil is a key player in the world meat market: in 2017–2021 it had 29% of world chicken exports, 14% of beef exports and 6% of pork exports.

03 How the research looked

Six reports read line by line

Of the six meat and derivatives companies on the Brazilian stock exchange, the author chose the one with the longest and most consistent reports and the best positions in B3's Corporate Sustainability Index (ISE). To protect the company, the dissertation calls it MeatCo.

  1. How complete? Each of the 14 topics got a score per year: 1 (not reported), 3 (partly reported) or 5 (reported everything the standard asks for).
  2. Did practice improve? Each requirement was compared year by year and classified: improvement, no change, decline or variation.
  3. Who shows up? Each group mentioned was counted as affected by the company or as affecting the company.

05 The twist

Reporting better is not doing better. Place your bet

These are the five topics in which the report became more complete. In each one, did practice improve too? Bet and flip the card.

07 What it found · stakeholders

Those the company affects are not those who move it

The reports mention many groups, the stakeholders. When they talk about who is affected by the company, mostly workers, suppliers and customers appear. When they talk about who affects the company, government dominates, followed by management itself and the bodies that set rules and standards. Society appears 34 times as affected and only once as someone who weighs on decisions.

08 The finding

The company is still on the first step: following the rules

Since government, regulators and management carry the most weight, the author concludes that the company acts reactively: it looks after social sustainability mainly to meet what is required. In a three-stage model (Miller and Serafeim, 2014), this is the compliance stage. The topics that advanced most, such as occupational health and safety, are precisely the ones most driven by laws and standards.

~51mentions of government as affecting the company
~23of regulators and standard setters

And this was the company with the longest and most consistent reports in the sector. For the author, this suggests the whole sector needs more proactive efforts, something studies of other companies should confirm.

09 Why it matters

Reading a report with fresh eyes

The main lesson applies to any company: measuring only whether the report is complete can hide setbacks. You need to look at what actually changed for people.

For companies

  • Go beyond reporting: track whether social indicators improve.
  • Be consistent year after year, without topics that vanish and return.
  • Listen to society and communities, not only to those who inspect.
  • Report the sector's sensitive topics, such as Indigenous rights.
Sections 4.2, 5 and 6 of the dissertation

For standard setters

  • More precise GRI guidance, to compare years and companies.
  • Percentages instead of raw numbers (e.g. return after parental leave).
  • Prevent changes in the standards from breaking the time series.
Sections 5 and 6 of the dissertation

For government

  • Laws and standards were what drove the advances most.
  • Policies focused on social indicators can improve company practices.
  • External auditing makes reports more trustworthy.
Section 6 of the dissertation

For investors, consumers and researchers

  • A complete report is not a seal of good social performance.
  • Be wary of topics that show up only in the good years.
  • The dissertation's method separates completeness from performance and can be applied to other companies.

10 Limits and credits

What this research does not say

  • Only what the company wrote. The analysis uses only the company's own reports, with no interviews or other sources to confirm them. · according to the author
  • One company, six years. There is no comparison with other companies in the sector; a longer period would show longer-term changes. · according to the author
  • Only the groups mentioned. Those who do not appear in the reports may be affected or influential and were left out of the count. · according to the author
  • More reports are not always more problems. The number of discrimination cases may also grow with more awareness or more employees. · according to the author
  • The ISE may reflect size and profit. Ranking well in the index does not guarantee greater social investment. · according to the author
