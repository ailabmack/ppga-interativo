The people who got the ERP running learned as a team, and the leaders did not see all of it

A large public services company in São Paulo took four years to replace its systems with an ERP, the software that links purchasing, maintenance, people and finance. Twelve people from four teams described how they learned together. Team members reported all 13 ways of learning as a group described in the literature; the leaders, only 10. Explained in 11 screens.

02 The case · sections 3.6.1 and 3.6.2

Replacing a whole company’s systems took four years and a crisis

An ERP is a single system that ties a company’s areas together: what purchasing records shows up in the budget, the accounts and maintenance. To work, it forces the company to rethink how it operates. The company studied, called Company JK to keep it confidential, is a mixed-capital company linked to the São Paulo State Government, with more than 5,000 employees.

03 The concept · sections 2.1.2 and 2.1.3, Table 2

A team learns when it does certain things together

It is hard to separate what each person learns from what the group learns. So the study looked at behaviors: collective actions that, according to the literature, make a team learn. The author gathered 13 of them from three studies (Edmondson, 1999; Savelsbergh and colleagues, 2009; Decuyper and colleagues, 2010) and used them as the starting list for reading the interviews.

04 The method · sections 3.4, 3.7.1 and 4.1, Tables 3, 5, 6 and 7

Four teams: two with many problems after go-live, two with few

The author chose the teams that handled a whole process (not a specialty) and, among them, two with many and two with few user complaints after the system went live (the go-live). The aim was variety in the stories, not to explain the difference. Click a module.

05 The result · Tables 1 to 23, section 4.3

Team members spoke of all 13 behaviors; the leaders, of 10

Each passage of speech that shows a behavior counts as one mention. Members added up to 122 mentions and leaders to 35 (our sums). The leaders did not speak of making sense of the new together, communicating errors or reflecting on results.

Eight one-on-one interviews against one 151-minute group conversation: the totals cannot be compared directly. What matters is whether the behavior showed up on each side. (our note)

06 The unexpected · section 4.2.10, Tables 16 and 17

Both sides clashed, but with different people

A constructive conflict is a disagreement born of different opinions that leads to a better solution. It was the behavior most cited by both sides. But members spoke mostly of arguments within their own team, and leaders of arguments with the consultancy. Click a type of conflict.

08 The context · sections 3.6.2 and 4.5.2

The consultancy left for nine months, and that is when the teams understood the system

In May 2014, with user training already under way, the consultancy pulled out: it felt the contract did not cover the work still to be done. At the same time, the company was going through an unprecedented crisis with its main raw material, and the government replaced its top executive. Click each moment.

11 So what?

Implementing an ERP is also a team learning project

An ERP is more than new technology: the teams had to dive into the company’s processes, make mistakes in a test environment, argue and share what they knew. For the author, paying attention to this from the start helps the next project.

For companies about to implement an ERP

  • Train teams in the system and also in learning as a group. (author)
  • Make room to test without fear: an isolated test environment (sandbox), testing workshops with all the modules and no punishment for mistakes. (sections 4.2.5 and 4.5.3)

For those leading the project

  • Plan the stages better: it was the lesson most cited by both sides. (Tables 24 and 25)
  • Leaders did not speak of three behaviors the teams lived through; it is worth asking the teams, not just the leaders, how the group is learning. (our reading)

For researchers and teachers

  • Repeat the study at other companies; study planning, how teams are chosen, leaders’ experience in negotiations and leadership support. (author)
  • Bring ERP and group learning into the classroom together. (author)

What this research does not say

  • One company, four teams. It is a qualitative study; it shows how things went there, not for every ERP. · according to the author
  • Memory and silence. Participants may have forgotten or left out important topics. · according to the author
  • It does not explain the post-go-live problems. Teams with many and few complaints were chosen to vary the stories, not to compare them. · according to the author
  • Mentions are not a measure. Eight interviews and one focus group of four leaders produce different amounts of talk; the researcher was the participants’ colleague on the project. · our note
