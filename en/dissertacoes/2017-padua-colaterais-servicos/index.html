Master’s dissertation · PPGA Mackenzie · 2017

Service firms with more buildings, machinery and vehicles owed more, especially those that rely on banks

Lenders want an asset they can take if the debt is not repaid. The dissertation compared 153 US firms in technology and transportation, from 2010 to 2016. Long-lived assets came with more debt, mostly long-term. Receivables did not: they came with less. Explained in 10 screens.

02 The topic

To lend, a creditor wants an asset it can take if the firm does not pay

This asset is the collateral. It lowers the risk for the lender, who knows less about the firm than its own managers do. With collateral, credit is easier and cheaper to get.

The dissertation looked at three kinds of collateral and one idea: matching maturities. Assets that last for years would go with long debts; assets that turn into cash within months, with short debts. Tap an asset.

Source: sections 1.1, 1.3 and 2.1.3 and Chart 2. Each asset and each debt was measured as a share of total assets. Fixed assets are net of depreciation.

03 Two sectors

Technology and transportation both sell services, but only transportation has many assets to pledge

A carrier needs trucks, planes, trains, ships. A software firm needs people and programs. For every US$ 100 of assets, transportation held US$ 52 in fixed assets; technology, less than US$ 9.

And transportation owed more: US$ 33 of debt per US$ 100 of assets, against US$ 19 in technology. Almost all of the debt was long-term in both. Switch sectors.

Source: Tables 6, 7 and 8 (2010–2016 averages; squares rounded). Inventory: average only for firms that report it (174 of 658 records in technology, 192 of 283 in transportation). The Mann-Whitney test confirmed that all three kinds of collateral differ between the sectors (p < 0.001). Over the period, technology firms’ debt rose and transportation firms’ fell slightly (Graph 1).

04 Who was in

153 Nasdaq firms, followed year by year from 2010 to 2016

The balance sheets came from S&P’s Capital IQ database. The sample covered technology service firms (programming and systems) and transportation firms (rail, road, water, air and support services) listed on the Nasdaq, a US stock exchange.

05 The hypotheses

Four bets on collateral, maturities and banks

The bets come from theories about why firms prefer one source of money over another. One of them: because the lender knows less than the firm, collateral is what reassures the lender.

The fourth bet brings in the credit rating (such as AAA or BB). Firms with a rating can raise money in the market by selling bonds, which may not need collateral. Firms without one rely on banks, which ask for collateral. Place your bets: the scoreboard is on screen 07.

Source: section 1.3. The dissertation assumes that having a credit rating means being able to issue bonds (debentures) in the market.

06 The result

Each kind of collateral moved with debt in its own way

Fixed assets came with more debt. Receivables came with less, in total and long-term. Inventory came with less short-term debt. And no short-term collateral came with more short-term debt.

With the other factors in the model (profit, growth, size and sector), fixed assets no longer weighed on long-term debt. Switch models and tap a block.

07 The scoreboard

The bets held for fixed assets, and only for them

Where the collateral idea worked, it was through fixed assets: the asset that weighed most on the balance sheet, especially in transportation. Inventory was almost nothing in these firms, and receivables moved in the opposite direction to what was expected.

Source: Tables 10, 11 and 12. Verdict from the sign and significance of each kind of collateral in the tables. The models explain 28% to 46% of the variation in total and long-term debt, but only 4% to 10% of short-term debt (R²).

08 The discovery

For firms that rely on banks, fixed assets came with much more debt

In firms without a credit rating, each 10 extra points of fixed assets came with 2.6 extra points of total debt. In those with a rating, this effect was 1.8 points smaller. Firms that can sell bonds in the market need collateral less.

For reference: technology, 9% on average; transportation, 52%.

09 The unexpected

Receivables came with less debt, and the sign flipped for rated firms

Sales on credit can also serve as collateral: the bank can take what customers owe. Even so, in service firms, more receivables came with less total and long-term debt.

10 So what?

Knowing which assets can serve as collateral helps choose where to look for money

For managers of service firms

  • Identifying the type and maturity of collateral helps raise money at a lower cost. (dissertation)
  • Without a credit rating, long-lived assets weighed much more on debt. (screen 08)

For firms with few physical assets

  • In technology, with 9% fixed assets, the collateral door is narrow; having a rating and market access reduces this dependence. (screens 03 and 08, our reading)

For Brazilian firms

  • The US case may guide Brazilian service firms on the use of collateral in credit and capital markets. (dissertation)

For researchers

  • Broaden the sample, compare with other sectors, control for rating level and use other debt measures. (dissertation)
  • Study the role of intangible assets such as software and brands. (dissertation)

What this research does not say

  • Two sectors, US only. 153 firms listed on the Nasdaq; it does not carry over directly to private firms or to Brazil. · per the dissertation
  • Rating as a shortcut. Having a rating was treated as being able to sell bonds; the dissertation did not observe how much each firm actually borrowed from banks or in bonds. · our note
  • Fewer balance sheets in the models. The regressions used 295 to 403 of the 941 balance sheets, because not every firm had all the data; inventory appeared in only 366. · our note
  • Short-term debt barely explained. The models explain 4% to 10% of the variation in short-term debt. · our note
  • Association is not causation. The models show what moved together; they do not prove that buying trucks would bring more credit. · our note
