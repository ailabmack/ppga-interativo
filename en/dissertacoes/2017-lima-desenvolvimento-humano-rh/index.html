For HR, developing people still means running training

The author interviewed HR managers at seven large companies that say human development and sustainability are part of their strategy. In their words, developing people has become courses and skills training, and sustainability has become not harming the environment. HR does not yet act as an agent of change.

Author: Vanessa Oliveira Lima Advisor: Prof. Dr. Janette Brunstein Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

02 The question

“Developing a person” can mean very different things

For the UN (UNDP), human development is the “expansion of people's capabilities and functionings”, and work is only one of its dimensions. HR scholars such as Kuchinke speak of human flourishing: development that also serves the group, the community and society.

Sustainability, since the Brundtland Report (1987), means meeting today's needs without compromising future generations, balancing the environmental, the social and the economic.

05 Human development

Almost all of them talk about flourishing, but inside the company the talk turns into training

The author found two views. The broad one speaks of citizenship, well-being and personal growth. The pragmatic one speaks of skills and knowledge for the job. When the question becomes “and for the company?” or “and for HR?”, the answers shrink to training, corporate university and quality-of-life programs.

That is not on our agenda, you know. What we have on the agenda today is the training agenda […]G1, private public-transport company, on human development in HR

06 Sustainable development

To them, sustainability is almost only the environment

The sustainability “triple bottom line” has three legs: environmental, social and economic. In the answers the environmental leg dominates, nearly always in the sense of causing less harm rather than creating benefits. When the economic shows up, it comes as “growing” and “results”. The social nearly disappears.

Here there was no difference between public and private companies. Only one manager, at a retailer, spoke of the supply chain, of many stakeholders and of inclusion, and credited that view to a personal history in NGOs.

Height = how many of the 6 quotes touch each leg (our count).
07 Talk vs document

The company report often says more than the HR manager can tell

The author set each manager's words next to what the company itself publishes. To her, the gap shows that HR either does not know what the company does or has a narrow idea of sustainability: either way, HR is disconnected from the company's sustainability projects. Pick a case.

09 The training actions

The actions cited are courses and campaigns: the traditional way of training people

Vasconcelos and Silva (2013) separate traditional training (formal courses, in a classroom, now and then, for the individual) from training for sustainability (learning by doing, in teams, continuously, solving real problems of the business and of society). 10 The finding

No one went past the first step, and the next one is on hold

For the author, what prevails is strategic HR: developing people for the company's targets. She found no behaviour linked to critical or holistic HR. Talking about them, in these companies, is still “one more narrative, a utopia”.

No architecture. HD and SD are occasional topics, outside strategy and training.
No agent of change. The pragmatism of the actions does not give HR that role.
No new skills. Nothing points to change under way in the medium or long term.
No bridge. Sustainability and HR sometimes sit in areas that do not talk to each other.

11 So what?

To leave the first step, HR needs to rethink its own role

HR professionalsThe author suggests rethinking the profile of the function, moving away from a bureaucratic role towards critical and holistic HR.
CompaniesThe meaning of HD and SD should be debated across the company, with HR connected to the other areas, including sustainability.
Training designersExperiences that bring managers into contact with the company's real social, environmental and ethical problems, through many teaching routes.
UniversitiesUndergraduate and graduate courses that train HR professionals can also take up these issues.

What the study does not say

  • Seven companies. A small share of the organisations that could be studied; no generalisation. (author)
  • HR only. Other areas, the sustainability team and staff at other levels were not heard. (author)
  • One person per company. What each manager says may not be what the whole HR team thinks; they themselves say the view is shared “largely”. (our note)
  • 2016, amid a crisis. Cuts and political uncertainty weighed on the answers about the future. (our note)

Next steps suggested by the author: study companies seen as sustainability benchmarks, other Brazilian states, deeper case studies and quantitative surveys of HR in Brazil.
