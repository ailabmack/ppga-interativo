The larger the stake of funds and other institutional investors among the owners, the smaller the premium a firm pays on its debentures

A debenture is a loan a firm takes directly from investors. It pays the government bond rate plus a risk premium. The dissertation analysed 139 issues by 79 Brazilian firms from 2010 to 2017: a larger institutional stake and a better credit rating came with a smaller premium; a family among the owners did not change it. 02 The topic

Lenders charge a firm more than they would charge the government

Lending to the government, by buying a Treasury bond, is the lowest-risk investment in the country. To lend to a firm, investors ask for something extra: the premium (the yield spread). The riskier the firm looks, the larger the premium.

All the debentures studied pay inflation (the IPCA index) plus a rate. So the author compared each one with the government bond that also pays inflation plus a rate (the NTN-B), with the closest maturity, on the issue date.

premium = (1 + debenture rate) ÷ (1 + government rate) − 1

03 The hypotheses

Three things investors look at before setting the premium

Two kinds of owner and the credit rating. Agency theory (the conflict among those who run the firm, own it and lend to it) and information asymmetry (lenders know less than insiders) offer arguments for each.

04 The credit rating

The rated issues all sat high on the scale

Agencies such as S&P, Moody’s and Fitch grade a firm’s ability to repay its debt (the rating). The author turned the grades into a 1 (worst) to 23 (best) scale and, when agencies disagreed, kept the lower one.

05 Who was included

Of 283 issues, 139 remained with known owners

The author started from 170 non-financial firms with inflation-linked debentures, 2010 to 2017. Many were privately held, with no public data on their owners; 79 firms remained. Ownership was collected by hand, firm by firm.

06 The context

After the 2011 incentive law, the amount issued nearly quadrupled

With the recession from 2014 on and the BNDES development bank lending less to large firms, debentures gained ground. Law 12,431 of 2011 exempted interest on infrastructure debentures (the “incentivised” ones) from income tax.

In all, the 139 issues added up to about R$ 26 billion, an average of R$ 187 million each (our calculation). Tap a year.

08 The result

Institutions and credit rating, yes; family, no

With the other factors accounted for (issue size, collateral, tax incentive, listing, the firm's debt and profitability), two of the three hypotheses held. The institutional effect only showed up now: on its own, it barely appeared (screen 07).

Source: Table 6 (pooled least-squares regressions, one per hypothesis, each on its own group of issues). p = the chance of such a result appearing by luck; below 0.05 counts as confirmed. Readings in percentage points: our calculations from the coefficients (the premium is a fraction in Table 3).

09 How much it weighs

One rating step is worth almost three times ten points of institutional stake

Each extra rating step came with 0.29 percentage point less premium. Each 10 extra points of institutional stake, with 0.1 point less. On an average issue of R$ 187 million, 0.1 point is about R$ 190 thousand in interest a year.

10 The unexpected

Family ownership seemed to make debt pricier, but the effect vanished once everything else was counted

Looking at just the two, more family ownership went with a larger premium (correlation +0.42). In the model with the other factors, the effect was close to zero and not significant (p 0.653).

11 The other factors

With collateral, the debenture paid a larger premium, not a smaller one

Collateral (a company asset set aside for creditors) should reassure lenders. In all three models it came with about 1 percentage point more premium. For the author, those who demand collateral already distrust the firm: collateral signals higher risk.

12 So what?

Who owns the firm enters the price of money raised

For issuing firms

  • The credit rating was the strongest factor: one step down cost about 0.29 point of premium. (screen 09)
  • Having funds and other institutions as shareholders, who monitor management, came with cheaper credit. (author)

For investors

  • The premium paid follows risk signals: rating, profitability and even the demand for collateral. (screens 08 and 11)
  • A larger premium is not guaranteed profit: it pays for greater risk. (our reading)

For the market

  • With the BNDES lending less, debentures became a route for large firms; the study shows what weighs on their cost. (author)
  • It can guide firms in other emerging countries choosing between banks and capital markets. (author)

For researchers

  • Add governance rules and creditor-protection clauses (covenants), especially for privately held firms. (author)
  • Study Brazilian firms’ bonds sold abroad, which have more recorded trades. (author)

What this study does not say

  • Few issues per group. The family model has 24 issues for 7 control factors; the author links the missing effect to this small number. · according to the author
  • Only the issue rate. The debenture market has few recorded trades after issue, so the premium could not be tracked day to day. · according to the author
  • Comparison within each group. The institutional effect compares issues that already had institutions among the owners (stakes from 20% to 100%); it does not compare them with firms without institutions. · our observation
  • Association is not causation. The models show what moved together; they do not prove that bringing in a fund as a shareholder would lower the premium. · our observation
