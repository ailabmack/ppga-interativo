What makes a team learn from its own mistakes?

At an air-conditioning manufacturer, 161 employees described how the company deals with mistakes, what support they get and whether their team learns from what goes wrong. A culture of handling mistakes came with more support, more approachable bosses and more feedback from the work itself, but only support for the work (information, help from specialists, training) was linked to learning from mistakes. 02 The problem

A manufacturer in the middle of change, where mistakes are part of everyday work

08 The finding

People with more support learn more from mistakes, and support goes hand in hand with the culture

Association, not cause: the questionnaire was given once. The route from culture to learning through support (0.701 × 0.395 ≈ 0.28) is our calculation; the dissertation did not test this indirect path. Together, support, boss and feedback explain 21.5% of the differences in learning.

10 How the study checked its measures

Of the 36 statements, 31 stayed in the final model, and the three negatively worded ones dropped out

Over three rounds in SmartPLS, the author removed statements that had little to do with the idea they were meant to measure. Three of them were “reversed” statements (agreeing means less support or less feedback), and all three dropped out. Tap a statement:

11 So what?

For the company, the author recommends talking more about mistakes and giving teams what they need to work

Internal communication

Share mistakes, from the top down

Share incidents (mistakes and failures) among leaders and then pass them on to the teams.

Meetings

Boss and team in the same room

Hold meetings between leaders and employees, so that a mistake becomes a conversation rather than a secret.

Developing leaders

Feedback on the task

Improve the leadership programme with a focus on giving feedback on the work, while watching HR indicators such as staff turnover and sick leave.

Support

Information, resources and recognition

Give teams access to information, resources and rewards, the only idea linked to learning in the model.

12 Limits and credits

What this study does not say

  • A snapshot, not a film. The questionnaire was given once, so we cannot say what causes what. The author suggests studies over time, such as action research. · according to the author
  • Just one company. The results apply to this manufacturer, at a time of change, not to industry in general. · according to the author
  • Whoever wished answered. The sample was not drawn at random (42% of those invited); managers answered more (57%) than the others. · section 3.3 and Table 2
  • “No effect” is not “unimportant”. The boss and feedback go along with learning, but lost their effect next to a stronger predictor, as the author herself notes. · Discussion
  • Indirect path not tested. The model has no direct link from culture to learning, and the route through support was not tested. · our observation
  • A slightly fragile learning scale. It kept 4 of its 6 statements and an AVE of 0.484, close to the 0.5 minimum. · Table 9
