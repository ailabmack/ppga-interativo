Sustainability staff in companies learn by doing and live by convincing others

The dissertation heard 12 sustainability professionals from Brazilian private companies. They learned mostly on the job, describe themselves as diplomats, conductors or ambassadors, and still have to prove the area is worth it.

Author: Ana Lúcia Kropf Stockler de Oliveira Advisor: Prof. Dr. Janette Brunstein Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

05 Learning from experience

All 12 say they learned by living through things, reflecting and trying again

For the author, the stories follow David Kolb's learning cycle: live an experience, reflect on it, draw a general idea and test it. Follow the cycle with a case told by Superintendent 1, from a time working at a bank:

There's the moment when you develop it, then you systematize it and turn it into knowledge so you can multiply it.Analyst 1

Sections 2.3.1, 4.2.11 and 5. The dissertation says the accounts resemble Kolb's cycle (1984); splitting this case into the four stages is our reading.

11 Takeaways

The sustainability professional's role is still being invented

For the author, most companies are still developing sustainability, and this professional's role is still searching for meaning and a place. The analogies reveal people trying to gain legitimacy, have a voice and justify their own existence.

For companies

  • In the accounts, the area loses strength when top management changes or when a crisis squeezes the budget.
  • According to the author, a culture geared to sustainability, present in values, vision and mission, is still missing.

For people in the field

  • Practice and exchange with other companies were these 12 people's school.
  • The author warns: reducing the job to making the area “palatable” to the corporate world impoverishes it.

For schools and universities

  • The vast majority say there is no specific training for the field.
  • Courses built on real cases and exchange between companies may fit better with how these professionals learn. (our reading)

What this research does not say

  • What other executives think. Only sustainability professionals were heard, not managers from other areas or top management. · according to the author
  • How the story goes on. Each person was heard once; it was not possible to follow how the area evolved over time. · according to the author
  • How far it applies to all companies. These are 12 people from medium and large companies, found through the author's network, with no claim to represent the country. · our note
  • Differences by size and region. The author suggests comparing small and large companies, states, and domestic and multinational firms. · according to the author
