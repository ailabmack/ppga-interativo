Can reading a classic change an executive?

At a business school in São Paulo, executives read novels such as Tolstoy’s The Death of Ivan Ilyich together and share what they felt. The dissertation listened to alumni, the method’s creators and program directors: the reading moves people, makes them rethink the place of work in their lives and, for some, changed how they work and lead. But the change stayed with the individual. Explained in 13 screens.

Author: Nélio Rodrigues Póvoa Neto Advisor: Prof.ª Dr.ª Janette Brunstein Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

02 The problem · management education

At business school, the “why” gets left out

The dissertation starts from authors who see business education as rigid and geared toward solving problems and hitting targets. Reflection becomes an add-on. Jack Mezirow, who created transformative learning theory, distinguishes three kinds of learning by the question each one asks. Tap each one.

Aesthetics comes from a Greek word linked to awakening. Drawing on Dante Gallian, the author recalls that in mythology the Muses, patrons of the arts, exist to wake up those who live “anaesthetized”.

Mezirow’s (1985) kinds of learning, section 2.1. The examples in the panel are ours.

03 The concept · transformative learning

Transformation starts with a jolt, and a book can deliver it

Mezirow studied women returning to work or college after many years and described ten phases of a deep change. The first is the disorienting dilemma: something that does not fit one’s usual way of thinking. It may be a divorce or a change at work but, in his words, also “a discussion, a book, a poem or a painting”.

04 The case · Ethics and Literature program

The method uses three meetings per book, each with its own task

The method was born in 2003 at the Escola Paulista de Medicina (Unifesp) as the Humanities Laboratory, to humanize medical training. ISE Business School in São Paulo has used it since 2011 under the name Ethics and Literature. Participants are asked to arrive having read the whole book.

05 Method · qualitative case study

Of 263 alumni invited, 35 wrote back and 11 were interviewed

06 Result · books read

All 11 read The Death of Ivan Ilyich

Tolstoy’s 19th-century book tells the life of a career-driven man who, on his deathbed, looks back on his choices. Seven interviewees read only that one; four also read other classics, up to four books. Tap a book on the shelf.

08 Result · the whole participant

Emotion comes first and opens the way to the question “why?”

Executives were moved in class, away from the companies where they work. Reflections on their own lives grew out of that emotion, above all on the weight of work. But not every reflection goes as far as questioning one’s own assumptions. Climb the steps.

09 Result · the will

Some changed their routine, how they lead and even their job

For the author, transformation only happens when a person starts acting differently. Some described concrete changes; others stopped at reflection. Tap a stack: each cube is an interviewee quoted with that kind of change.

10 The finding

The change stayed with the person and did not reach the company

No account showed an action or an effect on how the company works: decisions, processes, culture. What carried over from the person to work were relationships with colleagues and team members. Tap each ring.

11 The unexpected · resistance

Not everyone gets on board

Many students do not expect literature at a business school. Some did not read the book; in the program where the activity is elective, attendance fell over the meetings. In the written accounts, some people gave up.

“At first I was curious. As the classes went on, I gradually lost interest.”Respondent 23, written account

“No, it only showed me that I am on the right path.”Respondent 18, asked whether they now see their beliefs differently

“I think not everyone is ready to spend so much time discussing themselves.”EMBA program director (D1)

The question the dissertation leaves open: should the activity be mandatory? What do you think?

12 Synthesis · model after the analysis

The final model puts the aesthetic experience and the coordinator at the center

The author adapted a model of transformative business education (Longmore, Grant and Golnaraghi, 2018) and redrew it after listening to participants. Identification with the character, listening among peers and the coordinator at the heart of it all were added. Tap the parts of the drawing.

13 So what? · limits · credits

What changes in practice and what the study does not say

Business schools

Use classic literature as a way to humanize education and choose works in which students recognize themselves, such as Ivan Ilyich for those who live for their careers. · according to the author

Session leaders

Connect the themes students bring up with business themes, without taking away the method’s spontaneity or giving out an “answer key”. · according to the author

Companies and HR teams

The method may serve companies’ training spaces, but it is not yet known whether it works with colleagues and bosses from the same company in the room. · according to the author

People who lead others

The most reported changes were small and practical: having a time to go home, listening before demanding, letting the team look after their health. · our reading of the statements

Limits

  • Those who disliked it were barely heard. Of the 263 invited, 35 wrote back; those not interested in the activity declined interviews. · according to the author
  • The activity’s effect blends with the program’s. Some accounts do not separate what came from the book from what came from the whole program. · according to the author
  • Open classes, from different companies. With bosses and subordinates together, openness may differ; the author suggests studying single-company programs. · according to the author
  • Was Ivan Ilyich the most cited because it was the most read? Everyone read it, so one cannot tell whether it had the greatest effect. · according to the author
  • These are accounts, not measurements. The changes come from what participants said; no one observed them at work. · our note
