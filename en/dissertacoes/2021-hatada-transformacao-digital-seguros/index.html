Digitizing insurance is not the same as transforming it

Four large, traditional Brazilian insurers described how they went through their digital transformation. All of them say they planned it and put the customer at the center, but only one changed its business model: it began selling directly, with no broker in between, and delivers the policy in seconds. 02 The question

Putting the old process on a screen does not change the business model

The dissertation separates two things. Digitizing is moving what was already done into digital form. Transforming is rethinking the business around what the customer wants. Follow a policy's journey in the three ways.

"Before" and "Transform": how the Insurer B interviewee described the old and the new process (sections 5.3 and 6.5). "Only digitize" is our illustration of the dissertation's conclusion: in practice, the other three digitized the model they already had, and the policy still takes a few days (Table 15).

03 The setting

Brazilian insurance is large, concentrated and sells through brokers

The sector weighs more and more in the economy, and a few long-established companies, the so-called incumbents, dominate it. By law and by tradition, the product reaches the customer through a broker.

05 How the research looked · 4 interviews

The research heard from the people leading digital transformation at four large insurers

In the middle of the pandemic, the author interviewed online the person leading digital transformation at four traditional general insurers. Three are among the five largest in the country. The conversations were recorded, transcribed and read as narratives.

07 Second leg · value configuration

Three insurers let customers use their policy on their own, and only one lets them buy it on their own

Configuring value means organizing partners, channels and product management. On partnerships all four say the same thing: they look for partners that speed up their digital ecosystem. The difference shows up in the channels.

08 Third leg · value appropriation

Two insurers opened their doors to people who had never had insurance

Capturing value means turning what was created into loyal customers, brand and revenue. The item that most separates the four is democratization: reaching the people traditional insurance left out.

Table 17 of the dissertation.

How Insurer B did it · illustration

Windstorm cover across all of Brazil

Many insurers do not sell windstorm cover where windstorms are frequent. B did the opposite: the same coverage for everyone, with a slightly higher price where there is almost no windstorm paying for the losses where there is. The customer base became "one big pool", in the dissertation's words.

09 The finding

Only the insurer that took the broker out of the way closed the value cycle

Insurer B created an insurtech, a 100% digital insurer born inside it, to sell directly to customers. Creating, configuring and capturing began to reinforce one another, according to the author.

30 days → secondsuntil the policy reaches the customer
3×production, in less than a year and a half

Figures reported by the Insurer B interviewee (sections 5.3 and 6.5); the dissertation includes no documents confirming them.

Why scale makes it cheaper · illustration

In the old model, more customers required more staff to serve them, change policies and dispatch service providers. When customers quote, make changes and upload photos themselves, the structure "does not need to keep pace almost linearly" with growth, the dissertation says. Grow the customer base.

11 In practice, limits and credits

Transforming starts by looking at the customer, not at the structure already in place

Traditional insurers

The author recommends building the business model around customer needs rather than the current structure; creating new platforms with teams of varied backgrounds and digital experience; and offering digital channels for both buying and using the product.

Customers

In B's case, customers got their policy in seconds, buying and service at any hour, the price of each coverage in plain view, and access for people traditional insurers turned down. These gains are the insurer's own account.

Brokers

Three of the four kept the broker at the center of sales, and C deliberately avoided conflict with brokers. The research did not hear from brokers; what direct sales mean for them remains open. Our reading.

Researchers

Create a scale to measure digital transformation across the three value legs, hear from the other large insurers, or look at one specific product, such as car or life insurance.

What this research does not say

  • It cannot be generalized: these are four insurers with different niches, origins and models (three sell through banks). · according to the author
  • One person per company: each insurer is seen through the account of the person leading the transformation, with different roles from one to the next. · according to the author
  • Accounts, not measurement: B's results (production, ranking, claims) come from the interview; no financial data were checked. · our observation
  • No view by product: the insurers spoke about the whole company, not each type of insurance. · according to the author
  • "Does, plans, does not" is our reading of the comparison tables, not a score given by the author. · our observation
