When the partner factory says no

A repellent company with no factory of its own needed to produce much more. The partner factory replied by asking to become a co-owner. The dissertation follows this decision to the end and asks: does agreement among the managers help the decision work in practice? Explained in 12 screens.

04 Method · chapter 7

One company, one decision, four managers

It is a case study: instead of many companies, just one, examined closely. The author looked for medium and large cosmetics companies in a contractual alliance that had made a strategic decision about it in the previous two years.

07 Power and coalitions · section 8.3.3

ANVISA’s rule gave the factory leverage without anyone asking

Three outside forces pulled on the decision: the partner factory, owner of the resource everything depended on; ANVISA, with its registration rules; and the shareholders, who pressed for results and for not tying up money. Click each one.

The author calls this a “virtual” external coalition: ANVISA never sided with the factory, but its rule (switching factories requires new registrations, which take time) increased the factory’s power. Mintzberg did not list this kind of influence. Arrow thickness is our illustration.

    12 So what?

    Before signing the alliance, think about the next hard decision

    For the author, consensus may be the link between deciding and doing: understanding what the decision is for, trusting colleagues’ competence and committing to it helped the team adjust the plan and get started quickly.

    For those who set up alliances

    • The contract cannot foresee everything: beyond the clauses, the two companies need to agree on how to coordinate future decisions. (author)
    • Assess the balance of dependence: whoever depends more on the other is exposed to opportunistic behaviour. (author)

    For those who lead decisions

    • However organised the process, groups inside and outside will try to influence the decision; whoever leads it must see them. (author)
    • Seeking information, hearing different views and doing technical analysis eases both the choice and its execution. (author)

    For those who study decisions

    • Implementation should not be studied apart from the decision: what happens before weighs on what comes after. (author)
    • Industry rules can give power to one side without anyone arranging it (the “virtual” coalition). (author)

    What this research does not say

    • A single case. The propositions are explanations to be tested, not general rules; a case study generalises to theory, not to other companies. · according to the author
    • One side of the alliance. Only the Company’s managers spoke; the old factory was not heard, and its portrait as opportunistic is the view of those who broke with it. · author and our observation
    • Success told by those who decided. The results come from the managers’ own words and confidential documents; there are no sales or cost figures after the switch. · our observation
    • No comparison. There is no decision without consensus that went wrong to compare with; the link between consensus and success is a reading of the case. · our observation
