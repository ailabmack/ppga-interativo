The barriers to employment for autistic people lie in the workplace

More and more adults are finding out they are on the autism spectrum (ASD). The dissertation heard 19 professionals with autism, 6 from HR and 4 from organizations that prepare people for work. The barriers show up in diagnosis, in myths and at every stage of people management. Explained in 11 screens.

Author: Débora Cunha Romanov Advisor: Prof. Dr. Darcy Mitiko Mori Hanashiro Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

11 Takeaways

Removing barriers is up to the organization

For the author, the challenges in the job market do not come from the way autistic people are, but from barriers in organizations and relationships, and each person needs different supports. Hearing the three sides together shows where to act.

For companies and HR

  • Show the setting and routine before hiring: at the HR team that did this, turnover fell.
  • Describe the job’s required skills clearly and adapt group exercises and interviews.
  • Create performance metrics and a career plan with objective criteria.

For support organizations

  • They can bridge autistic professionals and HR, serving both sides.
  • Train technical skills, not mold the person to neurotypical norms.

For autistic professionals and families

  • The accounts show that adult diagnosis and doubts about disclosing are common.
  • Studying, networking and seeking career guidance were paths mentioned. (our reading: not a recipe)

What this research does not say

  • How far it applies to everyone. As a qualitative study, its findings are limited to those interviewed. · according to the author
  • Other support levels. All 19 are at level 1; other barriers may emerge at levels 2 and 3. · according to the author
  • Many companies and organizations. Few agreed to take part: 3 companies and 4 support organizations. · according to the author
  • Whether turnover is higher or lower. One organization reports dismissals before evaluation; one HR professional sees autistic staff staying longer. · according to the author
  • All of Brazil. Of the 19, 13 live in the Southeast and 14 identify as white; who answered the call online may matter. · our observation
