Investors use the broker to trade and look for information elsewhere

Twenty-four stock-market investors described what they value in the companies that serve them. To decide what to buy, 18 say the broker matters little or not at all: information comes from research firms, their own study and people they know. And what brokers promise is not always what customers perceive. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The context

With interest rates at rock bottom, many people moved to stocks without knowing quite what to expect

Fixed income stopped paying as it used to, and the number of individual investors in stocks more than tripled in a year and a half. See the two figures the dissertation uses to open the topic.

Value proposition

What the company promises to deliver: zero fees, a modern app, a dedicated advisor, tradition.

Perceived value

What customers feel they get, from their own experience. When the two do not match, the promise did not get through or the customer sees something else.

Figures quoted in the dissertation's introduction: B3 (2020) for individual investors in equities; Brazil's Central Bank (2021) for the Selic benchmark rate, which ranged from 10% to 14% between 2010 and 2017. The research question: how do investors perceive the value proposed by the companies in this market?

04 How it was done

Twenty-four investors told their stories in online interviews

10 The discovery

What companies promise is not always what investors perceive

The author set side by side what each company promises on its own website and what interviewees said about it. In 9 of the 17 cases the promise does not get through in full. Tap a block or a name.

matchespartlydoes not match

Quadro 28 (proposition read on websites; perception from interviewees). The three-group classification is our reading: "matches" when perception repeats the promise; "partly" when it repeats part and adds a complaint or limit; "does not match" when none of the promise shows up in perception, or perception says the opposite.

12 In practice, limits and credits

Simple information from a trusted source can give investors the confidence they lack

Brokers

Interviewees ask for consolidated, plain-language information, help with taxes after trading, analysis at no extra cost and products from foreign exchanges. Poor service is what most often makes clients switch brokers.

Exchange and regulators

For investors, the value of B3 and the CVM lies in regulation. Several ask for faster action against misleading information and easy-profit advertising.

People who invest or want to

Interviewees gained confidence by studying: learning about companies and the economy was the change they cited most. Take the path as a clue, not a recommendation. Our reading.

Researchers

Interview the brokers themselves, B3, research firms and regulators; link perceptions to the amount invested and time in the market; and interview people who gave up investing.

What this research does not say

  • The companies were not heard: the value proposition of brokers, B3, the CVM and research firms was read on their websites, not gathered in interviews. · according to the author
  • Not a national sample: 24 investors from Southeast Brazil, with no data on income or amount invested. · according to the author
  • Counts describe statements: the number of people per answer shows what came up in the interviews, not how common it is among all investors. · our note
  • Unnamed companies: brokers and research firms appear as letters; you cannot tell which is better, and this page is not investment advice. · our note
