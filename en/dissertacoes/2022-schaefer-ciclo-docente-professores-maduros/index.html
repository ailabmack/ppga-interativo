After 20 years in business, the classroom: what is it like to become a lecturer after 45?

The dissertation listened to the life stories of nine lecturers at private higher education institutions in São Paulo who came to teaching from the job market. All of them went through four common phases; after that, paths split into five possible phases and four types of lecturer. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The problem

University lecturers are getting older, and many arrive from the job market

Classic studies of teachers’ careers follow people who enter the classroom right after university. But at private institutions it is common for someone to arrive with decades of business experience, hired precisely for that practical experience. The dissertation’s question: how does this mature lecturer’s view of teaching guide their path?

05 What it found · the transition

The crossing from company to classroom is short: three years at most

In the corporate phase, these people entered the job market at around 17 and left, on average, at 45, after about 27 years and four companies. Four asked to leave; five were let go by the company. 08 The unexpected

Doubts about carrying on come earlier for those who come from the market

In the “questioning” phase, lecturers take stock and ask themselves whether it is worth staying in the profession. 11 So what?

Knowing this lecturer helps institutions prepare for them

The institution is one of the elements that shape teaching, and the mature lecturer tends to stay: only 2 of the 9 expressed reservations about staying in teaching longer, because of the future of education and the difficulty of living on teaching alone.

For institutions

  • Get to know these lecturers’ profile to anticipate difficulties and prepare responses to longevity.
  • Offer training and management practices aligned with this reality (the author’s suggestion).
  • Pay attention to the start: insecurity and lack of teaching preparation mark the first years.

For those already teaching

  • The types serve for self-reflection: knowing which phase you are in helps you prepare for the next.
  • Building competence is the critical point: from there the cycle can move to enthusiasm or to questioning.

For those thinking of switching our reading

  • In the stories, those who started a master’s or taught while still at the company had a planned transition.
  • Practical experience counts, but it is not enough: interviewees went looking for theory and teaching skills.
12 Limits and credits

Nine stories do not make a rule, but they open up a little-studied topic

What the research does not say

  • Nine people, one city. Lecturers in São Paulo, from finance, accounting, HR and logistics; the results do not automatically apply to other places and courses. (author)
  • Almost all paid by the hour. The two full-time lecturers do administrative work; the view of full-time researchers is missing. (author)
  • Types, not a diagnosis. The four types came from two or three lecturers each; other studies need to confirm them. (author)
  • Our own counts. Who went through each specific phase was counted by us from the dissertation’s summaries.

Next steps suggested by the author: more lecturers, more full-time ones, institutions outside São Paulo and courses outside management.
