Two neural networks tried to guess whether stocks rise or fall the next day

The study trained the LSTM and GRU networks on the Ibovespa index and 11 stocks of B3, Brazil’s stock exchange, with 1 to 20 years of history. They got the direction right on about half of the days, close to a coin. Explained in 11 screens.

02 The question

The study does not try to hit the price, only whether it goes up or down

For an investor, knowing tomorrow’s direction may matter more than getting close to the exact value. So the dissertation measures directional accuracy (MDA): the share of days on which the network correctly forecast whether the price would rise or fall compared with the day before.

03 The data · Tables 1 to 3

Twelve B3 series, with up to 20 years of daily closing prices

The Ibovespa index and 11 stocks that traded on almost every trading day from 2000 to 2019, from seven sectors. Each series was cut into five periods ending on 30 Dec 2019 and split into days to train, test and validate the network.

Trading days per period, Table 2 (the same for every series). Closing prices from Bloomberg and Economática. The last 60 trading days of each period were kept out of training for the final forecast.

04 The method · sections 3.3 to 3.6

LSTM and GRU are networks with memory; GRU has one gate fewer

Both read the series day after day and keep what seems useful. The difference lies in the “gates” that decide what to forget, what to note down and what to show. Choose a network and tap the gates.

06 Result · Table 2

On the Ibovespa, both networks scored between 46% and 52%

The best result was GRU with 10 years of history: 51.5%. The worst, GRU with 1 year: 45.8%. For the author, direction stayed “around 50%” because the index swings a lot with politics and the economy. Tap the columns.

08 The comparison

The rule “LSTM for short, GRU for long” holds for the Ibovespa, not for the 12 series together

This is the rule the dissertation’s abstract highlights. It comes from the Ibovespa. Counting all 12 series, LSTM wins more often in three of the five periods (including 10 years), GRU in one, and the two networks’ averages stay within 1 point of each other, except with 1 year.

09 our reading

Coins tossed the same number of times spread out almost the same way

Judging by the percentages, accuracy was measured on the test days: 48 in the 1-year period, 988 in the 20-year one. With few days, even a coin goes above 60% or below 40%. The grey band shows where 95% of heads-or-tails results fall. Toss the coins.

LSTMGRUcoin (our draw)

Our calculation: the published percentages match fractions of 48, 97, 245, 493 and 988 days (the test days of Table 2, minus the first, which has no previous day). The band is the 95% binomial range for each number of days. The dissertation does not make this comparison.

10 The finding

Forecasting the market’s direction is hard, and the dissertation itself concludes so

“Forecasting this movement is not easy”, the author writes. Before repeating a sentence about neural networks on the stock market, check what the study supports. Tap each sentence.

11 In practice · limits · credits

A good starting point for testing neural networks, not a buy signal

Investors

Nothing here says when to buy or sell. The dissertation does not simulate trades, costs or taxes, and getting the direction right is not the same as making money.

Model builders

A 48-day test says little. Compare accuracy with a coin’s band and with simple rules before celebrating a 60% (our reading).

Researchers

The author suggests adding inflation and exchange rates, looking at the pandemic period and comparing the Ibovespa with more stable indices.

Teachers and students

LSTM and GRU side by side, with the same data and the same yardstick, are a clear example of how to compare two tools fairly.

What this study does not say

  • A forecast is not a profit. There is no simulation of buying and selling, brokerage costs or taxes. · our note
  • No baseline. The dissertation does not compare the networks with chance or with simple rules (such as “repeat yesterday’s direction”); the coin band on screen 09 is our calculation. · our note
  • Few test days in the short periods. With 1 year of history, accuracy was measured over 48 days; a single training run per series and period. · our note
  • Only up to 2019. The pandemic period was left out, and other variables, such as inflation and exchange rates, could change the result. · according to the author
