Builders and developers in Latin America owed more than those in the US, and the classic theories explained little of their debt

The author followed 108 listed companies in seven countries, from 2007 to 2017. In the US, debt tracked size, growth and risk, as the trade-off theory predicts. In Latin America, what mattered was the price of interest and the years after the crisis. Explained in 11 screens.

03 The context

A big boom in Latin American stock markets ended in the 2008 crisis

From 2002 to 2008, with China growing and commodities up, the region’s stock markets rose far more than the US market. Many Brazilian builders and developers went public in that period. Then came the 2008 global crisis, which started in US banks.

The author wanted to know whether the theories, built by looking at US companies, hold for this sector on both sides, in a period with a crisis in the middle.

05 What was measured

The debt yardstick compares what a company owes with what the stock market says it is worth

Leverage = debt ÷ (debt + market value of the shares). So if the shares fall, leverage rises, even without a single new loan. Play with the made-up company on the right.

07 The finding

In the US, debt followed the trade-off theory; in Latin America, it followed the price of interest

Build a typical company in each region and change one feature. Only the clear effects in that region’s table enter the calculation; the others stay grey.

Our simulation with the coefficients, all else equal

We start from the region’s averages (Tables 9 and 13) and add coefficient × change (Tables 11 and 15). The effects are small: +10 points of profit on assets take about 3 points off debt. The models explain from 13% (US) to 26% (Latin America) of the variation in debt; the rest depends on other things.

10 So what?

Theories built for US companies were not enough to explain the debt of Latin American ones

For the author, in Latin America neither the trade-off theory nor the pecking order theory explained the choice of debt, except for profit. There, the cost of credit is what mattered, as basic economic theory predicts: low interest pulls debt up, high interest holds it back.

For managers in the sector

  • In the region, debt moved with the price of interest, which varies a lot: in Brazil, the policy rate went from 14.25% in August 2016 to 6.5% in March 2018. (author)
  • Developing property needs cash until the units sell; planning for that gap is part of the business. (our reading)

For company analysts

  • Higher profit came with lower debt in both regions: those who profit use their own money first. (screen 06)
  • Comparing developers with heavy construction firms by debt, without separating the sector, distorts the picture. (our reading)

For public policy

  • In 2009, the Brazilian government invested in infrastructure, cut taxes on materials and opened housing credit; for the author, the crisis weighed less on emerging countries. (author)

For researchers

  • Test the theories outside where they were born, by sector and in crisis periods. (author)
  • Add economic variables and variables on managers’ behavior. (author)
Association is not causation. The dissertation shows what moved together in these 108 companies. It does not show that raising interest rates would make a given company cut its debt. This page is not investment advice.
11 Limits and credits

What this research does not say

  • Few companies. The author says the sample is small, especially the Latin American one (40 companies, 27 of them Brazilian). · according to the author
  • Listed companies only. Private ones may finance themselves differently; including them would enrich the study, but the data are hard to get. · according to the author
  • Most of the debt remains unexplained. The models explain 13% to 26% of the variation; economic and behavioral variables were left out. · our note and the author’s
  • A 5% threshold. Growth in Latin America just missed it (p 0.056) and therefore appears as no clear effect. · our note
  • The 2010s crisis. The author attributes the higher debt after 2010 to crises in some of the region’s countries, but says this was not studied. · according to the author
