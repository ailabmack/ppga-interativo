When the pandemic shook the market, what did managers bring to the crisis?

In 2020 and 2021, a customs broker and a security and cleaning company had to change how they worked with no manual. The dissertation talked to six managers at the two companies to see how what they know, who they know and how they think helped them adapt. Explained in 12 screens.

Author: Wilson Carlos Abreu Junior Advisor: Prof. Dr. Walter Bataglia Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

03 The problem · the ideas

A company adapts through the hands of those who run it

06 What it found · Beta, moderate pace

In security, clients cut costs and swapped posts for cameras

Beta outsources concierge, cleaning and security staff and monitors condominiums and companies remotely. In the crisis, clients swapped security guards for doormen or remote concierge, cut posts and asked for more cleaning. And many staff had to take leave because of Covid. See what happens to one post:

“[…] when the crisis started people stopped to think, well, let me see, where can I cut here? […] and then they saw they could drop the security guard contract […] and replace it with an access controller or an electronic concierge […]”C02E-SD · head of Beta (section 4.2.1); translated by us
Illustration of the dissertation’s example

Example from section 4.2.1: a fixed 24-hour post, on a 12-on/36-off roster plus a relief worker, takes at least four people; when the building switches to the electronic solution, they leave the post. The dissertation does not count how many posts Beta lost or gained.

10 The unexpected

The capital that weighed most was the one you cannot see on a résumé

Make a guess first: of the three capitals, which one does the author say stood out most in the interviews, to the point of having “strength over the others”?

“[…] The pandemic showed me something personally that I try to keep in my head… not everything is under my control! […]”C01E-DT · Alpha’s IT and quality head (Table 9); translated by us
“[…] we always have to keep our feet on the ground, and keeping your feet on the ground means not acting out of excitement […] because your decision will affect how many families down there depend on what you decide […]”C02E-SD · head of Beta (Table 9); translated by us
“[…] I didn’t get Covid, I didn’t get Covid. I believe very much in God […]”C02E-CO · Beta’s operations coordination (section 4.2.3); translated by us

Section 4.3.2 and Table 9 (quotes from 5 of the 6 managers, our count). At Beta, faith appeared as part of cognitive capital; the dissertation records it as the managers’ belief, not as a cause of the outcome.

11 So what?

To decide in a crisis, the author proposes a tripod

From the two companies, the author draws a “support tripod” for adaptation decisions. Remove a leg and watch the seat:

For managers

  • Listen to peers and teams and decide without delay (P01 and P02).
  • Revisit your own skills and reach out to your network, inside and outside the company (P03 and P04).

For companies

  • Create spaces where strategic and tactical managers decide together, like Alpha’s small council.
  • Include these capabilities in the training and development agenda.

For teams and society

  • Keeping the team was one of the three legs: deliveries and jobs.
  • In both companies, the crisis sped up the digitisation of processes (the author’s reading).
Our illustration of the author’s tripod

Tripod from the final remarks (section 5). The seat tipping over is our metaphor: the dissertation proposes the tripod and suggests testing it in other crises, but does not measure what happens without one of the legs.

12 Limits and credits

These are ideas drawn from two cases, ready to be tested

What this research does not say

  • Only two cases. One company of each market pace; comparing companies of the same pace with each other was missing. · according to the author
  • No measure of strength. There was no statistical test of how much each capital influences adaptation. · according to the author
  • One crisis, one country. Everything comes from the 2020 and 2021 pandemic, in Brazil; the author suggests repeating it in another crisis and in other countries. · according to the author
  • Propositions, not conclusions. The author calls the five lessons “initial, not definitive ideas”. · section 4.3
  • The view of those who decided. Six managers are heard; the dissertation does not compare financial results or hear from clients or other staff. · our observation
