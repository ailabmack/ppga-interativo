Signals of what is coming enter a company through the board and also through operations

The dissertation heard six managers from three companies: in each, someone from strategy and someone who leads operations. Of the 679 passages from the conversations, 356 came from operations. For the author, these anticipatory signals only become management for sustainability when someone notices them, chooses among them and turns them into routine.

02 The starting idea

Whoever waits for the signal to grow strong finds out about the change after it has already happened

In the oil crisis of the 1970s, large companies were caught by surprise, even though forecasts were public and “on the desks” of some of the surprised managers. That is when Ansoff (1975) began to speak of weak signals: hints of a change that appear before the fact. Strong signals only become clear afterwards, and are usually the only ones that make it into planning.

The dissertation calls both anticipatory signals and asks: does watching them help managers act entrepreneurially toward sustainability?

03 Who transforms the signal

Noticing a signal is only the first of five moves of entrepreneurial capability

A signal does not act on its own: someone has to notice it and do something with it. The dissertation uses the idea of managers' entrepreneurial capability, with five dimensions. The first four come from Abdelgawad and colleagues (2013); the fifth, purpose, was added by Cavalheiro Neto (2020).

Tap a move. The quotes are examples the dissertation itself links to each dimension (section 4.2.3, category C1).

04 How the research looked

Six conversations: in each company, the person who sets strategy and the person who runs operations

In the first half of 2022, the author held interviews of 45 minutes to an hour, with 18 open questions, in three companies in the state of São Paulo chosen because she had access to them. She then read the transcripts, marked each relevant passage, grouped the passages by similarity into ideas, and the ideas into categories.

06 Strategy × operations

Operations talk more about partners, consultants and conversations; the board talks more about the market, customers and product

Overall, both sides said about the same amount: 356 passages from operations and 323 from strategy. But each side brought up different topics. For the author, there are signs that the operations view is as important as the strategic view for noticing signals, although the topic is usually studied only with top management.

07 Objective 1 · information sources

Besides magazines and competitors, managers cite podcasts, unions and their own routine as sources of signals

The author compared the sources mentioned in the interviews with the lists of three classic authors (Ansoff, Freitas and Janissek-Muniz, Sharp). Most come from outside, but the managers brought up sources that sit inside the company or on both sides: routines, resources, the product itself, the environment and the community. For her, the literature should include them and look at the product from inside and outside.

10 An observation by the author

Three companies with no business ties already show how one sector depends on another

Animal nutrition buys commodities to make what cattle eat; the farm sells the steer to the meatpacker at the market price per arroba, the unit cattle are priced by; construction generates waste that can be used on the farm. For the author, even a small sample shows that sustainability concerns the whole community, alliances and networks, not a single company.

11 The dissertation's answer

Watching signals helps, the author concludes, if four pieces are in place

To the starting question, the author answers yes: monitoring anticipatory signals contributes to the entrepreneurial capability of management for sustainability, as long as there are people able to detect and interpret them. She points to four key success factors. Tap each one.

12 So what?

Listening to those who run operations, too, widens the company's radar

Owners and directorsThe author suggests training the team to spot opportunities, aligning the manager's purpose with the company's, and mapping what each stakeholder expects, as one of the companies already does for every project.
Operations leadersConversations with suppliers, consultants and other producers show up more in what operations said. Our reading: it is worth taking these signals to the board in an organised way, as the monitoring method the author cites suggests.
RegulatorsThe author proposes an idea that came from the farm: the law covers protective equipment for people, but not for working animals, such as a horse's shoes. It would be a “PEA”, protective equipment for animals.

What the research does not say

  • Six voices, three companies. All in São Paulo state and chosen because the author had access; they do not represent other sizes, sectors or regions. (author)
  • Passages measure how much was said. More passages on a topic do not prove it weighs more in decisions, nor that one side notices more signals. (our note)
  • A map to be tested. The links between categories and the four factors are a proposal, not a measured result. (author)
  • No measured outcomes. The dissertation does not compare companies that watch signals with those that do not, nor does it measure environmental or financial gains. (our note)

Next steps suggested by the author: interview managers from companies of other sizes, sectors and regions, and use focus groups or the Delphi technique (rounds of consultation with experts).
