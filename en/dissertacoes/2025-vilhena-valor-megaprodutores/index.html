What a bank is worth to those who farm on a large scale

Farmers earning R$ 10 million or more a year are served by a major Brazilian bank as high-net-worth clients. The study heard 8 of these "megaproducers". The finding: in a relationship of decades, built on trust, a bad experience costs the bank some business, but does not erase the value. In 11 screens.

02 The context · who the megaproducer is

To the law, a farm earning R$ 2 million and one earning R$ 200 million are the same “large producer”

Agribusiness accounted for 23.8% of Brazil’s GDP in 2023, according to Cepea (a research center at the University of São Paulo). Brazil’s rural credit rules sort producers by annual gross farm revenue, and anything above R$ 1.6 million is “large”. In 2011, one of the country’s main banks created an extra category, the megaproducer, for those earning from R$ 10 million a year, and began serving them through its private banking unit, the one for high-net-worth clients. 03 The idea · value in experience

Value is not in the product: it is decided in use

In the view the author follows, clients do not buy a loan for its price, but for what it does for their lives. So the value of a financial service depends on two things: what clients expect and what they live through when using it. A good experience creates value; a bad one can destroy it. 04 The method · 8 interviews

Eight megaproducers each told their own story with the bank

The bank studied, called Bank X in the dissertation, opened its megaproducer portfolio to the research. To cover the crops that weigh most in these clients’ revenue, the author heard at least two producers from each. The video calls followed a two-part script: what they expect from a bank and what they have already been through with it. The answers were then grouped by theme. Change the view:

06 Part 1 · relationship

Five decide by the relationship; the other three, by rate and speed

On average, the eight have been Bank X clients for 26 years; only one for less than 20, having taken over the family business 6 years ago. 08 Part 2 · the change in service

Leaving the regular branch was, for seven of the eight, a step up

Before becoming “megaproducers”, they were served in the bank’s retail branches, like any account holder. Everyone who lived through both phases says the relationship improved with the move to private banking. Compare:

09 Part 2 · shared experience

The experience does not stay on the farm: it passes from producer to producer

Six of the eight have recommended the bank to someone: to suppliers and buyers in their chain, to friends, to family. One got a thank-you call from a friend who moved to private banking. Several also value gatherings with other producers, to swap ideas and technical know-how. Tap the circles:

10 The finding

A bad experience costs some business, but does not destroy the value of a long relationship

The literature said bad experiences destroy the value created. That is not what the author found. Those annoyed by red tape take part of their business to another bank, but remain clients, and even those who do most of their business with a competitor consult Bank X before closing a deal, because of the history built up. Try it:

11 Takeaways

For megaproducers, the relationship outweighs everything else

Rates, speed and good apps weigh on the decision, but to the participants none seemed bigger than a solid relationship. The author suggests managers serving this group start from there.

For banks serving agribusiness

  • Start with dialogue: understand each producer’s business before offering products.
  • Be close: a manager who knows the farm, an agronomist who visits the crops, a team that understands agribusiness.
  • Respond on the farming calendar’s schedule.

What can cost business

  • Red tape in contracting and repeated requests for documents.
  • Hard-to-use apps and online tools, when competitors are simpler.
  • Clients left out of invitations to events with other producers.

For research

  • A bad experience does not erase value when there is trust: an addition to theories of value in experience.
  • Hear clients with fewer years at the bank and measure, in a quantitative study, how much the relationship weighs on decisions.

The three cards are our reading of the findings; the author’s practical implication is the paragraph above.

What this research does not say

  • How many megaproducers think this way. These are 8 clients of one bank; the study seeks to understand experiences, not measure proportions. Our counts only summarize the tables. · per the author and our note
  • Whether it holds for newer clients. The average relationship was 26 years, and only one participant had fewer than 20; the author suggests hearing those who arrived recently. · per the author
  • A researcher from the inside. The author works with this segment at the bank studied, which may bias the reading; interviewees were talking to someone from the bank. · per the author and our note
  • Memory. The experiences were told after the fact, which may reduce the accuracy of the accounts. · per the author
