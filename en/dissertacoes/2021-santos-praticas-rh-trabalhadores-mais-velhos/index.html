Companies open jobs to people over 50 with the customer in mind, but do little for those who grow older inside

The dissertation interviewed 11 HR managers at large companies. Programmes to hire people aged 50 and over are created to serve the older customer and, according to them, they work. But only one company is preparing a plan to develop people already that age, and the workforce shrinks after 55.

02 The problem

The workforce is ageing, but almost no one has studied what HR does about it

People over 50 struggle to find a job or keep the one they have. Newspapers and magazines report programmes at large companies to hire “50+” professionals, but the author found almost no studies about them. The question: what HR practices do companies adopt to hire and keep older workers, according to HR managers?

03 How the research looked

Of 23 companies with 50+ programmes, 11 explained how they work

The author listed large companies known for practices that favour older people (through the press and through referrals from interviewees) and invited their HR managers through her professional network and LinkedIn. The conversations, in 2021, were online, recorded with permission and analysed by theme. Companies and people remained anonymous.

06 The reason

What drives the programmes is the older consumer, not a shortage of workers

The managers know the ageing figures, and some fear there will be too few people to hire in the future. But, for the author, what really leads companies to create 50+ jobs is the customer of the same age: serving buyers better and creating products for this public. The positive traits from the previous screen play a supporting role: patience and empathy are gold in customer service.

37% of one company’s customers were 50 or over, and its “extremely young” team could not talk to them.

08 What managers report

Those who hired over-50s tell the same story: plenty of applicants and few departures

Managers point to low turnover, little absenteeism and more satisfied customers. And demand is startling: thousands of applicants for a few jobs. Choose a programme:

12 So what?

Hiring over-50s is not enough if the company does not look after those who grow older in it

CompaniesThe programmes worked where they were tried. For the author, without training, career paths and accommodation for those already inside (flexible hours, schedules and benefits), hiring does not raise the share of older people.
GovernmentThe author calls for policies that encourage hiring programmes for older people, such as the bill giving tax incentives to companies that hire people over 60 (under discussion in 2021).
Professionals over 50Managers value those who keep learning and see those who stop as “settled”. Our reading: the burden falls on the individual, while the company offers almost no training.

What the research does not say

  • São Paulo only. Large companies in the Southeast, headquartered or with a branch in the capital; other regions and smaller cities may look different. (author)
  • No breakdown of who was hired. It was not possible to separate the gender, race or education of the people hired through the programmes. (author)
  • The voice is HR’s. Programme results are managers’ accounts; older workers were not heard, and companies were chosen because they already had programmes. (our note)
  • Five companies, one snapshot. Only 5 of the 11 sent figures by age, and they show one moment, not departures over time. (our note)

Next steps suggested by the author: study conflicts between young staff and interns or trainees over 50, and hear older workers themselves on lifelong learning.
