How much of their income do people who know investing save?

To sell investments in Brazil, you have to pass an exam run by ANBIMA, the financial market association. The research asked 185 certified professionals how much of what they earn they put aside. The finding: those who have invested for longer save a bigger share, women save smaller shares than men, and salary made no difference. In 11 screens.

Author: André Filipe Liberali Magajewski Marchesi Advisor: Prof. Dr. Dimária Silva e Meirelles Program: Professional Master’s Program in Economics and Markets · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

02 The context · figures cited by the dissertation

Just over 3 in 10 Brazilians managed to save money in 2022

The author opens the dissertation with a country that is in debt and saves little. That is why he wanted to look at people who, in principle, know what to do with money. First: out of every 100 Brazilians, how many do you think saved in 2022?

Figures the dissertation cites in its introduction: ANBIMA, Brazilian Investor X-Ray (2022), and Febraban, the banks’ federation (people with a default record in July 2022: 67.6 million, 41.8% of adults). These are surveys from other sources, with other methods; they cannot be compared directly with this dissertation’s questionnaire.

04 The method · online questionnaire

185 certified professionals answered 14 questions

195 answers came in. The first question was a filter: only people with an ANBIMA certification stayed in, leaving 185. The author then crossed the share of income each person saves with each characteristic, using a statistical test called chi-square: it says whether savings are spread differently across groups (for example, men and women) more than chance would explain. 08 The finding · time investing

Those who have invested for longer save a bigger share of their income

It was the strongest cross-tab in the study (p = 0.006). Among those who have invested for less than a year, 7 of 20 save nothing; among those investing for over 10 years, almost half save 20% or more. Choose what to highlight and tap a column:

11 Takeaways

Among certified professionals, savings went along with time investing, not with salary

The author concludes that financial knowledge and information are tools for well-being, and that people need to be encouraged to look after their own finances.

For those who teach finance

  • Knowing is not enough: even among certified professionals, 1 in 9 save nothing.
  • The habit of investing seems to matter: it is worth following those who are just starting.

For financial firms

  • Certified women saved smaller shares; the study does not say why.
  • Certifying staff does not guarantee that they save themselves.

For research

  • Compare certified and non-certified people and analyze the products they invest in.
  • Repeat by region and with other methods, as the author suggests.

The three cards are our reading of the results; the author’s conclusion is the paragraph above, and the suggestion to repeat by region is his.

What this research does not say

  • Whether certification makes people save. All 185 are certified; without an uncertified group, there is no comparison. · our observation
  • How much certified Brazilians save. The sample is not random, and almost half are from São Paulo. · according to the author and our observation
  • Cause. Chi-square shows association, not direction or cause; and many combinations had few people (for age and time, over half the cells expected fewer than 5 people), which makes the test less reliable. · our observation
  • What happens at high incomes. 72% picked the same range, above R$ 6,000. · our observation
  • What they invest in. The questionnaire asked about products, but they do not appear in the results: the hypotheses on risk and savings accounts went unanswered. · our observation
