Master's dissertation · PPGA Mackenzie · 2018

What makes a client company dignified in the eyes of those who sell to it?

Employees, customers and neighbours have already been asked what makes a company dignified. This dissertation put the question to those on the other side of the counter: the suppliers. Their answers became a 22-statement scale, tested with 108 suppliers. The statements formed a single measure, and almost every rating landed at the top. Explained in 12 screens.

In business-to-business sales, the buyer usually sets the rules

06 The scale · Table 10 · 108 suppliers

Now it's your turn: rate a client you know

In the validation, each supplier picked one client and said, statement by statement, how well it reflected that company, from 1 (“does not reflect it at all”) to 6 (“reflects it very much”). Six points, no middle, so no one could sit on the fence. Here, to keep it short, you give one rating per group of statements:

The suppliers answered between 30 August and 17 September 2018, invited one by one by 14 IESM buyers (about 15 suppliers each; 160 contacted). Their average is the average of the 22 statements (Table 18). One rating per group is our simplification.

12 So what?

A ready-made scale for listening to suppliers, which still needs further tests

For the author, the 22 statements say what a dignified client is for those who sell to it, and the scale can become an indicator tracked by managers.

For buyers

  • Keeping contracts, paying fairly and on time, respecting and telling the truth is what suppliers call dignity. · Table 10
  • The scale can be used right away, as a management indicator. · author

For those who want to hear suppliers

  • The invitation should not come from the rated client's purchasing team, because that contact may bias the answers. · author
  • Hearing from former clients also helps comparison. · author

For researchers

  • Ask what an undignified client is and build the opposite scale. · author
  • Compare large clients the supplier depends on with clients without that dependence. · author

What this research does not say

  • It does not speak for all suppliers. They all came from a single institution's register, by convenience. · according to the author
  • The scale was tested once. 108 answers, close to the minimum of 110 (5 per statement) the author cites, and without confirmation in a new sample. · author and our observation
  • The ratings may be inflated. The invitation came from the client's own buyers. · according to the author
  • Very similar statements. Some found the questionnaire repetitive; with an alpha of 0.99, several statements may be saying the same thing. · author and our observation
  • Only the good side. The question was what is dignified, not what is undignified. · according to the author
