Five professionals rebuilt their workplace conflict with figurines

Five women in conflict with a coworker or a manager took part in a systemic constellation: they placed figurines on a table, one for each part of the conflict. Between 15 and 30 days later they were interviewed again, and all of them said the conflict felt lighter. The dissertation records these accounts, with no comparison group. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The problem · conflict and dignity

A conflict at work can hurt the way people see themselves

An interpersonal conflict is a clash between people who depend on each other, with anger, fear or frustration in the mix. The dissertation draws on a model with four kinds of dignity (Tadd, Vanlaere and Gastmans, 2010). Tap each one:

Sections 1 and 2.1. For the author, conflict and hurt identity dignity form a cycle: the conflict hurts self-esteem and self-confidence, and people who feel this way tend to feed the conflict. Searching for studies with "dignity at work" and "interpersonal conflict" together in the title, she found none.

03 The proposal · another path besides talking

The author proposes handling conflict with a method that came from family therapy

The most cited path for conflict is mediation: a guided conversation toward an agreement. For the author, it works at the conscious level and may leave emotions out. The alternative studied is the systemic constellation. Flip the coin or pick an aspect:

06 The method · the constellation with figurines

Each figurine stood for a part of the conflict, but the participant did not know which

The participant placed the figurines on the table as she felt, without knowing what each one stood for, and then "gave them a voice". Only at the end were the roles revealed. Try it: drag the figurines, then reveal.

    12 Takeaways

    The topic calls for larger studies, with comparison and longer follow-up

    For the author, conflicts that arise at work may have deeper roots that conversation alone cannot reach. The research opens this question with five accounts; answering it calls for larger studies with comparison.

    For HR and leaders

    • Conflict with a coworker or manager can shake the confidence and performance of those involved; it is worth looking beyond the formal agreement.
    • The author suggests the constellation as a tool; the dissertation did not compare it with mediation. (our caution)

    For people in a conflict

    • Participants said that stepping back emotionally and seeing the other person differently helped.
    • Two felt unwell in the days after; the study offered follow-up with a psychologist. (accounts from five people, not a treatment recommendation)

    For researchers

    • The author suggests studying the method within a single organization, between leaders and teams, and with more diversity.
    • She also suggests following participants for longer to see whether conflicts return.

    What this research does not say

    • A single organization. Participants worked at different companies; the study did not handle conflict within one organization. · according to the author
    • Whether the effect lasts. The second interview was 15 to 30 days later; longer follow-up is left as a suggestion. · according to the author
    • Whether it was the method. There was no comparison group and no before-and-after measure: without them, the session's effect cannot be told apart from time, the attention received or expectation. · our note
    • How far it applies to others. Five women from the researcher's contacts; she also led the sessions and interviews. The 2021 review cited finds it too early for conclusions. · our note
