How much credit unions get out of what they spend

Using 20 years of balance sheets from 446 credit unions, the study drew the line of the maximum possible and measured how far each one is from it. On average, credit unions raise about two thirds of the deposits they could with the same resources. Explained in 12 screens.

Author: Eloara Nathane Valentim Cavalcante Advisor: Prof. Dr. Eli Hadad Junior Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

Credit unions group together in tiers and in systems

06 The method · stochastic frontier

The frontier separates waste from luck

First you draw the line of the maximum deposits achievable at each level of spending. A credit union’s distance from it is its inefficiency. But part of the distance may be a bad or lucky year, and the stochastic frontier estimates that part. Tap a credit union and switch the method.

12 In practice · limits · credits

Measuring the distance to the frontier helps planning

Credit union managers

The author argues for tracking efficiency as a sustainability factor and planning gains of scale and technical efficiency. A score of 0.67 points to room to raise more with the same resources.

Systems and central unions

For the author, central unions, which pool their members’ money and operate on a larger scale, raise inputs without raising deposits at the same pace. Their role is different, and their score calls for its own reading (our reading).

Members

In a credit union, surpluses can go back to members as higher interest on deposits or cheaper loans and fees, the dissertation notes. Gaining efficiency opens up that room.

Researchers

Comparing groups calls for a common frontier and a test of the differences; adding loans granted as an output would complete the picture (our suggestions).

What this study does not say

  • One ruler per group. Each group was measured against its own frontier, and the differences between averages were not tested (screen 10). · our observation
  • Only deposits as output. Loans granted, the main service of credit unions, were not included as an output; a credit union that lends a lot and raises little appears far from the frontier. · our observation
  • Only those that lasted 20 years. Credit unions that merged, closed or failed to report in any year were left out. · our observation
  • Few central unions. The central group has 5 credit unions, all from Sicoob, and their model attributed almost all the distance to inefficiency and none to chance (Table 6). · our observation
  • Size was not measured. The third question (size) was answered through permanent assets, with no size variable in the model. · our observation
