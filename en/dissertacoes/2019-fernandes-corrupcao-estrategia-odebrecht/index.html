Odebrecht ran corruption as a business strategy

Admired for the quality of its work, one of Brazil's largest private groups ran, alongside the official company, a second structure nobody could see: a department with its own budget, targets and software for paying bribes. Using documents from the Car Wash (Lava Jato) investigation, the dissertation shows that this was planned in phases and rested on six pillars. Explained in 12 screens.

Author: Luiz Campos Fernandes Advisor: Prof. Dr. Dimária Silva e Meirelles Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

02 The question

One company, two management models

On the outside, Odebrecht taught every employee its own philosophy, TEO (Odebrecht Business Technology): trust in people, customer satisfaction, return to shareholders. On the inside, a hidden business unit ran in parallel, managed separately but tied to the projects, and it needed the company's good name to deliver what it sold.

The author asks: what strategy did an admired company follow to grow through corruption? He found no strategy research on strategies deliberately built on illegal practices.

Table 12 of the dissertation (official and parallel management model). A detail the author notes: the word “ethics” does not appear in TEO's four declared pillars.

Declared principle: act by TEO's pillars (fundamental principles, essential concepts, general criteria and corporate responsibility).
Hidden face · not public

The parallel business unit

  • It also had a management model and an organized structure
  • Used the company's image and technical structure to win business
  • Parallel accounting (the DOE department)
  • Black-market money changers for illegal transfers and banks in tax havens
  • Systemic corruption, media control and state capture
  • High rates of return on the money invested in bribes
In practice: it broke TEO itself, such as members' integrity, creating wealth for government and society, and respect for the environment.
03 The method

No interviews: the case was built from court documents

It is a single-company case study. The author did not interview executives or officials, for privacy reasons and because what they said would be hard to record and verify. He used what the courts made public: sworn testimony recorded in plea deals, agreements, indictments and rulings, plus data from BNDES, Brazil's national development bank.

To read the material, he used the idea of strategy as practice: looking at what people actually do day to day (meetings, spreadsheets, payments), not just the stated plan.

04 Result 1 · the phases

The parallel structure was built in three phases over 13 years

None of this was born ready-made. The author split the path into incubation, modeling and execution: first getting close to the new federal government, then internal organization, and finally scale, in Brazil and abroad.

05 Result 2 · the model

The model stood on six pillars, and only one was visible

Technical excellence was the public part: those who hired in exchange for bribes could not be criticized for shoddy work. The other five pillars stayed out of sight and were used more or less heavily depending on how weak each country's institutions were.

06 Result 3 · managing bribes

Bribery had its own department, with its own budget and software

Set up in 2006, the Structured Operations Division (DOE) worked like any other part of the company: it planned, and had targets, a budget and approval levels. Except that the budget was a “shadow” one, outside the official books.

07 The finding · the return on bribes

In Brazil, each dollar of bribes brought about 5.4 dollars in benefits

For the author, corruption was treated as an investment: each manager had an expected “rate of return,” used to set the bribe for each project. The U.S. plea agreement put numbers on this, country by country.

08 Result 4 · public credit

The group borrowed R$20.5 billion from BNDES between 2002 and 2015

Cheap credit from public banks was one of the six pillars. In engineering-services exports from 2007 to 2015, Odebrecht took US$8.4 billion of US$11.9 billion, about 70% of what BNDES financed.

The author does not judge whether each loan was proper. What he points out is that nearly unlimited access to public credit let the group plan long term and grow.

09 Result 5 · the growth

Revenue grew about 7.7-fold in 12 years

From R$17.3 billion in 2003 to R$132.4 billion in 2015. The author acknowledges the group's technical skill but sees the pace as out of line with the market, and links it to public money and government projects.

He goes as far as suggesting that the 444% return on bribes in Brazil “may explain” the jump. It is a hypothesis: the dissertation does not separate how much of the growth came from corruption and how much from other causes.

Chart 6 of the dissertation (company public data, R$ billions of each year, not adjusted for inflation). The dissertation writes “765%”; going from 17.3 to 132.4 is 7.65 times, that is, a 665% increase (our calculation).

10 Result 6 · the cycle

Grand corruption became a cycle that fed itself

With more projects than they could handle, the big builders began managing projects and bringing in smaller firms to carry them out. According to the investigations cited, they organized themselves into a “club” to share out the work and passed 1% to 5% of the contract value on to political parties.

12 In practice, limits and credits

More mature institutions help prevent grand corruption

Companies

In 2016 Odebrecht published the letter “Sorry, Odebrecht was wrong” and made ten compliance commitments. In its agreements with Brazil, the U.S. and Switzerland, it accepted a US$3.28 billion fine, and Braskem one of US$975 million, payable over 22 years. A values code is not enough if there is a parallel structure.

Governments and oversight bodies

The author wants to feed a reflection on how mature institutions must be to prevent new cases. Simplified procurement, targeted public credit and campaign financing were the entry points he describes.

People who work at a company

The scheme depended on many employees cooperating, who saw it as part of the job. Asking “where is this money going?” and having a safe channel to say no makes a difference. Our reading.

Researchers

The author suggests applying the same lens to other sectors that had government “partner companies,” building a measure of how much grand corruption “pays off,” and following up, years later, on the compliance programs of the companies involved.

What this research does not say

  • A snapshot up to June 2019: investigations were still under way and many cases could still change. This page does not update what came later. · according to the author
  • A bird's-eye view: the dissertation looks at the group's strategy as a whole, not at each project or company. · according to the author
  • Phases and pillars are the author's reading: built from testimony and documents; the company itself was not consulted for the research. · our note
  • Correlation is not causation: public credit and growth moved together, but the dissertation does not measure how much of the growth came from corruption. · our note
