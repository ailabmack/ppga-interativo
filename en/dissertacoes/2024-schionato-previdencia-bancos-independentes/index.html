Who manages your pension fund better?

Almost all new money in Brazil's open private pension plans (PGBL and VGBL) goes to companies linked to the big banks. The research compared 4,090 pension funds, from 2010 to 2022, to see whether the managers of the five largest banks deliver more or less than independent managers. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The problem

The public pension will weigh more and more on fewer people

In Brazil's public system (INSS), today's workers pay the pensions of those who have retired. With fewer children and longer lives, the math gets tight, and the country has passed seven reforms since 1993. So each family takes on part of its own planning.

Data cited by the dissertation (Senate News Agency; INSS). In 2022, the INSS granted 1,327,990 pensions, with an average value of R$ 1,710.18.

03 The product

In a pension plan, the tax falls the longer the money stays

In a PGBL, people who file the full tax return deduct their contributions (up to 12% of gross income) and pay tax on everything when they withdraw. In a VGBL, tax applies only to the earnings. Under the regressive table, the rate falls from 35% to 10% over time.

04 The market

Five bank-linked companies take almost all the new money

The banks' branch networks make it easy to sell the plans, and the result is a market the author calls an oligopoly. The research question comes from there: do people who buy at the bank get better management, or just more expensive management?

05 The data

The research gathered 4,090 pension funds and split them into two teams

On one side, the funds of the managers of Itaú, Bradesco, Banco do Brasil, Santander and Caixa (1,788 funds, our count). On the other, those of all other managers (2,302), such as XP, BTG Pactual, Icatu, SulAmérica and JGP.

06 The two teams

The banks' funds are bigger and charge a little more

The number of independent funds grew fast after 2018 and, in 2022, overtook the banks'. But in size, the banks' funds are still much bigger. And their average management fee is a little higher.

07 How to measure

Earning more is not enough: you have to see how much risk the fund took

The dissertation measured each fund with classic yardsticks. All of them compare the fund with the Selic rate, what you would earn with no risk. Pick an invented fund and move its return to see how each yardstick reacts.

12 In practice · limits · credits

What changes for savers, managers and regulators

Savers

Look at the fee and the risk yardsticks before accepting the branch's plan. Portability lets you switch funds or companies without withdrawing and without paying tax on the switch.

Banks and managers

The dissertation notes that higher fees are justified in actively managed funds, which try to beat the market. With more managers and open platforms, competition tends to push fees down.

Regulators

The author sees open banking and open finance as a way for savers to compare products and for the concentrated market to become more competitive.

Researchers

Suggested next step: check whether the banks' funds earn less because they track their benchmarks more closely, with more passive management.

What this research does not say

  • Only the funds that survived. The data have the funds active in January 2023; those that closed along the way were left out. · according to the author
  • Maximum fee, team by name. The research used each fund's maximum fee (larger investors usually pay less) and classified a fund as a bank fund by its name. · according to the author
  • Association, not cause. The model shows that the banks' funds went along with worse results, not why. The author herself speaks of "correlation" and of "partial" results. · our observation
  • Direction more than size. Without the units of alpha, Sharpe, Sortino and Treynor, the page shows only which way they went. And the full models use about half of the data and explain little of what changes within each year (13.8% for return). · our observation
