Master’s dissertation · PPGA Mackenzie · 2017

Do those who absorb new things well also learn more in knowing, feeling and doing?

Absorbing means recognising, digesting and using knowledge that comes from outside. The dissertation heard 128 employees of a chain of building-supply stores in western Paraná, Brazil, and compared how each says they absorb the new with three ways of learning. Explained in 10 screens.

Author: Aline Cristiane Rocha Lacerda Advisor: Prof. Dr. Silvio Popadiuk Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

02 The problem · Web of Science search, 1990–2017

Two topics with plenty of studies and not one article joining them

Knowledge absorption was born to study companies and measures almost only the rational side. Bloom’s taxonomy was born in education and separates three ways of learning. The author looked for articles linking the two. Pick the period:

03 The idea · Lowik and colleagues, Bloom and Dave

Absorbing has four steps, and the author links each to a way of learning

First a person comes across something new, then understands it, then transforms it by talking with others and, finally, puts it into practice. Tap each step:

Sections 2.3 and 2.5 (Figure 3, based on Lowik and colleagues, 2016). The links are the author’s theoretical argument for her hypotheses, not a result. In the questionnaire, absorbing was measured with the scale by Wang and colleagues (2014), in three parts: identify, assimilate and transform, apply.

08 The finding · the model in two stages

Absorbing goes together with all three ways of learning, most strongly with knowing

Each bridge links two measures; its width is the strength of the link. In stage 1, the author linked absorbing to each way of learning, one at a time. In stage 2, she combined the three into a single learning score.

Guess: which of the three goes most closely with absorbing?

Figures 8 and 9 and Table 14 (128 answers, SmartPLS 2.0). The numbers are standardised coefficients: how much one measure moves with the other (0 = not at all, 1 = fully together); all significant at 5%, according to the dissertation. R² is the share of each measure’s variation the model explains. For stage 1 we follow Figure 8 (the text gives 0.483 for feeling; the figure and Table 14, 0.433).

09 The unexpected · two different orders

Knowing goes most with absorbing, but feeling weighs most in learning

In stage 2, the model builds one learning score from the three ways. Guess: which of them weighs most in that score?

Figures 8 and 9 and Table 14. “Weight in learning” is each way’s loading on the stage-2 learning score (0 to 1), with R² below; “link with absorbing” is the stage-1 coefficient. The loadings are close to each other (0.822 to 0.899): our reading, all three weigh about the same, feeling slightly more.

10 So what?

Those who say they absorb the new well also say they learn more, and not only with their heads

For the author, studies on absorbing knowledge should also look at feeling and doing, not only at knowing.

For those who manage stores and teams

  • Use the statements as indicators to plan actions that improve how the team acquires, digests, transforms and applies knowledge (according to the author).
  • In training, attend to attitude and practice too, not only content (our reading of the author).

For those who teach at work

  • Bloom already saw that teaching tends to stay at passing on and recalling information, the lowest step (Introduction).
  • The staircases provide verbs for writing objectives for each step (Charts 1 to 3).

For research

  • Ask each person to assess a colleague (projective technique), instead of themselves.
  • Hear other groups and also apply the model at the company level.

What this study does not say

  • Everyone assessed themselves. Scores may be higher than reality. · according to the author
  • A single company. 128 people from one chain in western Paraná, most in operational roles; and the theory linking the two topics still needs deepening. · according to the author
  • Moving together is not cause. The two rounds were a week apart, but the same people spoke about themselves in both; the model shows a link, not that absorbing produces learning. · our note
  • Doing was measured with the body outside work. Dancing, guitar, brushing teeth: statements far from a store’s tasks. · our note
