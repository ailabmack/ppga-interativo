Generation Z recognizes itself only in part in the portrait others paint of it

A dissertation interviewed 18 young people aged 19 to 27. Faced with the typical description of their generation, only 4 saw themselves fully; 10 saw themselves in part. Generational identity takes shape in shared experiences, at work and also in the family. Explained in 12 screens.

Author: Patricia Alves Brunido Advisor: Prof. Dr. Darcy Mitiko Mori Hanashiro Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

12 Takeaways

Treating Gen Z as a single block hides the diversity within it

For the author, people born in the same period do not form a uniform group. Generational identity is built in many settings at once: events, culture, life stage, work, family and social background.

For companies and managers

  • See the diversity within Gen Z itself, instead of relying on generational stereotypes.
  • Reverse mentoring and multigenerational teams to handle conflicts.
  • Clear communication about tasks and good onboarding for newcomers. (suggestion cited by the author)

For young people and families

  • Family support weighed on studies, work and choices. Not everyone has that network, as the young people themselves noted.
  • The accounts show one can belong to a generation without seeing oneself in each of its labels. (our reading)

For educators and public policy

  • Understanding what shapes this generation helps support youth initiatives.
  • Scholarships appear in the paths of those who moved up; the author argues for quotas and other affirmative policies paired with student retention measures.

What this study does not say

  • What all of Gen Z is like. These are 18 young people; percentages only show trends in the group. · according to the author
  • Young people with other profiles. All have or are pursuing higher education, are single, childless, and most live in São Paulo state. · according to the author
  • Those outside the researcher’s network. Invitations went through the author’s contacts and referrals; she sought more diversity through two social projects. · according to the author
  • The older generations’ view. What is said about other generations is the young people’s perception; older colleagues and managers were not interviewed. · our note
