What makes a bank team talk about its own mistakes?

In the fraud prevention department of a large bank, the research talked to all 12 people on the team, including the person who leads it, to understand how inclusive leadership and psychological safety help turn mistakes into learning. Explained in 12 screens.

Author: Elisabete Caetano Barreto Advisor: Prof. Dr. Diógenes de Souza Bido Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

02 The problem

People who are afraid of failing hide their mistakes, and the team loses the lesson

Mistakes are everywhere at work. A company can treat them only as something to avoid, or also as valuable feedback to learn from. The author starts from Amy Edmondson: where there is fear, ideas are held back and mistakes stay covered up. Switch the environment:

04 How the research looked · one case, 12 interviews

The whole team was heard, one conversation at a time

The case is the fraud prevention department of a century-old Brazilian bank with more than 50,000 employees, which says it treats mistakes as a source of learning. Between June and September 2021, the author interviewed the 12 people on the team over Microsoft Teams, 100% of the team, with 14 open questions. She then marked excerpts of what they said in the ATLAS.ti software and grouped them. Tap a stage:

09 What it found · monitoring

A check halfway through breaks the chain of mistakes

After a mistake, the team agrees with the leader on a way to watch for it. PA explained checking whether the numbers coming in are out of the ordinary before starting their own part. Move the slider and see when the alert goes off:

“[…] I have […] an average intake in my process of 150 to 200, suddenly 500 come in; even if something might raise it by 20% or 30%, I don’t think it would ever go up 300% […], this check makes an alert go off, and that way we try to find out what happened, the root of the problem.”PA · section 4 (category C08)

12 So what? Limits and credits

At the bank studied, this emerged without a plan; the author suggests three steps to plan it

For companies that want to try

  1. Raise awareness of mistakes, psychological safety, inclusive leadership and reflection.
  2. Take stock of the situation: recall past cases, mistakes, actions taken, barriers and consequences.
  3. Act to bring the leader closer to the team, with reflective practice and shared mistakes.

What the department already did: a 15-minute daily meeting, regular one-to-one conversations, a Kanban board, a spreadsheet with each person’s history of mistakes and a management pillar called “we unlearn and relearn”.

What this research does not say

  • A single case. One small team at one bank; it was not possible to extend it to other leaders. · according to the author
  • Through the screen and in their own words. Online interviews, during the pandemic; the answers depend on how each person understands their experience. · according to the author
  • No measured results. Commitment and the effect on performance were not measured; the author suggests action research and in-person observation. · according to the author
  • Years together. The process matured over time: the leader has been at the bank for 24 years and with this team for 5. · concluding remarks
  • A familiar starting point. The author already knew this leader’s work from consulting and also chose the department for access. · our observation, from section 3.4
