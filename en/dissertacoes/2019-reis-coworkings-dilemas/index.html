How a coworking space works out its own business

The author listened to the founders of four coworking spaces in São Paulo and Guarulhos and rebuilt the story of each one. None was born with its business ready: the model was put together one dilemma at a time, by trial and error, and those who started listening to customers found their way. In 12 screens.

02 The problem · a booming sector

Coworking has spread, but 6 in 10 spaces make no profit

A coworking space is a shared office: instead of renting a room, people pay to use a desk, meeting rooms and the company of other entrepreneurs. The dissertation gathers 2018 surveys that show how fast these spaces have spread:

But a study cited by the author (Foertsch, 2011) estimates that 60% of coworking spaces are not profitable. For the author, this points to mistakes in how the business is created, organized and makes money, and almost no one had studied how these business models come about.

05 The method · narrative analysis

In each story, the author looked for the dilemma and how it was resolved

Instead of counting how often something comes up, the author read the interviews as stories: in each decision, a tension between two paths (the dilemma) and the reasoning that led to the choice (the outcome). 07 Result · private offices vs. open space

Owners dreamed of an open room; customers wanted a door they could close

Two of the four coworking spaces started without a single wall, thinking that was what members wanted. It wasn't: many people visited and did not sign up. Customers wanted the common area for coffee and networking, but preferred a room of their own for their company.

10 The unexpected

Only one of the four studied its customers before opening, and it had to change the least

The author suspected that many coworking spaces start without thinking about what members value. In the cases studied, that was true of three: at the start, they could not even say who their audience was. After redefining the business around the customer, they began to succeed. The exception was Urban Coworking.

The case that planned

Urban Coworking: changed little because it started well structured

The founder already had experience as an entrepreneur (they had owned a college), visited innovation spaces abroad before opening and started with more private offices than shared space. They used a family building, with no rent, and worked out that the business would not be profitable below 500 m². The audience was clear: entrepreneurs aged 22 to 32, 70% to 80% of them in marketing and communications.

At the time of the interviews, it was opening a unit inside Guarulhos airport, with a shower, armchairs and flight screens for people waiting for a connection.

11 So what?

A business model is a process, not a plan you make once

For the author, the right way to think about a coworking business is dynamic and emergent: answers come from facing the dilemmas, and each choice changes the next ones. The cards below are our reading of what this suggests in practice, based on the cases.

For anyone opening a coworking space

  • Define your audience before renovating: three of the four cases redid their space afterwards.
  • Ask people who visited and didn't sign up: that is how Sumforces found the demand for offices.
  • Set prices by the market, not just by the owner's bills.

For those who already run a space

  • An empty space delivers no networking: partners moved in for services in kind and brought Sumforces to life.
  • Talking in person engages more than WhatsApp messages.
  • Earn beyond the desk: virtual office, business address, meeting rooms by the hour, acceleration.

For research

  • A first reading of the create, configure and capture value lens in coworking spaces, through value co-creation with customers.
  • Next step: apply the same model and method to other small firms and to large, established ones.

Contributions and recommendations: section 7.2. The advice in the first two cards is our reading of the cases, not recommendations written by the author.

12 Takeaway

Four stories show how a coworking space learns its own business

What this research does not say

  • Whether it holds for every coworking space. These are four small cases in São Paulo and Guarulhos; the conclusions do not generalize. · according to the author
  • Whether these spaces are profitable. The dissertation reports no revenue or margins; the 60% unprofitable figure comes from a 2011 study. · our note
  • What members think. Only the owners spoke, telling their own story; the members' view comes through them. · our note
  • Whether it would be the same in a large chain. The author suggests testing the model in larger, established firms. · according to the author
