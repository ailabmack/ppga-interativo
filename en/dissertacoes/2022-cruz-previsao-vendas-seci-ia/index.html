Can a salesperson’s “feel” become data for AI?

In large sales between companies, the people who predict how much will be sold, and when, are the sales team, in a weekly conversation. Much of what they know is never written down. The dissertation listened to nine salespeople from two companies and proposes a path, the SECI model, to bring some of that know-how to artificial intelligence. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The problem

Forecasting a big sale means predicting two things: how much and when

In an online shop, the buyer chooses and pays alone. In a complex business-to-business (B2B) sale, many people on both sides take part, the deal is expensive and takes months. Even so, every week the salesperson must tell the company how much will close and on what date: that is the forecast. Compare the two kinds of sale.

03 The concept

The SECI model links know-how in people's heads to written knowledge

There is explicit knowledge, what can be written in a manual or a system, and tacit knowledge, the experience a person has but cannot quite explain. The SECI model, by Nonaka and colleagues, describes four passages between the two. Tap each letter: each one brings a quote from the salespeople that the author linked to that passage.

Section 2.2 and Figure 3 (adapted from Nonaka, Toyama and Konno, 2000). The red cube marks where, in the author's proposal, the knowledge is already written down and can become data (Figure 5). The place where the exchanges happen, a room or a video call, the model calls Ba.

04 The dissertation's idea

The proposal: carry part of the team's know-how to the AI through SECI

Today, the AI that helps forecast sales learns from what is on record (customer system, e-mails, history). What the salesperson knows from experience is left out. The author proposes that, in the Externalization and Combination passages, this know-how be recorded and enter the AI's dataset. Switch the view and tap the boxes.

7-ElevenThe inspiration: in Japan, the chain used SECI to carry what store clerks knew into each store's stock forecast (case cited by the dissertation).

05 First stage · 21 executives

Before the groups, the author talked to 21 sales executives

Between October 2021 and March 2022, he interviewed partners, directors, vice presidents and presidents of 21 different information technology companies, all with complex sales. 06 The method · focus groups

Nine salespeople from two companies talked in groups, by video

In a focus group, a moderator (here, the author himself) asks open questions and people answer and comment on each other's answers. There were two conversations of about an hour and a half, with cameras on, one in each company. Both appear as leaders in the Gartner consultancy's rankings and authorized the use of their names.

08 What it found

Sharing knowledge was the topic that came up most

The "knowledge exchange" category added up to 112 of the 350 marks (32%, our calculation) and led the first three questions. The salespeople said they adjust the forecast by talking: with colleagues, with the technical team, with partners and, above all, with the customer. Choose a question.

09 The surprise

While explaining how they get the forecast right, nobody mentioned AI

In the first three questions, the salespeople talked about how they share and combine knowledge to adjust the forecast. There were 280 theme marks. In how many of them do you think artificial intelligence came up?

all marks in the questionmarks in the AI category

Tables 5 to 8. Question 4 was the only one that mentioned AI. Our calculations: 0 of 280 in questions 1 to 3; 28 of 70 (40%) in question 4. Our reading: in the day-to-day the groups described, the forecast is made in conversation between people; AI entered the conversation when the moderator asked about it.

10 Question 4 · what could go into AI

Some signals are already on record, but the “feel” is not

Asked what AI could learn, the salespeople named very different signals. For each, say whether it is tacit knowledge (stays in the person), explicit (already on record somewhere) or both, and compare with the author's reading.

"Both" label on the last quote: the author sees there "evidence of tacit knowledge", such as reading the customer's moment, alongside recorded data.

11 Conclusions and next steps

The groups showed the knowledge exchange, but the AI that would use it has not been built yet

What the groups showed

the author's conclusions (chapter 6)
  • Salespeople share sales knowledge with each other, with customers and with partners.
  • This knowledge is largely tacit; part of it leaves a trail in some record.
  • The four SECI passages appear in what they say, but the companies do not know the model or how to use it.
  • For those interviewed, AI would help get the forecast right if it captured part of the tacit know-how.

What has not been done yet

our observation
  • Building the system: the "feasibility of the model" (Figure 10) comes from the groups' opinions, not from a test.
  • Measuring whether the forecast becomes less wrong, in amount or in date.
  • Using SECI on purpose in a forecast meeting and seeing what changes.
  • Hearing other sectors and more companies (the author suggests a quantitative study).

Sales managers

Organize the weekly forecast meeting around the four SECI passages and record what is learned there, as data. · the author's suggestion

Data and operations teams

Use that record to adjust the datasets from which the AI learns to project sales. · the author's suggestion

Salespeople

Part of the "feel" can already be counted: how many proposals the customer asked for, how fast they answer, whether there was a meeting. Writing it down is a start. · our reading of the quotes

12 Limits and credits

What this research does not say

  • Two companies. Nine people from two large technology and consulting companies do not represent all sales teams. · according to the author
  • By video. The groups were online; in person, gestures and reactions would give more information. · according to the author
  • Opinion, not a test. The research heard what salespeople think; it did not build the AI or measure whether the forecast improves. · our observation
  • A similar group. The nine were men with a university degree, and seven had more than ten years in the field; salespeople with a different profile might tell another story. · Table 2 · our observation
  • One reader's counts. The themes were marked by the author himself; the dissertation does not report a second, independent coding. · our observation
