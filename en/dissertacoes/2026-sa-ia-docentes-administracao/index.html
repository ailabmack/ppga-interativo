The professor and their digital intern

How, and for what, do business administration professors use generative artificial intelligence? Thirteen professors described what they gain, what they distrust and how their own role is changing. Explained in 14 screens.

06 How the conversations became findings

From 65 keywords to 4 ideas

The interviews were transcribed and read in search of patterns. Five keywords came out of each one, backed by excerpts from what was said. They were grouped into codes, the codes into themes, and the themes became four concepts. Click a layer.

Method: systematic thematic analysis (Naeem et al., 2023), carried out with the help of the AI tool NotebookLM and reviewed by the author. Source: Chapters 4 and 5. The total of 65 keywords (5 × 13) is our count.

08 What she found · what they use it for

A case study that took days now takes 15 minutes

One professor said she used to write a case study “in 2, 3 days. Now I do it in 15 minutes”. The study calls this catalysis of efficiency: operational tasks move faster, leaving more time for work that demands attention.

09 What she found · the other side

…but nobody trusts it 100%

The same professors who save time distrust what AI delivers and fear what it does to thinking, both theirs and their students’. The study calls this technological cognitive vulnerability. The professors explained it with everyday images: click each card.

By our count, 8 of the 13 spoke of mistakes or of the need to check everything, and 8 of the risk of depending on the tool. To protect themselves, some only use AI on subjects they know very well (Interview 14). Source: Section 5 and Theme 2.

10 What she found · the professor’s role

From keeper of the truth to “safe link”

With AI in the classroom, the relationship is no longer just between professor and student: a third element appears. For the interviewees, the professor stops being the one who hands down content and becomes the one who guides, checks and makes sense of what the machine produces.

The study calls this shift the mediating redefinition of the teaching role. The opposite fear also came up: one professor thinks AI may reduce the number of teaching jobs (Interview 4). Source: Theme 3 and Section 7.

12 The unexpected

If AI disappeared tomorrow, almost nothing would change

The professors acknowledge that AI is a “road with no way back”. Even so, when the author asked how they would feel if it did not exist, only one professor said they would feel different. The others would feel as they always had.

The dissertation does not identify who that professor was. Source: Chapter 5.

Reflection exists, but it is still shallow

AI led the professors to rethink their practice, but the author interprets this reflection as happening “in a superficial and pragmatic way”: the focus is on keeping up with demands and “not falling behind”. Drag the marker to explore each end of the scale.

13 Why it matters

So what now?

Business administration is one of the degree programs with the most students and graduates in Brazil. The way its professors deal with AI reaches a lot of people, and employers expect both professors and students to know how to use it.

For universities and colleges

Many interviewees said their institutions still have no rules on the use of AI. Without guidelines, each person decides alone, and Brazil’s own Ministry of Education warns that this can deepen inequalities and create ethical problems.

Dissertation, Chapter 5

For professors

The interviewees themselves offer the practical rules: treat AI as an intern that needs supervision, review everything and use it only on content you know well. In class, it can become an “instrument of critical alert” to show students where the machine gets things wrong.

Interviewees’ words · our selection

For students

AI is “a means and not an end”. The fear professors repeated most is that students will swap reasoning for the tool. Using AI without losing the ability to think critically is the skill they hope to develop.

Our reading of Theme 2

For business administration programs

Programs tend to prioritize “learning by doing”. For reflection on AI to go beyond the pragmatic, the author suggests that the education system give professors time and space to think about their own practice, not just courses on tools.

14 Limits and credits

What this study does not say

  • It is a portrait of 13 people, not of the country. Qualitative research seeks depth, not representativeness: 12 of the 13 professors work in São Paulo state.
  • A specific generation. The interviewees are 45 to 71 years old and trained before generative AI existed. Younger professors may see the tool differently.
  • A concentrated profile. 10 of the 13 are men and 11 of the 13 teach at private institutions (our observation from Table 5).
  • It is what professors say. The study gathers perceptions reported in interviews, not observation of classes.
  • Suggested next steps: hear from professors in other regions and in countries without AI guidelines, and understand how AI could prompt deep reflection on practice, not just pragmatic reflection.
