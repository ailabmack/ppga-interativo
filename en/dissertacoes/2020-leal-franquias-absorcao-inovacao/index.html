In language-school franchises, performance went with applying the network’s know-how well and being open to new ideas

Among 127 franchisees from six language-school networks in São Paulo, absorbing the network’s knowledge, taken as a whole, made no clear difference. Only applying what comes from the network was linked to better performance, and that link ran almost entirely through the capacity to innovate. In 10 screens.

Author: Bruno Henrique Santana Leal Advisor: Prof. Dr. Silvio Popadiuk Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

02 The setting · franchises

Buying a franchise means buying a business that has already worked

Under Brazil’s franchise law (Law 8,955/94, quoted in the dissertation), the network grants the unit the use of its brand and, often, the way to set up and run the business. The author recalls an old definition: a franchise is a way of replicating success. Open the package, layer by layer:

03 The vocabulary · absorptive capacity

Absorbing knowledge has four stages, from contact with the network to the classroom

Cohen and Levinthal (1990) called absorptive capacity the ability to recognise the value of outside knowledge, assimilate it and apply it. Zahra and George (2002) split it into four stages: the first two make up the potential part, the last two the realised part. The dissertation also measured innovativeness (the capacity to innovate) and performance. Tap each stop along the path:

Section 2.1 and Charts 1 to 3. The statements come from the questionnaire (scales by Jiménez-Barrionuevo and colleagues, 2011; Hurley and Hult, 1998; Tsai and Yang, 2013, adapted by the author), translated by us.

04 How the research looked

127 franchisees from six language-school networks answered

From the Brazilian Franchising Association (ABF) website, the author built a spreadsheet of every member network and filtered the service networks based in São Paulo with the ABF seal. He chose language schools because they all do the same thing. Of the networks invited, six agreed, on condition that their brands stay undisclosed. In 2019, franchisees answered online or in person.

05 What franchisees said

Innovation got the highest marks, and performance stayed close to “unchanged”

Statements about absorbing and innovating ran from 1 (strongly disagree) to 6 (strongly agree). Performance compared 2018 with 2017, from 1 (fell a lot) to 5 (rose a lot), with 3 for “stayed the same”. Pick a block and tap the dots:

06 The first answer · 127 franchisees

Taken together, the four stages barely explained performance

The dissertation’s first hypothesis was that absorptive capacity, taken as a whole, improves the unit’s performance. Before you look, place a bet:

Figures 10 and 11 (SmartPLS models) and section 4.4. The tank level is the R² (full = 100%): the share of the performance differences between units that the model explains. “Not significant” means the result could be down to chance at 5% (for the whole model, t = 1.454).

07 What it found · the four stages

Taken apart, two stages counted: applying pulled up and transforming pulled down

As the whole showed no effect, the author put the four stages side by side in the same model. Each bar shows how much performance changes, in standard deviations, when the stage rises one standard deviation and the others stay the same. Tap a bar:

Figure 11. Together, the four stages explain 22.5% of the performance differences. Hollow bars: not significant at 5%. Height is proportional to the coefficient.

08 The surprise · transforming

Transforming only turns negative when applying is in the model too

Transforming, in the scale used, means holding informal meetings, passing information to the team regularly and knowing how to spread knowledge. See its coefficient in three of the dissertation’s models:

The author’s reading

A franchise is a copy of a business that worked; the more a unit transforms itself, the further it drifts from the network’s model and the lower its performance tends to be.

Our reading

The statements measure talking and spreading information within the team, not changing the model. And, on its own, transforming does not weigh in (−0.157, not significant; correlation with performance of −0.101). The sign appears when comparing units that apply equally well: among them, those reporting more meetings and information flow reported slightly lower performance.

Figures 11 and 12, section 4.4 and Table 10. The author calls this a “suppressor effect” (Hair and colleagues, 2014). Our reading describes what a partial coefficient measures; it is not an explanation tested in the dissertation.

09 The finding · the second hypothesis

With the capacity to innovate in between, the direct link from applying to performance almost vanished

The second hypothesis said innovativeness sits halfway between absorbing and performance. Since the whole showed no effect, the author tested the path with only the stage that counted, applying. Switch innovation on and off and watch the flow:

Figures 13 and 14 and section 5.1. Pipe thickness and the number of dots follow the coefficients. The author concludes there is “full mediation”; the dissertation has no separate test of the path through innovation, so the product 0.732 × 0.334 is our calculation.

10 So what? · limits and credits

Performance went along with applying the network’s know-how well and letting the team suggest improvements

For franchisees

A unit is, above all, the one that applies the knowledge coming from the network. Backing proposals, encouraging creativity and valuing the team’s good ideas was what went most closely with performance. (author)

For the franchisor

Train units so they can apply what they receive, and make room, within the network’s rules, for improvements that start at the front desk. (our reading) Hearing the franchisors too is the next step the author proposes. (author)

For researchers

Repeat the study in other regions, other services and product franchises, with larger samples and franchisees at the same stage. (author)

What this research does not say

  • One city only. The networks are based in São Paulo, chosen for convenience, in one of the country’s richest regions. · according to the author
  • Perception, not accounts. Franchisees rated their own unit; there were no revenue or profit figures, and marks may be inflated. · according to the author
  • Mixed sample. Some had less than a year in the business and others more than 20; three networks account for 93% of answers. · author and our count
  • One model after another. Each model was built from what the previous one showed; it is an exploratory path that another sample needs to confirm. · our note
  • Path without its own test. The dissertation does not report the significance of the path through innovation; the 0.24 is our calculation. And since everything was measured at a single point in time, the numbers show links, not causes. · our note
