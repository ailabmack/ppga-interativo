Can judicial reorganization save a football club?

Brazilian football clubs piled up debt for decades. Since 2021, the law lets them file for judicial reorganization (a court-supervised restructuring, similar to Chapter 11) and become companies, the SAF (football corporation). The dissertation followed eight clubs from 2020 to 2024 to see what actually improved their financial health. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The problem

A club can go broke in three ways

The dissertation starts from a distinction common in finance and law: the crisis can lie in results, in cash or in the balance sheet. Each calls for a different remedy.

In Brazilian football, according to the author, management driven by political interests, expensive signings and debts for taxes, wages and short-term loans tend to feed all three at once.

Illustrative bars, not real values. The link between each crisis and an indicator of the thermometer (screen 05) is our reading.

03 The context

In 2021, the law opened two doors for clubs

Until then, most clubs were non-profit associations. For the courts, the reorganization and bankruptcy law (Law 11,101/2005) applied only to companies. An indebted club faced creditors' lawsuits one by one.

The SAF Law (Law 14,193/2021) changed that. Compare before and after and tap each door.

04 How the research looked

Eight clubs, two splits

The author chose four clubs under judicial reorganization (JR) and four similar ones that did not file. In each group, two became SAFs and two remain associations. This makes it possible to separate the effect of JR from the effect of the club's legal form.

All are more than 50 years old, played in Brazil's first or second division (Série A or B) and publish financial statements. In the JR cases, the process had been running for over two years. Tap a club.

05 The method

A 0-to-1 thermometer for a club's health

The author created the Financial Health Indicator (FHI): the average of five indicators, each with the same weight (20%).

Each indicator runs from 0 (the club's own worst year in the period) to 1 (its best); negative values count as zero. Move the sliders or pick a real year.

06 What it found · 2020 to 2024

Under judicial reorganization, the thermometer went up and down

In 2021, all four clubs under JR were in the insolvency band. Three reached the top of the scale in 2023, and all fell back in 2024.

07 Inside the thermometer · clubs in JR

Cash reacted first; profit and equity swung

Under JR, debt collection is put on hold and debts are renegotiated. That is why, according to the dissertation, liquidity and interest coverage respond faster. Equity and return on assets depend on deeper changes.

08 The finding · question 1

Judicial reorganization bought breathing room, but it did not last

For the author, JR on its own was not effective at producing a lasting improvement in the clubs' financial health. It eases the short term, but the profit, interest and equity indicators remain under pressure.

09 The finding · question 2

The club's legal form mattered more than the court process

For the author, the organizational model influenced performance: clubs that became SAFs, even under JR, showed more favorable and less unstable paths; associations under JR were stuck in cycles of incomplete recovery.

12 In practice, limits and credits

The law buys time; management decides what to do with it

Club directors

JR puts debt collection on hold and reorganizes debts. Without changing how the club makes money, the relief tends to be short-lived, as at Avaí in 2024.

Investors

New capital and governance helped stabilize the club-companies studied, but did not guarantee operating profit: Botafogo kept posting operating losses.

Lawmakers and regulators

For the author, recovery depends less on legal instruments alone and more on governance, structure and financial discipline. He notes that Brazil lacks enforced rules such as European financial fair play.

Fans (our reading)

One good year on the balance sheet is not a cure. It is worth checking whether the club now profits from operations, covers its interest and rebuilds its equity.

What this research does not say

  • Eight mid-sized clubs over five years: they may not represent all of Brazilian football, and the period is short to see a full recovery. · according to the author
  • Only financial indicators: management, governance and the presence of investors were not measured directly. · according to the author
  • Equal weights in the FHI: the author suggests revalidating the formula and the weights in new studies. · according to the author
  • A comparison of cases, without statistical testing. The FHI compares each club with itself, so it shows rises and falls better than which club is healthier. The values here were read from the dissertation's charts. · our note
