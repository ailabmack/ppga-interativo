What helps people learn from their own mistakes

Making mistakes at work is unavoidable, and learning from them does not happen by itself. With 176 people who work in organisations, the dissertation measures how much weight lies with the person’s own capabilities, their psychological capital and the way the organisation handles mistakes. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The problem

Mistakes cannot be eliminated, and learning from them depends on many things

Mistakes hurt. Failures stand out and stir up anxiety, anger, shame and guilt, and from school onwards we are rewarded for making fewer mistakes. So the tendency is to hide a mistake rather than study it. The dissertation starts by telling three words apart:

The two paths summarise the introduction and sections 2.1 and 2.2 (Cannon and Edmondson, 2001; Dahlin, Chuang and Roulet, 2018; Frese and Keith, 2015). The dissertation treats error, failure and mistake as interchangeable, because the boundaries between them blur.

11 What it found · the scoreboard

Three bets were confirmed, and the environment acted the opposite way to what was expected

Agentic capabilities are linked to psychological capital (H3) and to learning from mistakes (H2). Psychological capital is too (H1), but that link weakens with the environment in the model. The moderations (H4 and H5) appeared with the opposite sign. Tap a column or a card:

12 So what?

Learning from mistakes starts with each person’s capabilities

For the author, organisations gain by developing these capabilities and by replacing punishment of mistakes with conversation about them.

For HR and managers

  • Train problem solving, constructive feedback and reflection on practice; offer coaching, mentoring and psychological support. (author)
  • Take these capabilities into account when attracting talent. (author)

For the organisation

  • Replace the punitive approach with transparency, with regular “lessons learned” sessions and quick feedback. (author)
  • Our reading: from screen 9, a good environment matters more to those with less psychological capital or fewer capabilities, such as people who are just starting out.

For people at work

  • After a mistake, reflect on what you did and watch how more experienced colleagues handled similar situations. (our reading, based on the questionnaire statements)

What this study does not say

  • Who answered. 176 people from a convenience sample, concentrated in Brazil; it does not represent all workers. · according to the author
  • Self-assessment. Everything comes from the same questionnaire, about the person themself; it shows what goes together, not what causes what, and does not measure actual mistakes. · author and our observation
  • Very close measures. Agentic capabilities and learning from mistakes correlate at 0.853, almost at the limit for being different measures; the author treats them as distinct. · author and our observation
  • Translated and shortened scales. Statements that did not work were removed, not rewritten. · according to the author
  • What was not tested. The effect that passes through psychological capital had no significance test, and the moderation came out opposite to what was expected. · our observation
