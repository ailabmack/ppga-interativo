Did stocks with an ESG seal earn more than the market?

B3, the Brazilian stock exchange, runs eight indexes of companies with good environmental, social and governance practices. The research compared each one with the Ibovespa from 2019 to 2023 and at the start of the pandemic. None earned more; on ordinary days they swung a little less; in the Covid shock they fell further.

02 The starting point · Table 1

Joining an ESG index works like earning a seal

A stock index is a theoretical portfolio: a list of companies whose price is tracked every day. The Ibovespa gathers the country's most traded stocks. In ESG indexes, each exchange sets its own entry rules, and being included works as a seal of good practice.

B3 created the first of them, the governance index (IGC), in 2001; the carbon index (ICO2) came in 2010 and the best-places-to-work index (IGPTW) in 2019. Tap an index.

The grouping by letter (E, S, G or all three) is ours, based on each index's description in Table 1.

03 The question · sections 2.1 and 3.2

Three theories, three bets on the ESG seal

Caring for the environment, people and governance costs money. Does that take away or bring return to shareholders? Each finance theory gives a different answer. Before seeing the data, pick yours.

The dissertation bet on the first: ESG indexes would have earned more than the Ibovespa, because the market watches these companies more closely.

05 Article 1 · Table 4

None of the eight ESG indexes earned more than the Ibovespa

Over the period, even the Ibovespa ended in the red on the average day: it lost about 0.011% per trading day. Every ESG index lost more. The closest was ITAG; the furthest, IGPTW, which lost 0.07% a day, more than six times the Ibovespa.

Each column is the average loss on one day (mean log return from Table 4, × 100). Tap a column. The dissertation's hypothesis that the ESG seal would earn more was not confirmed.

06 Article 1 · Table 3

Statistically, five of the eight can't be told apart from the Ibovespa

Losing a little more on average doesn't mean much when days vary a lot. The author ran two tests for each index, comparing it day by day with the Ibovespa: one looks at the shape of returns (the ups and downs), the other at the average.

07 Article 1 · Table 4

On the risk yardsticks, the Ibovespa came last

The Sharpe and Sortino ratios divide the gain above the CDI, Brazil's benchmark fixed-income rate, by the swings (Sortino counts only the falls). All came out negative: none paid for the risk compared with the CDI. But on both yardsticks, the eight ESG indexes came ahead of the Ibovespa, as the author points out.

Our reading. Turn on the swings: when the gain is negative, whoever swings more gets a less negative score. IGPTW, the one that swung and lost the most, got the best score.

08 Article 1 · Table 5 · CAPM

When the market swings, ESG indexes swing a little less

Beta measures how much an index follows the market premium (the Ibovespa minus the CDI). All eight came between 0.81 and 0.89, all safely below 1. For the author, this makes ESG indexes more defensive portfolios than the Ibovespa.

The market premium explains 55% (IGPTW) to 79% (IGCT) of each index's ups and downs (R²). The simulation uses only the betas from Table 5, without the constant: it is an illustration, not a forecast.

09 Article 2 · Table 5 and Figure 6

When Covid arrived, ESG indexes fell more than the Ibovespa predicted

Using 116 trading days before the crisis, the author estimated how much each index usually moves when the Ibovespa moves. Then, over the 20 days before and 20 days after the first case, he added up the gap between what happened and what was expected: the cumulative abnormal return. Below zero, the index did worse than expected.

Curves rebuilt by us by adding up the daily abnormal returns in Table 5; they match Figure 6. We show the shape and the order, in the table's unit. The band marks, by our count of trading days, 9 to 18 March 2020, when B3 halted trading six times (circuit breakers).

10 Article 2 · our sum of Table 5

The carbon index fell least; the best-places-to-work index, most

ICO2 (carbon) and ISE (sustainability) ended up far less below expectations than the rest. For the author, energy efficiency may signal steadier costs to investors. IGPTW, made of companies rated highly by their employees, fell most: perhaps the market saw them as more exposed to the pandemic's social uncertainty, he suggests.

The text names IGPTW and ITAG as the most sensitive; by the sum of the table, IDIVERSA (diversity) sits between the two.

11 Both articles together

In calm times, more defensive; in the storm, no shelter

The two articles seem to say opposite things. On average over four and a half years, ESG indexes swung less than the market. In the Covid shock, they did worse than the market predicted. Flip the card.

The author's reading: in an extreme crisis, investors' priority becomes financial survival, and ESG practices take a back seat.

12 In practice · limits · credits

The ESG seal guaranteed neither higher returns nor protection in a crisis

For investors

Many index funds (ETFs) copy portfolios like these. The research shows that, from 2019 to 2023, the ESG seal did not bring higher returns or hold back the falls at the start of the pandemic. Past results do not guarantee the future, and this page is not investment advice.

For the exchange and companies

The author suggests revisiting each index's entry criteria and publicizing them more, and using the results when building portfolios and creating new indexes, in Brazil or other exchanges.

For researchers

Split the sectors within each index, look at the financial statements of member companies (ROA, ROI, EBITDA), study the companies that stay longest in the indexes and understand why ICO2 and ISE followed the market with fewer jolts.

What this research does not say

  • One period only. 2019 to 2023, with a pandemic in the middle, on an exchange ranked 19th in the world; the companies in each index also change over time. · according to the author
  • Theoretical portfolios. Indexes pay no fees or trading costs; a real investor would get different results. · our note
  • Yardsticks on different scales. The CDI in Table 2 (mean 0.0304) seems to be on a different scale from the daily returns (close to −0.0002); so we show only the order on Sharpe and Sortino. The abnormal returns in Table 5 reach −0.44 in a day, too large for an ordinary daily return; so we show the shape and order of the curves, without percentages. · our note
  • Article 2 betas without strength. In the GMM model, no beta has a t-statistic close to 2; so we do not show the reading that six indexes moved against the market. · our note
  • Association, not cause. The research compares portfolios; it does not show that ESG practices caused the losses or the falls. · our note
