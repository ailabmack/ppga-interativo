Master’s dissertation · PPGA Mackenzie · 2018

How do the people who create the supermarket’s own-brand products learn and innovate?

The rice, soap or biscuits that carry the chain’s own name are the store brand. The dissertation surveyed 76 managers and analysts who develop these products at four large chains in São Paulo: how they absorb new knowledge, how they learn and how innovative they see themselves. 03 The question · Wang, Bloom, Dave and Hurt

Absorbing the new, learning and innovating: does learning sit in the middle?

Absorbing is recognising the value of new knowledge, digesting it and using it. The question: does the link between absorbing and innovating run through the way people learn? Tap each link:

10 The unexpected · what weighs in each measure

The research’s novelty was feeling and doing, yet knowing weighs most

The author added feeling and doing because absorption studies looked almost only at knowing. Guess: which of the three weighs most in the learning score the model builds?

Table 14: the loading of each part on the measure above it (0 to 1) and, below, the R², the share of it that the measure above explains. Our reading: doing was measured with physical-activity statements (guitar, dancing, brushing teeth), far from the work of creating a product.

11 So what?

Those who absorb the new well also see themselves as more innovative, and part of that runs through how they learn

In the author’s reading, the questionnaire can serve as a diagnostic so that companies act more specifically on activities that call for absorbing knowledge.

For people who lead product teams

  • Use the scale as a diagnostic: where the team absorbs less, where it learns less (according to the author).
  • Look beyond technical knowledge: attitude and practice are part of learning too (our reading of the author).

For retail

  • Today store brands compete on quality, not only on price; that calls for people who bring in and use new knowledge (context from Section 3.1).
  • Creativity was the part that weighed most in the group’s capacity to innovate.

For research

  • Ask each person to rate a colleague (a projective technique) instead of themselves.
  • Go to other chains and states and also hear consumers.

What this research does not say

  • Everyone rated themselves. The scores may be higher than reality. · according to the author
  • Few people, one place. 76 answers from 4 chains in the city of São Paulo, chosen for convenience. · according to the author
  • The path through learning was not tested. The dissertation compares the direct bridge before and after; the indirect effect has no test of its own. · our note
  • Moving together is not cause. Everything was measured at once, in the same questionnaire; the innovating measure had a reliability of 0.671, below the usual 0.7 and accepted by the author because the study is exploratory. · our note
