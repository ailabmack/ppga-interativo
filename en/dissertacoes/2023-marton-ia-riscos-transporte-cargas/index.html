Master's dissertation · PPGA Mackenzie · 2023

Against cargo theft, the artificial intelligence that already works is the rule “if this happens, do that”

The dissertation heard six professionals with 17 to 21 years in the field. All of them describe systems of rules written by specialists, which standardise and speed up the response to a suspected theft. An AI that learns on its own and predicts risk was not shown in use by anyone: it is what they expect from the future.

02 The problem

Cargo thefts fell by a third, but losses fell far less

Brazil depends on the truck: about 65% of freight travels by road, and only 12.4% of the roads are paved. Add thefts and accidents. That is why insurers require, and a whole industry sells, “risk management” for every trip.

03 How a load is protected

Before, during and after the trip, each tool tries to take a chance away from the thief

“Managing risk” in freight means combining measures so that the load arrives at the right place, on time and in one piece. Some lower the chance of a theft; others lower the damage when it happens. Tracking, the most used in Brazil, arrived in 1992, because public security could not cope with the problem.

Tools described in chapter 2 of the dissertation. The split into before, during and after the trip and the chance and damage labels are ours, based on those descriptions.

04 Two ideas of AI

One AI follows rules written by people; the other learns patterns by itself

Since the 1950s, artificial intelligence has followed two paths. Expert systems use logical rules, “if this, then that”, dictated by specialists in the subject; they work well, but get heavy as the possibilities multiply. Neural networks imitate the brain: instead of rules, they get lots of data and recognise patterns on their own.

[...] setting up a risk management plan means getting a specialist to understand what kinds of things will happen in given situations, and the map of situations is wide, today there are more than 400 possible situations for you to configure [...]E02 · translated by us

05 How the research looked

Six professionals, from four ends of the business, told how AI enters their daily work

The author held face-to-face interviews, following a question guide, with people in charge of cargo security: insurance, the industry that ships the goods, companies that make trackers and software, and risk management firms. He then read the transcripts and grouped the passages by meaning, until he arrived at five themes.

06 Theme 2 · Players

Five players share the security of a load, and insurance sets the rules

The industry hires the carrier; the carrier installs trackers; the risk management firm watches the trip with the manufacturer's software. The rules of this security plan come from the insurance policy: the insurer writes them together with the risk management firm, so that the risk becomes acceptable. 07 Theme 1 · What kind of AI

All six describe “if, then” rules; nobody showed a neural network at work

All of them spoke of expert systems. Four said outright that neural networks are not in use; the other two mentioned none. In insurance, the price of a risk still comes from the analyst's experience, without AI. Tap a code to read what they said.

09 Theme 3 · Benefits

The rules filter the alerts, and the control room does more with fewer people

With rules, the system separates routine from what looks like theft and only calls the operator when needed. The interviewees mention faster response, standardised processes and financial gains, above all on payroll, the largest cost of risk management firms.

10 The surprise

Five of the six see the biggest gain outside the company: on the road, at the pharmacy, in the street

Asked about benefits, the interviewees talked about lives and public health. An accident avoided does not close the road or fill the hospital; a stolen medicine does not return, badly stored, to the shelf; a gang uncovered is less crime. For the author, companies hold information that could help public security.

Unit of meaning US20 (positive social impact): E01 to E05.

11 Theme 4 · Barriers

The brake is not technology: it is a closed market that moves in short steps

Firms in the field know each other and use similar security plans. There is no specific training for people who work in risk management, and the culture comes from the military and insurance worlds. On top of that, new technology is added without making the most of what is already installed. Tap each barrier.

12 Theme 5 · Expectations

The next step they ask for: predict risk and look at the whole chain

The interviewees want to move beyond “just if, then” to an AI that learns from history to choose routes and anticipate risky volumes and regions. They also want to connect the security system with the logistics one, to deal with accidents and shortages, not just theft. Choose a road to see the dilemma.

13 So what?

AI against cargo theft is already routine, but it is still the rule-based kind

Risk management and technology firmsFor the author, the interviewees' expectations (predicting risk, joining security and logistics) are a map for anyone who wants to invest in the field.
InsurersIn pricing insurance, two interviewees say there is still no AI, only the analyst's experience. The author suggests a study of its own on that side. Our reading: this is where the claims history could pay off most.
GovernmentThe author sees private companies' data as a possible support for public security in fighting cargo theft, through partnerships.

What the research does not say

  • Six voices. Six interviewees, from one region, do not speak for the whole of Brazil and all its differences. (author)
  • The service providers' side. The research looked at those who offer risk management and technology, not at insurance pricing. (author)
  • Accounts, not measurements. Gains such as “from 30 or 40 people to six” are told by the interviewees; the dissertation did not measure the effect of AI on thefts or costs. (our note)
  • What counts as “AI”. “If, then” rules count as AI because the literature used and the interviewees themselves classify them that way; some people only call AI what learns by itself. (our note)

Next steps suggested by the author: focus groups or the Delphi technique with experts, and quantitative studies that measure and relate the themes found.
