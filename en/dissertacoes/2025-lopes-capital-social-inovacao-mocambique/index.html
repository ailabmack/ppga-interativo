Trust, innovation and results in Mozambique

In a country where institutions inspire little trust, knowing whom you can count on inside the company may make a difference. With 70 professionals from private companies in Maputo, the dissertation asks whether these ties improve results, and by which path. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The problem

In Mozambique, companies grow in a difficult, low-trust environment

Mozambique lies in south-eastern Africa, with its capital in Maputo. The dissertation describes a market with little access to technology, a low-skilled workforce, unpredictable rules and low trust. 05 How the research looked

70 professionals from private companies in Maputo answered

The author ran the survey remotely, from Brazil, with a Google Forms questionnaire. Each person answered about their own company: the ties between colleagues, the way it innovates and how its 2024 results compared with 2023. The answers were analysed with structural equation modelling (PLS-SEM), which measures links between ideas that cannot be observed directly.

08 The discovery

Ties reach results by way of innovation

On its own, social capital is linked to performance (0.424). When innovation capability enters the model, this direct arrow drops to 0.095 and no longer has a clear effect. Ties are linked to innovation (0.631), and innovation to performance (0.521). The dissertation calls this full mediation. Switch the model:

Figures 12 and 13 and section 4.3. β = standardised coefficient (0.424 and 0.631 significant at 1%; 0.095 not significant at 5%). Effect through innovation: 0.631 × 0.521 ≈ 0.329, and 0.329 + 0.095 = 0.424, the same as the ties-only model (our calculations; the dissertation does not report a statistical test of this indirect effect). R² of performance: 18.0% → 34.4%; of innovation: 39.9%.

11 So what?

Investing in trust pays more when the company turns ties into innovation

For the author, investing in social capital is strategic, but not enough: relationships need to turn into learning, shared knowledge and actual innovation.

For company managers

  • Build structures, processes and a culture that favour innovation: encouraging experimentation, supporting creativity, tolerating mistakes and learning-oriented leadership. (author)
  • Go beyond promoting networks and trust: create conditions for these relationships to become shared knowledge. (author)

For companies in fragile markets

  • In markets with fragile institutions, such as Mozambique’s, cooperation between companies can help overcome shortages of resources and infrastructure. (author)
  • Our reading: the survey measured ties between colleagues within each company; cooperation between companies is the extension the author proposes, not something the questionnaire tested.

For public policy

  • Programmes that foster business networks, partnerships, clusters and collaborative environments, together with incentives for innovation, as a way to promote sustainable economic development. (author)
12 Limits and credits

What this research does not say

  • Small sample. 70 responses, below the 81 calculated for the analysis; the findings cannot be safely generalised. · according to the author
  • Only Maputo. Companies in other regions of the country may be different. · according to the author
  • A single moment. The questionnaire was applied once: it shows what goes together, not what causes what over time. · according to the author
  • Perceptions. Ties, innovation and results are what each person reported, without the companies’ accounting data. · according to the author
  • The path through innovation. Mediation was read from the drop in the direct arrow; a statistical test of the indirect effect does not appear in the dissertation. · our observation

Next steps suggested by the author: larger samples from other regions, studies over time, other factors (culture, leadership, absorptive capacity) and comparisons between countries.
