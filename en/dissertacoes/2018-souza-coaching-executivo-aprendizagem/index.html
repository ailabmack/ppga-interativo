What does an executive learn when they hire a coach?

The study listened to 10 coachees (executives, independent professionals and entrepreneurs who went through coaching) and 4 coaches to understand how that learning happens. The lens is transformative learning: when something unsettling forces a person to rethink what they thought they knew. Explained in 13 screens.

02 The problem

Executive coaching is growing fast, but little is known about how the coachee learns

Companies pay coaches to develop their executives, and many people pay out of their own pocket. The author searched the research databases and found few studies on how the coachee learns and what the “active ingredients” of the process are. Compare:

03 The lens · Mezirow

For Mezirow, real learning starts when something stops making sense

Jack Mezirow’s transformative learning describes how an adult changes their own “frame of reference”: the beliefs they use to make sense of the world. It goes through up to ten phases, not always in this order. Tap each one; those outlined in red show up in what one coachee, L, said.

04 How the study looked

Fourteen people told their story: ten coachees and four coaches

The author held in-depth interviews with a ten-question open script and analysed what people said by themes (Braun and Clarke’s thematic analysis). The coaches were external to the companies, had at least three years of executive coaching and a past as executives. They were the ones who pointed to the coachees, “snowball” style: people whose process the coach saw as transformative.

05 Theme 1 · How learning happens

The coach’s first job is to make the coachee discover what they don’t know

The coaches interviewed use a four-stage ladder (Burch, 1970). Many people arrive on the first step: they think they are doing their best and cannot see what is missing. Tap the steps.

06 Theme 1 · The disorienting dilemma

No one looks for a coach for nothing: it all starts with unease about one’s own work

Mezirow calls this moment a “disorienting dilemma”: the usual way stops working. In the accounts it shows up as dissatisfaction with results, as not knowing that you don’t know or as knowing that you don’t know, always tied to the career. And it arrives in three ways:

07 Theme 1 · Dialogue and self-reflection

Conversation is the vehicle: questions and paraphrases lead the coachee to see themselves

The coach asks questions, restates what they heard (paraphrasing) and does not judge. That opens room for self-reflection and critical thinking, which act as filters: the dilemma, beliefs and experiences go in and come out as clear goals, self-knowledge, action and learning. Tap the boxes.

08 Theme 1 · The coach–coachee relationship

Those who sought a coach on their own resisted less than those sent by the company

09 Theme 1 · From sessions to action

There is no single method: each coach draws tools from their own background, but all of them demand action

The two coaches trained in psychology, with a background in HR, rely on profile assessments. The two who come from education and accounting, with a background in management and sales, adapt business tools. What they share is the task between one session and the next: it is at work that the coachee tests what they reflected on. Tap a tool.

10 Theme 2 · The results

Every coachee changed what they do; half also changed how they see the world

To decide what counts as transformation, the author used Chad Hoggan’s six categories, drawn from 206 articles on transformative learning. Open each door to see the definition, what is known about how many changed and what they said.

11 The finding

For 7 of the 10 coachees, the change ran deep, spread from work into life and lasted

Hoggan asks for three tests before calling a change transformative: depth (its impact), breadth (it shows up in more than one part of life) and stability (it stays). Seven coachees passed all three. Three (NE, A and J) reported no change outside work. Choose the test and tap a tower.

Section 4.2.7 and Tables 24 and 25 (the quotes in the towers). Our reading: the coaches pointed precisely to coachees they saw as cases of transformation, so the 7 of 10 describe this group, not everyone who does coaching.

12 The unexpected

Coaching can also show a person that they are in the wrong place

Three cases break the pattern: someone who already watched themselves so closely that nothing was shaken, someone who found out they no longer fit the company, and the coaches themselves, who say they went through a transformation when they were coachees.

Sections 4.2 and 4.2.1. The rule of stopping the process is the ethical recommendation the author describes; it did not apply to L, because L paid for the coaching.

13 So what? Limits and credits

Transformative learning works as a map for designing and assessing executive coaching

What the study suggests

  • For those who hire on the company’s behalf (HR): let the executive choose the coach and learn beforehand how the process works, to reduce resistance.
  • For coaches: start from the dilemma, look after the setting and trust, plan each session and use the phases and categories of transformative learning to check whether transformation happened.
  • For anyone thinking of doing coaching: come willing to change and to do the tasks between sessions; without that, coaches and coachees say, it does not work.

The suggestions for HR and coaches come from sections 4.1.1 and 5; the third item is our reading of the accounts.

What this study does not say

  • Only two sides. It heard coaches and coachees; the company, colleagues and outside observers were left out. The author suggests hearing them. · according to the author
  • The researcher’s own lens. The author is a coach and a psychologist, and acknowledges that this may have pulled the interpretation. · according to the author
  • Chosen cases. The coachees were referred by coaches who saw their process as transformative; we cannot say how many, among everyone who does coaching, are transformed. · our note, based on section 3.2
  • Memories, not measurements. The results are what people said, without measuring performance before and after. · our note
