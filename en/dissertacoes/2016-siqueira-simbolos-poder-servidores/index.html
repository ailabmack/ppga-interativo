What power means to a public servant

An ID badge, job security, a heart, a coffin. The author asked 73 federal public servants in justice and health, in São Paulo, to name and draw the symbols of power in their work. And she asked: what do these symbols reveal about people's values? Explained in 12 screens.

02 The problem · chapter 2

There are two ways to study values, and they barely talk to each other

One lens says values are universal, inherited from human evolution. The other says each group creates its own values by giving meaning to everyday things. They start from opposite ideas about what reality is. The dissertation tries to use them together (what the author calls a metaparadigmatic perspective). Compare by aspect:

Basic values · Schwartz

symbolsthe bridge the author proposes
Social values · Thomas and Znaniecki

The interpretivist lens

The author assumes that a symbol (an object, a drawing, a word) can carry both universal values and the group's values at once. This assumption is what lets her apply both lenses to the same data.

03 The first lens · section 2.1.3.3

For Schwartz, values form a wheel: neighbours get along, opposites clash

Shalom Schwartz's theory, the most widely used in the world, finds the same types of value in every culture. They sit in a circle: pursuing two neighbours at once is easy; pursuing two opposites creates conflict. Power, for example, sits opposite benevolence and universalism. Click a value.

Wheel simplified by us: the 19 types of the 2012 version (Table 1 and Figure 3 of the dissertation) grouped into 12 families. Hedonism, face and humility sit between two dimensions.

04 How the research looked · chapter 3

When words failed, the servants drew

The author, herself a public servant, heard 73 tenured servants from two federal organizations in São Paulo, one in justice and one in health. The organizations are not identified. Each dot is one person:

43 in justice30 in health

Purposive and convenience selection: people of both sexes and various ages, education levels and lengths of service. The analysis followed Flores's (1994) method: split the statements into units, group them into categories that emerge from the data itself, and compare.

06 Finding 1 · sections 4.1 to 4.4

For the servants, power has a name: the State

Almost every symbol led to the State. The author treats it as a "macro" social value that branches into contents (the people, the buildings, politics) and into "micro" values closer to daily life. Switch the area and click the blocks.

07 Finding 2 · sections 4.2 and 4.3

The same symbol speaks of power and of its absence

The author calls this the ambiguity of social values: the same value appears tied to exercising power and also to its absence. Click the cards to flip them.

08 Finding 3 · sections 4.3.4 to 4.3.6

In health, the servant's power depends on resources and politics

Only the health servants brought two values of their own: the material State (equipment, hospital, computer) and the State as a political arena (funding and decisions by those in power). Both speak mostly of lack: lack of resources, doctors and recognition. One servant drew the power of health as a chain; click a link to remove it.

“(...) it's the material, the professional, the administrative, the executive and the funding, everything is necessary for public health to happen” (S52)

09 Finding 4 · section 4.5

Each group value carries several universal values, and power is in all of them

Here the lenses meet. For each of the servants' "micro" values, the author read in their statements which values from Schwartz's wheel lay behind it. Each tower is a group value; each storey, a universal value, in the colour of its dimension. Click a tower.

10 The finding · chapter 5

In the group's daily life, values the theory places on opposite sides go together

For the servants, serving the public is power: the power to help. In these statements, power (self-enhancement) appears together with benevolence and universalism (self-transcendence), which sit on the other side of the wheel. Pick a group value and see the links; the red ones cross the wheel.

The author presents this as a supposition: value types that are opposite on the wheel may be reconciled depending on how the group lives its social values. She did not measure values with Schwartz's scale; she read them in the statements.

11 The unexpected · chapters 5 and 6

For Schwartz, power is controlling others; for the servants, it is also having time and being able to help

In the theory, the value power is control over people and resources, status and prestige. The servants used "power" for much more. Click each meaning.

The author's supposition

The motivation triangle

For future studies, the author proposes that group values satisfy human needs, that these needs are communicated through universal values, and that these in turn are expressed in the group's values (Figure 55).

12 So what?

Looking at symbols helps understand what drives people in public service

For the author, combining the two lenses broadens our understanding of values: universal values gain each group's context, and group values gain the human motivations behind them.

For public managers

  • Interpret the symbols the team uses to understand motivations built over time, not just immediate ones. (author)
  • Through symbols, see how each person expresses universal values through the group's values. (author)

For public service

  • The power of the post raises ethical issues, such as using the official ID for personal gain. (author)
  • In health, the power to serve patients well runs into the lack of resources and of recognition for careers. (author)

For values researchers

  • Test with Schwartz's scale (SVS or PVQ) what was read here in the statements, using mixed methods. (author)
  • Investigate whether group values change the hierarchy of universal values, and vice versa. (author)
  • Use drawings when people can't find the words for a topic. (author)

What this research does not say

  • Values read, not measured. Schwartz's values were inferred from statements and drawings; no scale was applied, and the author suggests mixed methods. · according to the author
  • Opposites coexisting is a supposition. The author raises the idea for future studies; it is not a test of the theory. · according to the author
  • Two organizations, one city. What holds for these 73 federal servants in São Paulo does not describe all of public service. · our note
  • Different techniques by area. In justice, mostly interviews, with easier access; in health, more drawings and a group, which the author says did not follow the full focus-group method. · author and us
