From value created to value captured

What happens between what a digital bank offers and what it gets out of it? With 117 customers of Brazilian banks, the research followed that path: the bank creates value, the customer perceives it and the bank captures it. It also tested whether inviting customers to co-create makes a difference. Explained in 12 screens.

08 What it found

Creating value pulls perception along, and both reach capture

The strongest link is the first: customers who see the bank create value for them also perceive more value. Creation and perceived value are each linked to capture, with smaller coefficients. The thicker the arrow, the stronger the link. Tap an arrow:

Standardized coefficients (β), estimated in SmartPLS 4 with 5,000 bootstrap resamples. Thickness proportional to β. They show association at a single point in time, not proof of cause. Source: Table 15.

09 The unexpected

Inviting customers to co-create did not change the path

The literature bets on co-creation: customers who take part, customize and give their opinion would strengthen each stretch of the path. In the data, this did not show up in any of the three. Move how much the customer feels like a co-creator and compare both sides:

The author's reading

Digital banking services are still designed for automated delivery, with little customer involvement in their development. Customers do not see their own participation as something that creates value.

11 Why it matters

Caring for what customers perceive links creating and capturing

For the author, success in capturing value depends on how customers perceive what they receive. And co-creation, for now, is a promise the banks have not yet kept.

For bank managers

  • Prioritize value creation through innovation, personalization, transaction security and efficiency.
  • Tie each innovation to the customer experience, so that perceived value turns into loyalty and return.
Section 5.2

For those who design the app

  • Rethink engagement practices: clear communication channels and incentives for customers to take part.
  • Interactive and collaborative tools can help customers notice their own participation.
Section 5.2

For tracking customers

  • Keep monitoring what shapes perceived value, because it links creation to results.
  • In the final measure, value for money, fair prices, feeling valued and service above expectations stood out (screen 06).
Section 5.2 and Table 14

For researchers

  • Follow the same customers over time and broaden the sample, including outside Brazil.
  • Study co-creation with interviews or experiments and include trust, digital experience and satisfaction.
Suggestions for future research

For the author, digital banks that manage to innovate, strengthen the customer experience and align creation, perception and capture in a continuous cycle will be better positioned in the market.

12 Limits and credits

What this research does not say

  • A small convenience sample. 117 people, three in four from the Southeast and almost all with a college degree. The results cannot be generalized to all customers. · according to the author, with data from Tables 06 and 12
  • A single moment. The data were collected once and do not show how perception changes over time. · according to the author
  • Brazil only. Culture and trust in the financial system may change the result in other countries. · according to the author
  • Capture as seen by the customer. Capture was measured with questions to customers (engagement, personalization, learning from the app), not with bank revenues or profits. · our note
  • The link, stretch by stretch. Both stretches of the path through perceived value were confirmed, the second only just (p = 0.048). The test of the indirect effect, which would measure the whole path, does not appear in the text. · our note
