04 The problem · Dec 2023 to Jan 2024

Customers and staff told opposite stories about the same service

For management and for the people asking for repairs, maintenance was failing: everything was late and nobody gave updates. For the team, requests were handled within a reasonable time. Whom do you believe?

Work with no record. Some jobs were done without opening a work order (WO).
Late closing. The WO was closed days later, or never: work done on time showed up as late or pending.
Dates set by people who do not plan. Deadlines were set by the service desk, not by the technical staff.
Customer left in the dark. Getting back to the customer was not standard practice.

Sections 2.3, 2.3.2 and 2.3.4. In the author’s words, the indicators proved the customer right, while the work actually done pointed the other way: “both realities are true”.

12 Takeaways

Before switching systems, check whether the figures tell what really happens

For the author, the path mattered as much as the solution: without the method, there would be no way to tell real delays from delays that existed only in the records. The author sees the same recipe serving other maintenance and facilities teams, in-house or outsourced.

For maintenance and facilities managers

  • A single request channel avoids duplicates and lost information.
  • Clear rules for opening and closing WOs make indicators reliable.
  • The people who know the work should set priority and deadline.

For teams serving internal customers

  • Even with no payment involved, there is a customer and service-provider relationship.
  • Status updates at every step were the main change for the customer.

For people leading change

  • Listening to the team from the diagnosis on reduced resistance, according to the author.
  • A pilot with the biggest customer quickly showed what to fix. (11 adjustments before opening to everyone)

What this research does not say

  • How much delays fell. The dissertation gives no WO figures after the change to compare with 2023. · our note
  • How many people rated. The number of ratings is not given, and answering is optional; the author calls the scores preliminary. · per the author and our note
  • An outside evaluation. The author works in managing the maintenance department studied and ran the diagnosis, rollout and evaluation. · our note
  • What is not yet measured. Response time, individual performance, cost, inventory and reasons for delays were still being built into the portal. · per the author
