What changes in the values of an executive disrespected at dismissal?

After interviewing dismissed executives, the author asked 446 who reported disrespect in their dismissal to rate 18 values twice: thinking about their unemployment and after recalling a career success. Thinking about unemployment, almost everything weighed less, above all social life and norms.

Author: Rubens Prata Junior Advisor: Prof. Dr. Maria Luisa Mendes Teixeira Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

05 The method · sections 3.2.4 to 3.3.2

Same people, same questionnaire, two states of mind

It was not possible to wait for each person to go back to work. At the suggestion of the questionnaire's creator, the author used priming: activities that bring an experience back to mind, here a time of success at work. Follow the steps:

Basic Values Questionnaire (BVQ) by Gouveia and colleagues (2008), 18 values, rated from 1 (not important at all) to 7 (extremely important); the most and least important of all become 8 and 0. The second time, questions came in a different order. In a 2016 pilot, with a mandatory 20-line essay, only 34 of 89 finished (38%), and the requirement was dropped.

11 So what?

A severance package does not erase a badly handled dismissal

All participants had career transition support paid by their former employer, and the author notes that executives usually leave with generous packages. Even so, three in four essays reported disrespect in the dismissal.

For leaders and HR

  • Review how dismissals are carried out, for any job, not only executives. · author
  • Indifference and exclusion start before the talk: tasks taken away, access cut, meetings with no invitation. · quotes and author

For career transition consultancies

  • Review programs taking into account that the weight of values changes in this phase. · author
  • Look after social life: people who lose their job tend to withdraw, and contacts are how they get back into the market. · author

For researchers

  • Follow the same people until their next job, instead of asking them to remember. · author
  • Look at time without work, other age bands and, through interviews, the difference between women and men. · author

What this research does not say

  • There was no comparison group. Without it, the effect of the recall exercise cannot be isolated. · according to the author
  • Work was recalled, not lived. Both answers came from unemployed people, always in the same order, 20 days apart. · our note
  • It does not compare with people dismissed respectfully. All were chosen for reporting disrespect, so we cannot tell how much of the change comes from the disrespect or from the dismissal itself. · our note
  • Executives with a package and support. The author notes that most unemployed people have none of that. · according to the author
  • Chosen by words. The automatic search in the essays decided who reported disrespect. · our note
