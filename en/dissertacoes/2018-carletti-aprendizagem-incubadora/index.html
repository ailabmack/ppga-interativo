What a young company learns inside an incubator

Five technology companies that went through the CELTA incubator in Florianópolis told their stories. The founders and the group of partners learn all the time; the company, with its rules, plans and routines, learns in leaps, pushed by moments such as entering the incubator or receiving money from an investment fund. Explained in 12 screens.

Author: Raquel Menezes Carletti Advisor: Prof. Dr. Diógenes de Souza Bido Program: Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) · Mackenzie Presbyterian University

02 The question

Does a company learn, or only the people inside it?

The dissertation starts from one idea: what a person learns can stay with them, can be shared with the group, or can become part of the company (a manual, a value on the wall, a way of hiring). Then it stays even if the person leaves. Try six cases taken from the stories: where is each one kept?

        05 How the research looked

        Five companies told their story from before they existed

        In October 2017, the author interviewed the founders of each company, with open questions, and gathered what was on their websites and in their materials. Then she rewrote each story in order, from before the company existed to its growth, and looked for the four steps in it. One company was still incubated; four had already left. Two others were interviewed and left out, because the stages of their story were not clear.

        07 The unexpected · against the theory

        When the company grows, people do not stop learning

        An earlier study (Brockman, 2013) predicted a change of shift: before opening, the person learns; at the start, the small group; during growth, the company takes the lead. In the five stories, growth brought more company learning, but the founders and the group also learned more. Switch between the two readings.

        11 The conclusion · section 6

        The entrepreneur makes the business and the business makes the entrepreneur

        The company’s learning often starts before it exists, in childhood games, classes and university teams. And the business itself becomes the founder’s school. Click each direction of the arrow to see examples from the stories.

        For the author, the region’s network of entrepreneurs, which the incubator is part of, works as a “community of practice” (Lave and Wenger): a place with its own ways and language where people learn faster. She also raises the idea that the incubator and the university learn from the companies, but says this would require hearing both sides.

        12 So what?

        The incubator teaches more as a meeting place than as a classroom

        For the author, the incubator’s most valuable contribution to learning is bringing together and feeding the network of entrepreneurs, more than offering courses.

        For incubator managers

        • Design programs and benefits knowing that the incubator teaches above all as a place for meeting and experimenting. (author)
        • Subsidised space and access to grants and funds free up energy to learn from the product and the market. (author)

        For founders

        • Our reading: outside demands (business plan, a fund coming in, hiring people) are good moments to write down what the group has already learned.
        • Our reading: talking to the “neighbours” and watching those who have walked the same path appears in several stories as a source of learning.

        For researchers

        • Also hear the incubator, mentors and other players in the network, to understand learning between organisations. (author)
        • Follow companies over time to see the role of reflection in the four steps. (author)

        What this research does not say

        • Five cases, one incubator. The results are leads, not general rules; the author herself says they cannot be taken as definitive. · according to the author
        • Only success stories. The companies were chosen by the incubator’s manager as success stories; companies that closed were not heard. · our observation
        • One voice per company, looking back. The stories are told by the founders, years later, and rewritten by the author; partners, employees and the incubator were not heard about them. · our observation
        • Steps that blend. Separating the four steps and the three levels was hard: they overlap, and the reading of each passage is the author’s. · according to the author
        • Sketches, not measurements. The ramp and steps on screen 6 are a proposal for future studies to test, not something measured. · our observation
