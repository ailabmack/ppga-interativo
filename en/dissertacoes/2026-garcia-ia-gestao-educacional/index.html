Artificial intelligence is already part of running the school

Spreadsheets, e-mails, price quotes, lesson plans: at a private school in São Paulo, Brazil, the management team now does much of this with the help of AI. The dissertation interviewed ten people on that team to understand what changed in their processes, what still holds things back and who decides in the end. Explained in 12 screens.

04 The method

Ten people from a school's management told how they use AI

It is a case study: instead of hearing from many schools, the research looked in depth at just one, a private K-12 school in the city of São Paulo that was already using AI. The author chose people for their experience with the subject, and each answered open questions about adoption, processes, rules, tools and decisions.

12 In practice, limits and credits

The path the author proposes for other schools

The dissertation closes with a three-phase model (Figure 6):

1 · Before: conditions

The team adopts what it sees as useful and easy to use, learns alongside the technology (including how to write good prompts) and works in secure environments, within the data protection law and with an ethics committee.

2 · During: new capabilities

From built-in tools (Microsoft 365, Google Workspace) to agents that carry out tasks on their own. In administration: costs, school assets, data auditing. In teaching: digital literacy, inclusive lesson planning and communication.

3 · After: better decisions

"Hybrid" decision-making: AI processes the data and acts as a sounding board, and the person curates and decides.

What this research does not say

  • Just one school. A private school with its own technology structure does not represent Brazil's schools, and lack of resources may leave public schools behind. · according to the author
  • Ten people, all from management and technology. Other groups might see AI differently; teachers of other subjects, families and students were not heard. · author and our note
  • A snapshot of 2026. AI changes fast; the results hold for this moment. · according to the author
  • Time reported, not measured. The time savings come from the quotes; the study did not time the tasks. · our note

Suggested next steps: compare public and private schools, study managers' workload and mental health (some accounts mention a "false sense of being on top of everything"), follow the arrival of agents and discuss authorship and plagiarism in documents made with AI.
