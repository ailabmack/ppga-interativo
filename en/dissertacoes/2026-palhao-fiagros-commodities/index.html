Farm prices go up. What about FIAGROs?

FIAGROs are Brazilian exchange-traded funds that channel investors' money into agribusiness. The study followed 40 of these funds, day by day, from 2021 to 2025, and compared their returns with the prices of eight farm products. The link was much weaker than expected. Explained in 12 screens.

02 The starting point

Agribusiness is a quarter of Brazil's economy and barely on the stock exchange

Agribusiness share of GDP (2020–2023)about 25%
Workers employed in the sectorabout 27%
Agribusiness firms among companies listed on B3under 5%

To bring the two worlds together, Law 14,130/2021 created FIAGROs: funds traded on the stock exchange (B3, Brazil's exchange) that invest in agribusiness chains. Anyone who buys a unit takes part, directly or indirectly, in the sector's growth.

Data from CEPEA–Esalq/USP (a University of São Paulo research center) and from B3, cited in the dissertation's introduction. In 2021, agribusiness reached 26.6% of GDP; in 2023, 28% of employed workers.

08 How strong the link is

Prices explain less than 1% of the funds' ups and downs

Imagine a thousand parts of all the daily variation in a fund's returns over time. Farm and economic prices, together, account for fewer than ten of them. The rest is the day-to-day noise of the market.

09 Robustness test

With a different yardstick, only sugar held

To check whether the results depended on extreme days (such as +169% or −86%), the author redid the calculations with another method, LAD (least absolute deviations): instead of adding up squared errors, it adds up errors without their sign, which gives less weight to unusual days.

11 Why it matters

Investing in agribusiness through a FIAGRO is not a bet on soybean prices

For the author, FIAGROs are an asset class with dynamics of their own: only loosely tied to commodities and also to the traditional stock market.

For investors

  • Being exposed to agribusiness does not mean following commodity prices.
  • FIAGROs can help diversify a portfolio, behaving differently from farm markets and the stock exchange.
Final remarks

When looking at a fund

  • What matters most is the fund itself: its portfolio, credit quality, contracts and management.
  • When counting returns, dividends matter: they are the main way unit holders are paid.
Sections 3.1, 4.1 and final remarks

For managers and regulators

  • The evidence can help improve how these funds are structured.
  • And help develop the capital market that finances agribusiness.
Final remarks

For researchers

  • Split the funds by the kind of asset that dominates their portfolio.
  • Use a longer time window and include credit quality, liquidity and sustainability indices.
Future studies

For the author, the main finding is not the few links that showed up, but their size: much smaller than theory led one to expect from funds tied to agribusiness.

12 Limits and credits

What this research does not say

  • A short history. FIAGROs have existed since 2021, and the period studied is just over four years. · according to the author
  • Very different funds lumped together. Credit funds, land funds and funds holding other assets, with different structures, were analyzed together. · according to the author
  • Short term. The model looks at each day's change and up to four trading days before. It says nothing about months or years. · our observation
  • Fewer days in the final model. With the lags, the final model used 4,749 of the 27,328 observations; the test with the other method used 27,317. · our observation, with data from Tables 12 and 14
  • Little link with the stock market. This conclusion comes from the low betas that made the Treynor ratio of little use; the beta values themselves are not reported. · our observation
